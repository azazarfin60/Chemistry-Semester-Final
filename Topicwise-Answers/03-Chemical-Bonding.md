### Q.1(a) Explain the conditions of formation of ionic bond and covalent bond. (06)

**Answer:**

**Conditions for the Formation of an Ionic Bond:**
An ionic bond is formed by the complete transfer of one or more electrons from an electropositive atom (metal) to an electronegative atom (non-metal). The primary conditions favoring its formation are:
1. **Low Ionization Energy of the Metal:** The metal atom must have a low ionization energy so that it can easily lose electrons to form a cation. (e.g., Alkali metals like Na, K).
2. **High Electron Affinity of the Non-metal:** The non-metal atom should possess a high electron affinity so that it can readily accept electrons to form an anion and release a large amount of energy. (e.g., Halogens like Cl, F).
3. **High Lattice Energy:** The electrostatic attraction between the newly formed cations and anions must be strong enough to release a high amount of lattice energy when they pack into a crystal lattice. The higher the lattice energy, the greater the stability of the ionic compound.
4. **Large Electronegativity Difference:** The difference in electronegativity between the two bonding atoms must be large (typically > 1.7 on the Pauling scale) to ensure a complete transfer of electrons rather than sharing.

**Conditions for the Formation of a Covalent Bond:**
A covalent bond is formed by the mutual sharing of valence electrons between two atoms to achieve a stable noble gas configuration. The conditions favoring its formation are:
1. **High Ionization Energy:** Both interacting atoms should have high ionization energies, meaning neither is willing to completely give up an electron to form a cation.
2. **High Electron Affinity:** Both atoms should have high or comparable electron affinities, making them eager to gain electrons. Thus, sharing is the only viable compromise.
3. **Small or Zero Electronegativity Difference:** The electronegativity difference between the two atoms must be very small or zero (typically < 1.7). If it is zero (e.g., $H_2$, $Cl_2$), a purely non-polar covalent bond forms. If it is small, a polar covalent bond forms (e.g., $HCl$, $H_2O$).
4. **Equal Requirement of Electrons:** Both atoms should require 1, 2, or 3 electrons to complete their respective octets (or duplets).

### Q.3(a) Distinguish between Valance Bond Theory (VBT) and Molecular Orbital Theory (MOT). (06)

**Answer:**

| Feature | Valence Bond Theory (VBT) | Molecular Orbital Theory (MOT) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Electron Localization** | Assumes that electrons in a molecule are localized in the specific atomic orbitals of the interacting atoms. | Assumes that electrons are delocalized and belong to the entire molecule as a whole, moving in newly formed molecular orbitals. |
| **Identity of Atoms** | The participating atoms retain their individual identity even after bond formation. | The participating atoms lose their individual identity; the molecule behaves as a single unit with a new set of energy levels. |
| **Orbital Intermixing** | Involves the overlapping of half-filled atomic orbitals from the combining atoms. | Involves the linear combination of atomic orbitals (LCAO) to form an equal number of bonding and antibonding molecular orbitals. |
| **Resonance & Hybridization** | Explains molecular geometry through the concepts of hybridization and resonance. | Does not require resonance or hybridization to explain the structure or stability of molecules. |
| **Magnetic Properties** | Fails to accurately predict the magnetic properties of certain molecules (e.g., VBT predicts $O_2$ to be diamagnetic, which is incorrect). | Successfully explains magnetic properties (e.g., correctly predicts $O_2$ as paramagnetic due to unpaired electrons in antibonding orbitals). |
| **Odd-Electron Molecules**| Struggles to explain the stability and bonding of molecules or ions with an odd number of electrons (e.g., $NO$, $O_2^-$). | Easily explains odd-electron molecules by calculating bond orders from filled molecular orbitals. |

### Q.3(b) Explain the energy level diagram of $O_2$ and $N_2$ molecule using MOT. (04)

**Answer:**

**Energy Level Diagram of Nitrogen ($N_2$) Molecule:**
*   **Electronic Configuration of N (Z=7):** $1s^2 2s^2 2p^3$
*   Total valence electrons for $N_2$ = $5 + 5 = 10$.
*   In $N_2$, the $2s-2p$ mixing is significant. This pushes the energy of the $\sigma_{2p_z}$ orbital higher than the $\pi_{2p_x}$ and $\pi_{2p_y}$ orbitals.
*   **MO Configuration:**
    $KK \ (\sigma_{2s})^2 \ (\sigma^*_{2s})^2 \ (\pi_{2p_x})^2 = (\pi_{2p_y})^2 \ (\sigma_{2p_z})^2$
*   **Bond Order:** $\frac{N_b - N_a}{2} = \frac{8 - 2}{2} = 3$ (Triple bond).
*   **Magnetic Property:** Diamagnetic, as there are no unpaired electrons.

**Energy Level Diagram of Oxygen ($O_2$) Molecule:**
*   **Electronic Configuration of O (Z=8):** $1s^2 2s^2 2p^4$
*   Total valence electrons for $O_2$ = $6 + 6 = 12$.
*   In $O_2$, the energy gap between 2s and 2p is large, so $2s-2p$ mixing is negligible. Therefore, the $\sigma_{2p_z}$ orbital is lower in energy than the $\pi_{2p_x}$ and $\pi_{2p_y}$ orbitals.
*   **MO Configuration:**
    $KK \ (\sigma_{2s})^2 \ (\sigma^*_{2s})^2 \ (\sigma_{2p_z})^2 \ (\pi_{2p_x})^2 = (\pi_{2p_y})^2 \ (\pi^*_{2p_x})^1 = (\pi^*_{2p_y})^1$
*   **Bond Order:** $\frac{N_b - N_a}{2} = \frac{8 - 4}{2} = 2$ (Double bond).
*   **Magnetic Property:** Paramagnetic, because it contains two unpaired electrons in the degenerate $\pi^*$ antibonding orbitals (one in $\pi^*_{2p_x}$ and one in $\pi^*_{2p_y}$), governed by Hund's rule.

*(Note: In an exam, sketching the actual diagram with vertical energy axes, showing atomic orbitals on the sides and molecular orbitals in the middle is highly recommended).*

### Q.3(a) State and explain molecular orbital theory (MOT). (03)

**Answer:**

**Molecular Orbital Theory (MOT):**
Developed by F. Hund and R.S. Mulliken, MOT provides a quantum mechanical explanation for chemical bonding. The key postulates (statements) are:

1.  **Linear Combination of Atomic Orbitals (LCAO):** Just as electrons of atoms are present in atomic orbitals, the electrons of a molecule are present in new orbitals called molecular orbitals (MOs). These MOs are formed by the linear combination (addition and subtraction of wave functions) of atomic orbitals of comparable energy and proper symmetry.
2.  **Number of MOs:** The number of molecular orbitals formed is exactly equal to the number of atomic orbitals combining. Two atomic orbitals combine to form two MOs:
    *   **Bonding Molecular Orbital (BMO):** Formed by the addition of wave functions. It has lower energy and greater stability than the combining atomic orbitals.
    *   **Antibonding Molecular Orbital (ABMO):** Formed by the subtraction of wave functions. It has higher energy and lower stability.
3.  **Loss of Identity:** When atomic orbitals merge to form molecular orbitals, they lose their individual identity. The new MO is polycentric, meaning the electron cloud is influenced by two or more nuclei.
4.  **Filling Rules:** The filling of electrons into these molecular orbitals strictly follows the Aufbau principle (increasing energy order), Pauli's exclusion principle (max 2 electrons per MO with opposite spins), and Hund's rule of maximum multiplicity.

### Q.3(b) Draw the molecular orbital of $He_2$ gas and proof that it doesn't exist. (03)

**Answer:**

**Proof of Non-existence of $He_2$ via MOT:**
1.  **Electronic Configuration of He atom (Z=2):** $1s^2$
2.  **Total valence electrons in hypothetial $He_2$ molecule:** $2 + 2 = 4$ electrons.
3.  **Molecular Orbital Diagram:**
    The two $1s$ atomic orbitals of the two Helium atoms will combine to form two molecular orbitals: a bonding $\sigma_{1s}$ and an antibonding $\sigma^*_{1s}$.

    *   **Energy Level Diagram sketch:**
        He(Atom A) -------- $He_2$ Molecule -------- He(Atom B)
        $1s$ [ ↑↓ ] ----------------------------------- $1s$ [ ↑↓ ]
                     \           $\sigma^*_{1s}$ [ ↑↓ ]         /
                      \                             /
                       \--- $\sigma_{1s}$ [ ↑↓ ] ---/

4.  **MO Electronic Configuration:** $(\sigma_{1s})^2 \ (\sigma^*_{1s})^2$
5.  **Bond Order Calculation:**
    $$\text{Bond Order (B.O.)} = \frac{N_b - N_a}{2}$$
    Where $N_b$ = electrons in bonding MOs = 2
    Where $N_a$ = electrons in antibonding MOs = 2
    $$\text{B.O.} = \frac{2 - 2}{2} = 0$$

**Conclusion:** A bond order of zero implies that there is no net attractive force between the two Helium atoms. The destabilizing effect of the 2 electrons in the antibonding orbital completely cancels out the stabilizing effect of the 2 electrons in the bonding orbital. Therefore, the $He_2$ molecule cannot exist.

### Q.3(c) Draw the molecular orbitals of CO and NO and also find the bond order and magnetic properties of each molecules. (04)

**Answer:**

**(I) Nitric Oxide (NO) Molecule:**
*   Total valence electrons: N (7) + O (8) = 15 electrons.
*   **MO Electronic Configuration:**
    Because NO is a heteronuclear molecule with more than 14 electrons, its energy filling order is similar to $O_2$.
    $\sigma_{1s}^2 \ \sigma_{1s}^{*2} \ \sigma_{2s}^2 \ \sigma_{2s}^{*2} \ \sigma_{2p_z}^2 \ \pi_{2p_x}^2 \equiv \pi_{2p_y}^2 \ \pi_{2p_x}^{*1}$
*   **Bond Order (B.O.):**
    $$B.O. = \frac{N_b - N_a}{2} = \frac{10 - 5}{2} = \frac{5}{2} = 2.5$$
    *(Implies a bond between double and triple).*
*   **Magnetic Property:** It contains one unpaired electron in the antibonding $\pi_{2p_x}^*$ orbital. Therefore, NO is **Paramagnetic**.

**(II) Carbon Monoxide (CO) Molecule:**
*   Total valence electrons: C (6) + O (8) = 14 electrons.
*   **MO Electronic Configuration:**
    For molecules with 14 or fewer electrons (like $N_2$ or CO), significant $s-p$ mixing occurs, pushing the $\sigma_{2p_z}$ orbital higher in energy than the $\pi_{2p_x}$ and $\pi_{2p_y}$ orbitals.
    $\sigma_{1s}^2 \ \sigma_{1s}^{*2} \ \sigma_{2s}^2 \ \sigma_{2s}^{*2} \ \pi_{2p_x}^2 \equiv \pi_{2p_y}^2 \ \sigma_{2p_z}^2$
*   **Bond Order (B.O.):**
    $$B.O. = \frac{N_b - N_a}{2} = \frac{10 - 4}{2} = \frac{6}{2} = 3$$
    *(Implies a strong triple bond, $C \equiv O$).*
*   **Magnetic Property:** All electrons are paired in the molecular orbitals. Therefore, CO is **Diamagnetic**.

An electrovalent bond is a type of chemical bond formed by the complete transfer of one or more valence electrons from an electropositive atom (a metal) to an electronegative atom (a non-metal). ### Q.8(b) Distinguish between sigma and pi-bond. (04)

**Answer:**

| Feature | Sigma ($\sigma$) Bond | Pi ($\pi$) Bond |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Mode of Overlap** | Formed by the head-on (axial or end-to-end) overlap of atomic orbitals along the internuclear axis. | Formed by the lateral (sideways) overlap of parallel atomic orbitals, perpendicular to the internuclear axis. |
| **Strength** | It is a very strong bond because the extent of orbital overlap is large. | It is a relatively weak bond because the extent of lateral overlap is small. |
| **Electron Density** | The electron density is concentrated symmetrically along the internuclear axis between the two nuclei. | The electron density is divided into two regions (lobes) lying above and below the internuclear axis. |
| **Independent Existence** | It can exist independently. A single bond is always a sigma bond. | It cannot exist independently. It is only formed after a sigma bond is already established between the two atoms (in double or triple bonds). |
| **Free Rotation** | Allows free rotation of atoms around the bond axis without breaking the bond. | Restricts free rotation around the bond axis because rotation would break the parallel orbital overlap. |

### Q.8(c) How to form metallic bond? (03)

**Answer:**

**Formation of a Metallic Bond:**
The formation of a metallic bond is best explained by the **Electron Sea Model** (or Drude-Lorentz theory).
1.  **Ionization:** Metals have low ionization energies and few valence electrons. In a solid metallic crystal, the metal atoms easily lose these loosely held valence electrons to achieve stable electronic configurations, leaving behind positively charged metal ions known as **"kernels."**
2.  **Electron Sea:** The lost valence electrons are no longer tied to any specific atom. They become completely delocalized and move freely throughout the entire crystal lattice, forming a highly mobile "cloud" or "sea" of electrons.
3.  **Bonding Force:** The **metallic bond** is the strong, non-directional electrostatic force of attraction that holds the positively charged metal kernels tightly within the negatively charged, mobile sea of delocalized electrons. This uniform attraction binds the entire structure together, giving metals their characteristic high tensile strength, malleability, and conductivity.

### Q.8(a) Define chemical bond and classify them. (04)

**Answer:**

**Chemical Bond:**
A chemical bond is defined as the attractive physical force that holds various constituents (atoms, ions, or molecules) together in different chemical species. Atoms bond to lower their overall potential energy and achieve a highly stable electronic configuration (usually resembling the nearest noble gas, following the octet rule).

**Classification of Chemical Bonds:**
Chemical bonds are broadly classified into strong (primary) bonds and weak (secondary) bonds:

**Strong (Primary) Bonds:**
1.  **Ionic (Electrovalent) Bond:** Formed by the complete transfer of one or more valence electrons from a metal to a non-metal.
2.  **Covalent Bond:** Formed by the mutual sharing of valence electrons between two non-metal atoms.
3.  **Coordinate (Dative) Bond:** A specialized covalent bond where the shared pair of electrons is provided entirely by only one of the bonding atoms.
4.  **Metallic Bond:** The electrostatic attraction between a lattice of positive metal ions (kernels) and a delocalized "sea" of mobile electrons.

**Weak (Secondary) Bonds:**
1.  **Hydrogen Bond:** A dipole-dipole attraction between a highly electronegative atom (F, O, N) and a hydrogen atom covalently bonded to another highly electronegative atom.
2.  **van der Waals Forces:** Weak intermolecular dispersion forces existing between all molecules (e.g., London dispersion forces).

### Q.2(a) Describe the factors favoring the formation of ionic bond. (04)

**Answer:**

An ionic bond is formed by the complete transfer of electrons from a metal to a non-metal. The formation of a stable ionic bond is favored by three main thermodynamic factors:

1.  **Low Ionization Energy of the Metal:** For a metal atom to easily lose its valence electron(s) and form a stable positive ion (cation), it must have a low ionization energy. (e.g., Alkali metals naturally form ionic bonds because they have very low ionization energies).
2.  **High Electron Affinity of the Non-Metal:** The non-metal atom must readily accept the transferred electron(s) to form a stable negative ion (anion). A high, negative electron affinity means a large amount of energy is released when the non-metal gains an electron, stabilizing the system. (e.g., Halogens).
3.  **High Lattice Energy:** The most crucial factor is the lattice energy, which is the massive amount of energy released when the gaseous cations and anions come together and pack into a rigid, 3D electrostatic crystal lattice. If the lattice energy is highly negative (highly exothermic), it fully compensates for the energy required to ionize the metal, ensuring the overall process results in a highly stable ionic compound.

### Q.2(b) Explain the dissolution of ionic compounds based on the relative magnitudes of lattice energy and hydration energy. (04)

**Answer:**

When an ionic compound is placed in a polar solvent like water, two opposing energetic forces govern whether it will dissolve:

1.  **Lattice Energy:** This is the energy that holds the ions tightly together in the solid crystal lattice. It is the energy barrier that must be overcome (absorbed) to break the solid apart into isolated gaseous ions.
2.  **Hydration Energy:** When the isolated ions are surrounded by polar water molecules (ion-dipole interactions), they become hydrated. This solvation process releases energy, known as Hydration Energy.

**Mechanism of Dissolution:**
*   **If Hydration Energy > Lattice Energy:** The energy released by the hydration of the separated ions is greater than the energy required to break the ionic bonds in the crystal lattice. The net process is energetically favorable (exothermic or entropically driven), and the **ionic compound dissolves**. (e.g., NaCl in water).
*   **If Hydration Energy < Lattice Energy:** The energy released by hydrating the ions is not sufficient to compensate for the massive energy required to break apart the strong crystal lattice. The forces holding the solid together "win," and the **ionic compound remains insoluble**. (e.g., $BaSO_4$ in water).

### Q.2(c) Metals are good conductors of heat and electricity. Explain. (04)

**Answer:**

The high conductivity of metals is perfectly explained by the **Electron Sea Model** of metallic bonding.

1.  **Electrical Conductivity:** In a metallic crystal, the metal atoms have released their valence electrons, becoming positively charged "kernels." The released valence electrons are highly mobile and completely delocalized, forming a "sea" of electrons that can move freely throughout the entire solid structure. When an electric potential difference (voltage) is applied across the metal, these mobile, negatively charged electrons are strongly repelled by the negative terminal and attracted to the positive terminal. This rapid, unidirectional drift of the electron sea constitutes a highly efficient electric current.
2.  **Thermal Conductivity:** When one end of a metal is heated, the kinetic energy of the free electrons in that hot region increases significantly. Because they are highly mobile, these energized electrons quickly zip through the lattice, colliding with other electrons and vibrating metal kernels in the cooler regions. These collisions rapidly transfer thermal energy throughout the entire metallic structure, making metals excellent conductors of heat.

### Q2(a) Define $\sigma$-bond and $\pi$-bond. Write down the theories of hybridization. Define $sp^3$ hybridization with $H_2O$, $NH_3$ and $CH_4$ molecules as example and also explain VSEPR theory. (08)

**Answer:**
**$\sigma$-bond (Sigma bond):** A type of strong covalent bond formed by the head-on (axial or end-to-end) overlap of atomic orbitals along the internuclear axis. The electron density is concentrated symmetrically between the two nuclei.
**$\pi$-bond (Pi bond):** A weaker covalent bond formed by the lateral (sideways) overlap of parallel p-atomic orbitals. The electron density is divided into two lobes lying above and below the internuclear axis. It only forms after a sigma bond is present.

**Theories of Hybridization:**
Hybridization is the theoretical concept involving the intermixing of slightly different energy atomic orbitals of the same atom to redistribute their energy, resulting in the formation of an entirely new set of an equal number of equivalent orbitals. These new orbitals are called "hybrid orbitals." They have identical shape, identical energy, and are directed symmetrically in space to minimize electron repulsion, leading to much stronger, highly directional covalent bonds.

**$sp^3$ Hybridization and Examples:**
It involves the mixing of one 's' and three 'p' orbitals of the valence shell to form four entirely equivalent $sp^3$ hybrid orbitals directed towards the four corners of a regular tetrahedron (ideal bond angle $109^\circ 28'$).
1.  **$CH_4$ (Methane):** The central Carbon atom undergoes $sp^3$ hybridization. It has 4 valence electrons and forms 4 sigma bonds (4 Bond Pairs) with 4 Hydrogen atoms. Because all four hybrid orbitals are used for bonding, there is no lone pair repulsion. The geometry is a perfect **Tetrahedron** with bond angles of exactly **$109^\circ 28'$**.
2.  **$NH_3$ (Ammonia):** The central Nitrogen atom is $sp^3$ hybridized. It has 5 valence electrons, using 3 to form bond pairs with Hydrogen and leaving 1 unshared pair (1 Lone Pair) in the fourth hybrid orbital. Due to Lone Pair - Bond Pair repulsion pushing the bonds closer together, the ideal tetrahedral shape is distorted into a **Trigonal Pyramidal** geometry, and the bond angle shrinks to **$107^\circ$**.
3.  **$H_2O$ (Water):** The central Oxygen atom is $sp^3$ hybridized. It has 6 valence electrons, forming 2 bond pairs with Hydrogen and holding 2 Lone Pairs in the remaining hybrid orbitals. Due to severe Lone Pair - Lone Pair repulsion, the bonds are squeezed even tighter. The resulting geometry is a **Bent or V-shape**, and the bond angle shrinks further to **$104.5^\circ$**.

**VSEPR Theory (Valence Shell Electron Pair Repulsion):**
Proposed by Sidgwick, Powell, Gillespie, and Nyholm, this theory is used to predict the 3D geometry of molecules.
1.  The shape of a molecule is entirely determined by the repulsive forces between the electron pairs (both bonding pairs and lone pairs) present in the valence shell of the central atom.
2.  These electron pairs will automatically arrange themselves as far apart as possible in 3D space to minimize electrostatic repulsion and maximize the stability of the molecule.
3.  The magnitude of repulsion follows this order: **Lone Pair-Lone Pair > Lone Pair-Bond Pair > Bond Pair-Bond Pair**. The presence of lone pairs causes significant distortion from the ideal, symmetrical geometries.

### Q2(b) Define "Band theory" of conductivity and explain metallic bond. (04)

**Answer:**
**Band Theory of Conductivity:**
Band theory is a quantum mechanical model based on Molecular Orbital Theory that explains the electrical conductivity of solids. In a solid crystal containing incredibly vast numbers of atoms, their closely packed atomic orbitals overlap to form molecular orbitals. Because there are so many of these molecular orbitals, their energy levels become so closely spaced that they form continuous "energy bands."
*   **Valence Band:** The highest energy band that is completely or partially filled with electrons.
*   **Conduction Band:** The next higher energy band, which is either empty or partially filled.
*   **Conductivity:** In metals (good conductors), the valence band and conduction band overlap (there is zero "band gap" or forbidden zone). Because of this overlap, valence electrons can effortlessly transition into the conduction band under an applied electric field, moving freely throughout the lattice to conduct electricity. Insulators have a massive band gap, preventing this transition.

**Metallic Bond:**
The metallic bond is the fundamental chemical bond that holds metal atoms together in a solid lattice. It is best explained by the **Electron Sea Model**.
Since metals have low ionization energies, they easily lose their valence electrons to become positively charged ions (called **"kernels"**). The lost electrons become completely delocalized, forming a highly mobile "sea" or "cloud" of negative charge throughout the entire crystal. The metallic bond is the strong, non-directional electrostatic force of attraction that exists between the fixed lattice of positively charged metal kernels and the negatively charged, free-flowing sea of mobile electrons.

### Q1(a) What is Chemical bond? Explain. (04)

**Answer:**
**Chemical Bond:** A chemical bond is defined as the attractive physical force that holds various constituents (atoms, ions, or molecules) together in different chemical species to form a stable, independent entity.

**Explanation:**
Under normal conditions, atoms of most elements (except noble gases) do not exist as independent entities in nature because they have incomplete valence electron shells, making them highly energetic and unstable. To achieve maximum stability (lowest potential energy), atoms interact with each other. A chemical bond is formed when atoms either completely transfer valence electrons (Ionic bond), mutually share valence electrons (Covalent bond), or pool their electrons in a sea (Metallic bond). This redistribution of electrons allows each participating atom to attain a stable, noble-gas-like electronic configuration (usually an octet), thereby lowering the overall energy of the resulting molecule compared to the isolated atoms.

### 1. Explain different types of van-der-Waals attraction forces using proper figure.
**Answer:**
Van der Waals forces are weak, short-range intermolecular attractive forces that exist between neutral molecules. They are classified into three main types based on the polarity of the interacting molecules:

1.  **Dipole-Dipole Interactions (Keesom Forces):**
    These occur between two permanently polar molecules (e.g., $HCl$). The partially positive end ($\delta^+$) of one permanent dipole is electrostatically attracted to the partially negative end ($\delta^-$) of a neighboring permanent dipole.
    *(Mental Figure: Two oval molecules, each with a $\delta^+$ on the left and $\delta^-$ on the right, aligning end-to-end so $\delta^-$ attracts $\delta^+$).*
2.  **Dipole-Induced Dipole Interactions (Debye Forces):**
    These occur when a permanent polar molecule approaches a completely non-polar molecule (e.g., $H_2O$ and $O_2$). The strong electric field of the permanent dipole distorts the symmetrical electron cloud of the non-polar molecule, "inducing" a temporary dipole within it. The two molecules then attract each other.
    *(Mental Figure: A permanent polar molecule distorting a perfectly spherical non-polar electron cloud into a temporary oval shape, causing attraction).*
3.  **Instantaneous Dipole-Induced Dipole Interactions (London Dispersion Forces):**
    These are the only forces that exist between completely non-polar molecules or noble gas atoms (e.g., $He, N_2, CH_4$). Due to the constant, random motion of electrons, the electron cloud can briefly shift to one side, creating an "instantaneous, temporary dipole." This fleeting dipole instantly distorts the electron cloud of an adjacent molecule, creating an "induced dipole," and they attract. These are the weakest forces but exist in all molecules.
    *(Mental Figure: A symmetrical atom suddenly having more electrons on one side, creating a temporary $\delta^-$ pole, which pushes away electrons on a neighboring atom, inducing a matching $\delta^+$ pole).*

### 2. "$N_2$ is diamagnetic but $O_2$ is paramagnetic" justify this statement with M.O.T.
**Answer:**
According to Molecular Orbital Theory (MOT), a molecule is diamagnetic if all of its electrons are paired in molecular orbitals, and paramagnetic if it contains one or more unpaired electrons.

**1. Nitrogen Molecule ($N_2$):**
Total electrons = $7 + 7 = 14$ electrons.
M.O. Configuration: $\sigma 1s^2, \sigma^* 1s^2, \sigma 2s^2, \sigma^* 2s^2, (\pi 2p_x^2 = \pi 2p_y^2), \sigma 2p_z^2$
*Justification:* Every single molecular orbital is filled with exactly two electrons (spin-paired). Because there are absolutely **zero unpaired electrons**, the $N_2$ molecule is repelled by a magnetic field, making it completely **diamagnetic**.

**2. Oxygen Molecule ($O_2$):**
Total electrons = $8 + 8 = 16$ electrons.
M.O. Configuration: $\sigma 1s^2, \sigma^* 1s^2, \sigma 2s^2, \sigma^* 2s^2, \sigma 2p_z^2, (\pi 2p_x^2 = \pi 2p_y^2), (\pi^* 2p_x^1 = \pi^* 2p_y^1)$
*Justification:* According to Hund's rule, the two highest energy electrons must singly occupy the two degenerate anti-bonding pi orbitals ($\pi^* 2p_x$ and $\pi^* 2p_y$) with parallel spins before pairing. Because the $O_2$ molecule possesses exactly **two unpaired electrons**, it is attracted to an external magnetic field, proving it is strongly **paramagnetic**.
