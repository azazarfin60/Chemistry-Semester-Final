### Q.8(b) Discuss the temperature effect on the solubility of gas in liquid. (03)

**Answer:**

**Effect of Temperature on Gas Solubility:**
The solubility of a gas in a liquid generally **decreases with an increase in temperature**.

**Discussion/Reasoning:**
1.  **Exothermic Nature:** The dissolution of a gas in a liquid is an exothermic process. According to Le Chatelier's principle, for an exothermic equilibrium process, increasing the temperature shifts the equilibrium backward. Therefore, the dissolved gas escapes from the solution, lowering its solubility.
    $$\text{Gas (solute)} + \text{Liquid (solvent)} \rightleftharpoons \text{Solution} + \text{Heat}$$
2.  **Kinetic Energy:** Non-polar gases have very weak intermolecular attractive forces with polar liquids like water. As temperature increases, the kinetic energy of the dissolved gas molecules increases. This added thermal energy allows the gas molecules to easily overcome the weak solute-solvent interactions and escape into the vapor phase above the liquid. 
3.  *(Example: This is why soda goes "flat" quickly on a hot day, and why aquatic life survives better in colder waters where dissolved oxygen concentration is higher).*

### Q.8(c) 5.0 g of toluene is dissolved in 225g of benzene. What is the molality of toluene in the solutions? (03)

**Answer:**

**Given Data:**
*   Mass of solute (Toluene, $C_7H_8$), $W_B = 5.0 \text{ g}$
*   Mass of solvent (Benzene), $W_A = 225 \text{ g} = 0.225 \text{ kg}$

**To Find:**
*   Molality ($m$) of toluene in the solution.

**Solution:**
**Step 1: Calculate the molar mass of the solute (Toluene)**
Chemical formula of Toluene is $C_6H_5CH_3$ or $C_7H_8$.
Molar mass of Toluene ($M_B$) = $(7 \times 12) + (8 \times 1) = 84 + 8 = 92 \text{ g/mol}$

**Step 2: Calculate the number of moles of solute ($n_B$)**
$$n_B = \frac{\text{Mass of Toluene}}{\text{Molar mass of Toluene}} = \frac{5.0 \text{ g}}{92 \text{ g/mol}} = 0.05435 \text{ moles}$$

**Step 3: Calculate the molality ($m$)**
Molality is defined as the number of moles of solute per kilogram of solvent.
$$m = \frac{\text{Moles of solute } (n_B)}{\text{Mass of solvent in kg } (W_A \text{ in kg})}$$
$$m = \frac{0.05435 \text{ mol}}{0.225 \text{ kg}}$$
$$m = 0.2415 \text{ mol/kg}$$

**Final Answer:** The molality of toluene in the solution is **$0.2415 \text{ m}$** (or $\text{mol kg}^{-1}$).

### Q.1(b) State and explain Henry's law. Mention its limitations. (05)

**Answer:**

**Statement of Henry's Law:**
Henry's Law states that *"At a constant temperature, the mass of a gas dissolved in a given volume of a liquid is directly proportional to the partial pressure of that gas present in equilibrium with the liquid."*
Alternatively, it can be stated as: The partial pressure ($p$) of the gas in the vapor phase is proportional to the mole fraction ($x$) of the gas in the solution.
$$p = K_H \cdot x$$
where $K_H$ is the Henry's law constant.

**Explanation:**
When a gas is in contact with a liquid, gas molecules continuously enter and leave the liquid phase. At equilibrium, the rate of dissolution equals the rate of evaporation. If the pressure of the gas above the liquid is increased, more gas molecules strike the liquid surface and dissolve, shifting the equilibrium to accommodate a higher concentration of dissolved gas. The higher the value of $K_H$ at a given pressure, the lower is the solubility of the gas in the liquid.

**Limitations of Henry's Law:**
Henry's Law is strictly valid only for ideal gases and ideal solutions. It holds good provided that:
1.  **Pressure is not too high:** At extremely high pressures, the law deviates because gases behave non-ideally.
2.  **Temperature is not too low:** At very low temperatures, the behavior of the gas deviates from ideality.
3.  **No Chemical Reaction:** The gas must not react chemically with the solvent (e.g., $NH_3$ in water reacts to form $NH_4OH$, so it does not strictly obey Henry's law).
4.  **No Dissociation or Association:** The gas molecules should not dissociate into ions (like $HCl$ in water) or associate in the solvent.

### Q.1(c) What happens when a tiny crystal of the solute is added to super saturated solution. (03)

**Answer:**

When a tiny crystal of the solute (called a **"seed crystal"**) is added to a supersaturated solution, it provides a nucleation site or template for the excess dissolved solute to deposit upon. 
Because a supersaturated solution is in a highly unstable, metastable state (containing more dissolved solute than it normally could at that temperature), the introduction of the seed crystal disrupts this fragile state. This causes an immediate and rapid precipitation (crystallization) of all the excess solute out of the solution. The crystallization continues until the concentration of the remaining solution drops to exactly the saturation point, restoring a stable equilibrium.

### Q7(a) Define: (i) Solute (ii) ppm (iii) Normality and (iv) Molality. (04)

**Answer:**
1.  **Solute:** In a homogeneous mixture (solution), the solute is the component that is dissolved into the solvent. It is typically the substance present in the physically smaller amount.
2.  **ppm (parts per million):** A unit of concentration used for extremely dilute solutions. It represents the mass of the solute in milligrams (mg) present in exactly one kilogram (kg) of the solvent. Or: $\text{ppm} = \frac{\text{Mass of solute}}{\text{Total mass of solution}} \times 10^6$.
3.  **Normality (N):** The concentration of a solution expressed as the number of gram-equivalents of the solute dissolved in exactly one liter ($1 \text{ dm}^3$) of the solution. It is temperature-dependent.
4.  **Molality (m):** The concentration of a solution expressed as the number of moles of the solute dissolved in exactly one kilogram (1000 grams) of the pure solvent. Because it is based on mass, molality is independent of temperature changes.

### Q7(b) State and explain Henry's law. Show that the volume of a gas dissolved in a given volume of solvent is independent of the pressure. (04)

**Answer:**

**Henry's Law:**
*   **Statement:** At a constant temperature, the mass of a gas that dissolves in a given, fixed volume of a liquid solvent is directly proportional to the partial pressure of that gas present in equilibrium above the liquid surface.
*   **Mathematical Expression:** $m \propto P \implies m = k \cdot P$
    (Where $m$ is the mass of dissolved gas, $P$ is the pressure, and $k$ is Henry's constant).

**Proof that Volume is Independent of Pressure:**
Let $m$ be the mass of the gas dissolved at an applied pressure $P$. From Henry's Law:
$$m = kP \quad \dots \text{(Equation 1)}$$
Assume the dissolved gas behaves ideally. According to the Ideal Gas Equation:
$$PV = nRT$$
Substitute $n$ (moles) with $m/M$ (mass / molar mass):
$$PV = \frac{m}{M} RT$$
Rearranging to solve for the volume of the dissolved gas ($V$):
$$V = \frac{mRT}{PM}$$
Now, substitute the value of $m$ from Equation 1 ($m = kP$) into this volume equation:
$$V = \frac{(kP)RT}{PM}$$
The pressure terms ($P$) cancel out completely:
$$V = \frac{kRT}{M}$$
At a constant temperature ($T$), Henry's constant ($k$), the universal gas constant ($R$), and the molar mass of the specific gas ($M$) are all strictly constant values.
Therefore, the calculated **Volume ($V$) equals a constant**. This mathematically proves that the physical volume of a gas dissolved in a given volume of solvent is entirely independent of the applied pressure.

### Q7(c) Define exothermic reaction. Discuss the effect of temperature on solubility of gas in liquid. (04)

**Answer:**
**Exothermic Reaction:** A chemical reaction or physical process that is accompanied by the release or evolution of heat energy into the surroundings. The enthalpy change ($\Delta H$) is negative.

**Effect of Temperature on Solubility of a Gas:**
The dissolution of a gas in a liquid solvent is almost universally an **exothermic process**. It is akin to condensation, where the gaseous molecules lose their high kinetic energy and settle into the liquid phase, releasing heat.
$$\text{Gas (solute)} + \text{Liquid (solvent)} \rightleftharpoons \text{Solution} + \text{Heat}$$
According to **Le Chatelier's Principle**, if a dynamic equilibrium is subjected to a stress (like an increase in temperature), the system will shift to counteract that stress. Since dissolving is exothermic, supplying external heat acts as a stress on the product side. To absorb this excess heat, the equilibrium violently shifts in the backward, endothermic direction.
Therefore, the dissolved gas molecules are driven out of the solution back into the vapor phase. **Conclusion: The solubility of any gas in a liquid always decreases as the temperature increases.** (This is why boiling water forcefully expels all dissolved oxygen).

### Q.1 Define the term solution. Molality is independent of temperature while molarity is a function of temperature- Give the reason. (5)
**Answer:**
**Solution:** A solution is defined as a perfectly homogeneous physical mixture of two or more chemically non-reacting substances, the composition of which can be varied within certain limits.
**Reason for Temperature Dependence:**
*   **Molality ($m$)** is defined as the number of moles of solute dissolved per kilogram ($1000 \text{ g}$) of the **solvent's mass**. Because the physical mass of a substance does not expand or contract when heated or cooled, molality is completely independent of temperature changes.
*   **Molarity ($M$)** is defined as the number of moles of solute dissolved per liter ($1 \text{ dm}^3$) of the **solution's volume**. Unlike mass, the physical volume of a liquid expands upon heating and contracts upon cooling. Therefore, as temperature changes, the volume changes, causing the molarity to change accordingly.

**Answer:**
**Henry's Law:** At a constant temperature, the mass of a gas dissolved in a given volume of a liquid solvent is directly proportional to the partial pressure of the gas present in equilibrium with the liquid. ($m = kP$)
**Limitations:**

1.  The pressure of the gas is not too high, and the temperature is not too low.
2.  The solution is dilute.
3.  The gas does not undergo any chemical reaction with the solvent (e.g., $NH_3$ in water reacts to form $NH_4OH$, violating the law).
4.  The gas does not undergo dissociation or association in the solution.

### Q.3 Distinguish between Ideal solution and Non-ideal solution. (4)
**Answer:**

| Feature | Ideal Solution | Non-Ideal Solution |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Raoult's Law** | Obeys Raoult's law perfectly at all concentrations and all temperatures. | Does not obey Raoult's law; shows positive or negative deviations. |
| **Enthalpy Change** | $\Delta H_{\text{mix}} = 0$ (No heat is absorbed or evolved when mixing). | $\Delta H_{\text{mix}} \neq 0$ (Heat is either evolved or absorbed). |
| **Volume Change** | $\Delta V_{\text{mix}} = 0$ (Total volume is exactly the sum of individual volumes). | $\Delta V_{\text{mix}} \neq 0$ (Volume expands or contracts upon mixing). |
| **Intermolecular Forces** | Solute-Solvent (A-B) attractive forces are perfectly equal to Solute-Solute (A-A) and Solvent-Solvent (B-B) forces. | Solute-Solvent (A-B) forces are either stronger or weaker than the pure component forces. |

### Q.4 Discuss briefly the effect of temperature on solubility of solid in liquid. (4)
**Answer:**
The effect of temperature on the solubility of a solid solute in a liquid solvent depends entirely on the enthalpy of solution ($\Delta H_{\text{sol}}$), governed by Le Chatelier's Principle:
1.  **Endothermic Dissolution ($\Delta H > 0$):** If the solid absorbs heat when dissolving (e.g., $KNO_3$, $NH_4Cl$, $KCl$), raising the temperature shifts the equilibrium forward to absorb the added heat. Thus, **solubility increases with an increase in temperature**.
2.  **Exothermic Dissolution ($\Delta H < 0$):** If the solid releases heat when dissolving (e.g., $Li_2SO_4$, $NaOH$, $Ca(OH)_2$), raising the temperature shifts the equilibrium backward to get rid of the excess heat. Thus, **solubility decreases with an increase in temperature**.

### Q.5 Calculate the molality of a solution prepared by dissolving 10 g of the antifreeze ethylene glycol ($C_2H_6O_2$) in 450 g of water. (3)
**Answer:**
**1. Calculate Molar Mass of Solute:**
Molar mass of Ethylene glycol ($C_2H_6O_2$) = $(2 \times 12) + (6 \times 1) + (2 \times 16) = 24 + 6 + 32 = 62 \text{ g/mol}$.
**2. Calculate Moles of Solute:**
Moles ($n$) = $\text{Given Mass} / \text{Molar Mass} = 10 \text{ g} / 62 \text{ g/mol} = 0.1613 \text{ moles}$.
**3. Calculate Mass of Solvent in kg:**
Mass of water = $450 \text{ g} = 0.45 \text{ kg}$.
**4. Calculate Molality ($m$):**
Molality = $\text{Moles of solute} / \text{Mass of solvent in kg}$
$m = 0.1613 \text{ moles} / 0.45 \text{ kg} = \mathbf{0.358 \text{ mol/kg (or 0.358 m)}}$.
