## 📄 Source: 2024 Exam

### Q.2(a) Define modern periodic law. Discuss the periodic properties of long form of periodic table. (06)

**Answer:**

**Modern Periodic Law:**
The Modern Periodic Law states that *"The physical and chemical properties of the elements are periodic functions of their atomic numbers."* This implies that when elements are arranged in increasing order of their atomic number, elements with similar properties recur at regular intervals or periods.

**Periodic Properties of the Long Form of the Periodic Table:**
Periodic properties are those characteristics of elements that are directly related to their electronic configuration and show a regular gradation as we move across a period (left to right) or down a group (top to bottom).
1. **Atomic Radius:**
   * *Across a period:* Decreases from left to right. The effective nuclear charge increases, pulling the valence shell closer to the nucleus.
   * *Down a group:* Increases. New electron shells are added, increasing the distance between the nucleus and the outermost electrons, despite the increase in nuclear charge.
2. **Ionization Potential (Energy):**
   * *Across a period:* Increases. Due to the decreasing atomic radius and increasing effective nuclear charge, more energy is required to remove an electron.
   * *Down a group:* Decreases. The atomic size increases, and the shielding effect of inner electrons makes the outermost electrons less tightly held.
3. **Electron Affinity:**
   * *Across a period:* Increases (becomes more negative). Non-metals on the right more readily accept electrons to complete their octet.
   * *Down a group:* Decreases. The incoming electron goes into a higher energy level further from the nucleus.
4. **Electronegativity:**
   * *Across a period:* Increases. Elements on the right have a stronger tendency to attract shared pairs of electrons.
   * *Down a group:* Decreases. The bonding electrons are further from the attractive pull of the nucleus.
5. **Metallic/Non-metallic Character:**
   * *Across a period:* Metallic character decreases and non-metallic character increases.
   * *Down a group:* Metallic character increases.

### Q.2(b) Classify the elements as per their electronic configuration. Give examples. (04)

**Answer:**

Based on their electronic configuration, elements in the long form of the periodic table are classified into four main blocks depending on the subshell into which the last differentiating electron enters:

1. **s-block elements:**
   * **Definition:** Elements in which the last electron enters the s-orbital of their outermost shell. They have the general valence electronic configuration $ns^{1-2}$.
   * **Characteristics:** They are highly reactive, soft, electropositive metals.
   * **Examples:** Alkali metals (e.g., Sodium, $Na: [Ne]3s^1$) and Alkaline earth metals (e.g., Calcium, $Ca: [Ar]4s^2$).

2. **p-block elements:**
   * **Definition:** Elements in which the last electron enters the p-orbital of the outermost shell. General valence configuration is $ns^2 np^{1-6}$.
   * **Characteristics:** Includes metals, non-metals, and metalloids. Together with s-block elements, they are called representative elements.
   * **Examples:** Carbon ($C: [He]2s^2 2p^2$), Oxygen ($O: [He]2s^2 2p^4$), Chlorine ($Cl: [Ne]3s^2 3p^5$).

3. **d-block elements (Transition Elements):**
   * **Definition:** Elements in which the last electron enters the d-orbital of the penultimate (n-1) shell. General configuration is $(n-1)d^{1-10} ns^{1-2}$.
   * **Characteristics:** Hard metals, show variable oxidation states, form colored ions, and act as good catalysts.
   * **Examples:** Iron ($Fe: [Ar]3d^6 4s^2$), Copper ($Cu: [Ar]3d^{10} 4s^1$).

4. **f-block elements (Inner Transition Elements):**
   * **Definition:** Elements in which the last electron enters the f-orbital of the anti-penultimate (n-2) shell. General configuration is $(n-2)f^{1-14} (n-1)d^{0-1} ns^2$.
   * **Characteristics:** Heavy metals, many are radioactive (Actinides). They are divided into Lanthanides and Actinides.
   * **Examples:** Uranium ($U: [Rn]5f^3 6d^1 7s^2$), Cerium ($Ce: [Xe]4f^1 5d^1 6s^2$).

### Q.2(c) Find the redox properties of following compounds from their electronic configuration.
$N_2O, NO, N_2O_3, N_2O_4, \text{ and } N_2O_5$ (06)

**Answer:**

**Electronic Configuration Context:**
Nitrogen ($N$, Atomic No. 7) has the valence electronic configuration: $[He] 2s^2 2p^3$.
It possesses 5 valence electrons. To achieve a stable octet, it can either lose all 5 electrons (maximum oxidation state = +5) or gain 3 electrons (minimum oxidation state = -3). Thus, Nitrogen can exhibit any oxidation state between -3 and +5.

**Redox Principle:**
*   If an element is in its **highest** possible oxidation state, it cannot lose more electrons (cannot be oxidized further). It can only gain electrons, acting purely as an **Oxidizing Agent**.
*   If an element is in its **lowest** possible oxidation state, it can only lose electrons, acting purely as a **Reducing Agent**.
*   If an element is in an **intermediate** oxidation state, it can either gain or lose electrons, thereby acting as **both** an oxidizing and a reducing agent.

**Analysis of the Compounds:**
We calculate the oxidation state (O.S.) of Nitrogen in each oxide (assuming Oxygen is -2):

1.  **$N_2O$ (Nitrous oxide):** $2x + (-2) = 0 \implies x = +1$
    *   N is in an intermediate state (+1). It can be oxidized to higher states or reduced to 0 or negative states.
    *   **Redox Property:** Can act as both an oxidizing and a reducing agent.
2.  **$NO$ (Nitric oxide):** $x + (-2) = 0 \implies x = +2$
    *   N is in an intermediate state (+2).
    *   **Redox Property:** Can act as both an oxidizing and a reducing agent.
3.  **$N_2O_3$ (Dinitrogen trioxide):** $2x + 3(-2) = 0 \implies 2x = +6 \implies x = +3$
    *   N is in an intermediate state (+3).
    *   **Redox Property:** Can act as both an oxidizing and a reducing agent.
4.  **$N_2O_4$ (Dinitrogen tetroxide):** $2x + 4(-2) = 0 \implies 2x = +8 \implies x = +4$
    *   N is in an intermediate state (+4).
    *   **Redox Property:** Can act as both an oxidizing and a reducing agent.
5.  **$N_2O_5$ (Dinitrogen pentoxide):** $2x + 5(-2) = 0 \implies 2x = +10 \implies x = +5$
    *   N is in its **maximum possible oxidation state (+5)** because it has only 5 valence electrons to share/lose. It cannot be oxidized any further.
    *   **Redox Property:** Can act **strictly as an oxidizing agent** (it can only be reduced).

### Q.6(a) Define and explain the following terms: (i) Shielding effect (ii) Effective nuclear charge (04)

**Answer:**

**(i) Shielding Effect (or Screening Effect):**
In a multi-electron atom, the outermost (valence) electrons are attracted towards the positively charged nucleus, but they are also repelled by the negatively charged electrons present in the inner electron shells. This repulsion by the inner-shell electrons partially blocks or "shields" the valence electrons from the full attractive pull of the nucleus. This phenomenon is known as the shielding or screening effect.

**(ii) Effective Nuclear Charge ($Z_{eff}$):**
Because of the shielding effect, the actual net positive charge experienced by an outer electron is less than the actual total nuclear charge ($Z$). This reduced, net positive charge actually felt by the electron is called the Effective Nuclear Charge ($Z_{eff}$).
It is mathematically expressed as:
$$Z_{eff} = Z - \sigma$$
Where $Z$ is the atomic number (actual nuclear charge) and $\sigma$ is the shielding (or screening) constant caused by the inner electrons.

### Q.6(b) "4s orbital is filled before 3d orbitals"-Evaluate the statement applying effective nuclear charge. (04)

**Answer:**

**Evaluation based on Effective Nuclear Charge and Penetration:**
According to the Aufbau principle, orbitals are filled in order of increasing energy. The energy of an orbital in a multi-electron atom is determined by the effective nuclear charge ($Z_{eff}$) experienced by an electron in that orbital, which in turn is affected by shielding and orbital penetration.

Although the $4s$ orbital is in a higher principal shell ($n=4$) than the $3d$ orbital ($n=3$), the $4s$ orbital has an azimuthal quantum number $l=0$, while the $3d$ orbital has $l=2$.
Because the $s$-orbital has a spherical shape with electron density maxima near the nucleus, an electron in the $4s$ orbital significantly **penetrates** the inner electron core. In contrast, the $3d$ orbital is highly diffuse and its electron density lies further out, causing it to be heavily shielded by the $1s, 2s, 2p, 3s$, and $3p$ electrons.

Due to this superior penetration, an electron in the $4s$ orbital experiences less shielding from the inner core and therefore feels a **higher effective nuclear charge ($Z_{eff}$)** compared to an electron in the $3d$ orbital. The stronger attraction to the nucleus gives the $4s$ orbital lower potential energy than the $3d$ orbital.
*(This is also verified by the $(n+l)$ rule: For $4s, 4+0=4$; for $3d, 3+2=5$. The lower value indicates lower energy).*
Consequently, the $4s$ orbital is lower in energy and is filled with electrons before the $3d$ orbitals.

### Q.6(c) Compute the electronegativity of carbon at Pauling scale. Given that $E_{H-H} = 104.2 \text{ Kcal mol}^{-1}$, $E_{C-C} = 83.1 \text{ Kcal mol}^{-1}$, $E_{C-H} = 98.8 \text{ Kcal mol}^{-1}$. Electronegativity of hydrogen = 2.1. (04)

**Answer:**

**Given Data:**
*   Bond energy of H-H, $E_{H-H} = 104.2 \text{ kcal/mol}$
*   Bond energy of C-C, $E_{C-C} = 83.1 \text{ kcal/mol}$
*   Bond energy of C-H, $E_{C-H} = 98.8 \text{ kcal/mol}$
*   Electronegativity of Hydrogen, $X_H = 2.1$

**To Find:**
*   Electronegativity of Carbon, $X_C$

**Solution:**
According to Pauling's scale, the difference in electronegativity between two atoms A and B is given by:
$$X_A - X_B = 0.208 \sqrt{\Delta}$$
*(Where $\Delta$ is the resonance energy in kcal/mol).*

$\Delta$ is calculated as the difference between the actual bond energy of the A-B bond and the geometric mean of the A-A and B-B bond energies:
$$\Delta = E_{A-B} - \sqrt{E_{A-A} \times E_{B-B}}$$

Applying this to Carbon and Hydrogen:
$$\Delta = E_{C-H} - \sqrt{E_{C-C} \times E_{H-H}}$$
$$\Delta = 98.8 - \sqrt{83.1 \times 104.2}$$
$$\Delta = 98.8 - \sqrt{8659.02}$$
$$\Delta = 98.8 - 93.054$$
$$\Delta = 5.746 \text{ kcal/mol}$$

Now, substituting $\Delta$ back into Pauling's equation (assuming Carbon is more electronegative than Hydrogen, so $X_C > X_H$):
$$X_C - X_H = 0.208 \sqrt{5.746}$$
$$X_C - 2.1 = 0.208 \times 2.397$$
$$X_C - 2.1 = 0.4985$$
$$X_C = 2.1 + 0.4985$$
$$X_C = 2.5985 \approx 2.6$$

**Final Answer:** The computed electronegativity of Carbon on the Pauling scale is approximately **$2.6$**.

### Q.7(a) Modern periodic table is more clear than Mendeleev's periodic table. Explain why? (04)

**Answer:**

The Modern Periodic Table is conceptually clearer and scientifically more rigorous than Mendeleev's Periodic Table for the following fundamental reasons:

1.  **Basis of Classification:** Mendeleev based his table on **Atomic Mass**. This led to anomalies where he had to break his own rule to group elements with similar properties (e.g., placing Argon (mass 39.9) before Potassium (mass 39.1)). The Modern table is based on **Atomic Number** (number of protons), which is a much more fundamental and precise property of an element. This naturally resolves all the mass anomalies.
2.  **Position of Isotopes:** Because Mendeleev's table was based on atomic mass, isotopes of the same element (having different masses) technically required different positions in the table, which confused the chemical grouping. In the Modern table, since all isotopes of an element have the identical atomic number, they naturally and correctly occupy the exact same single position.
3.  **Correlation with Electronic Configuration:** The Modern periodic table perfectly aligns with quantum mechanics and the Aufbau principle. The arrangement into s, p, d, and f blocks directly correlates with the outermost electronic configuration of the atoms. This provides a clear, logical explanation for *why* periodicity occurs (elements in the same group have the same valence electron configuration), which Mendeleev's table could not explain mathematically.

### Q.7(b) Classify the elements according to their electronic configuration. Give examples of each. (04)

**Answer:**

Based on the subshell (orbital) into which the last differentiating electron enters, elements are classified into four blocks:

1.  **s-block Elements:** The last electron enters the outermost s-orbital. They include Group 1 (Alkali metals) and Group 2 (Alkaline earth metals). General configuration: $ns^{1-2}$.
    *   *Example:* Sodium (Na, Z=11): $1s^2 2s^2 2p^6 3s^1$
2.  **p-block Elements:** The last electron enters the outermost p-orbital. They include Groups 13 to 18 (comprising metals, non-metals, and metalloids). General configuration: $ns^2 np^{1-6}$.
    *   *Example:* Chlorine (Cl, Z=17): $1s^2 2s^2 2p^6 3s^2 3p^5$
3.  **d-block Elements (Transition Elements):** The last electron enters the penultimate (n-1) d-orbital. They lie between the s and p blocks. General configuration: $(n-1)d^{1-10} ns^{1-2}$.
    *   *Example:* Iron (Fe, Z=26): $[Ar] 4s^2 3d^6$
4.  **f-block Elements (Inner Transition Elements):** The last electron enters the anti-penultimate (n-2) f-orbital. They are placed at the bottom of the table (Lanthanides and Actinides). General configuration: $(n-2)f^{1-14} (n-1)d^{0-1} ns^2$.
    *   *Example:* Uranium (U, Z=92): $[Rn] 7s^2 5f^3 6d^1$

### Q.7(c) Define ionization potential of element. It is a periodic property of elements. Explain. (04)

**Answer:**

**Ionization Potential (or Ionization Energy):**
It is defined as the minimum amount of energy required to completely remove the most loosely bound outermost electron from an isolated, neutral gaseous atom in its ground state to form a positive ion (cation).

**Explanation of its Periodicity:**
Ionization potential is a periodic property because it varies predictably in accordance with the atomic structure across periods and down groups in the periodic table.

1.  **Variation along a Period (Left to Right):**
    As we move across a period, the atomic number increases, meaning the nuclear charge increases. However, the new electrons are added to the same main energy shell, so the shielding effect remains roughly constant. This results in an increase in the Effective Nuclear Charge ($Z_{eff}$). The nucleus pulls the valence shell closer (atomic radius decreases), holding the electrons much more tightly. Consequently, more energy is required to remove an electron, so **Ionization Potential increases across a period**.
2.  **Variation down a Group (Top to Bottom):**
    As we move down a group, new principal energy shells are added. Even though the nuclear charge increases, the distance of the valence electrons from the nucleus (atomic radius) increases significantly. Furthermore, the inner shells provide a strong shielding effect, screening the valence electrons from the nuclear pull. The increase in atomic size and shielding effect outweighs the increase in nuclear charge. As a result, the valence electrons are held less tightly, and **Ionization Potential decreases down a group**.

### Q3(a) What is periodic table? Why the long form of periodic Table is more clear than that of Mendeleef's periodic Table? (06)

**Answer:**

**Periodic Table:**
The periodic table is a tabular arrangement of all known chemical elements, organized systematically in order of their increasing atomic numbers, electron configurations, and recurring chemical properties. It groups elements with similar characteristics into vertical columns (Groups) and horizontal rows (Periods), serving as a unified reference framework for chemistry.

**Why the Long Form (Modern) is clearer than Mendeleev's:**
The Long Form (Modern) Periodic Table is conceptually clearer and scientifically more rigorous for three fundamental reasons:

1.  **Basis of Classification (Atomic Number vs. Mass):** Mendeleev based his table on **Atomic Mass**. This led to unexplainable anomalies where he had to break his own rule to group elements correctly (e.g., placing Argon, mass 39.9, before Potassium, mass 39.1). The Modern table is based on **Atomic Number** (number of protons). This is a much more fundamental property that naturally resolves all of Mendeleev's mass anomalies without exception.
2.  **Position of Isotopes:** Because Mendeleev's table relied on atomic mass, isotopes of the exact same element (which have different masses) technically required entirely different positions in the table, destroying chemical groupings. In the Modern table, since all isotopes of an element have the identical atomic number, they naturally and correctly occupy the exact same single slot.
3.  **Correlation with Electronic Configuration:** The Modern table's structure perfectly aligns with quantum mechanics and the Aufbau principle. The arrangement into s, p, d, and f blocks directly and visibly correlates with the outermost electronic configuration of the atoms. This provides a clear, logical, and visual explanation for *why* periodicity occurs (elements in the same group have the exact same valence electron configuration), something Mendeleev's table could not mathematically explain.

### Q3(b) Explain the types of elements on the basis of electronic configuration. (04)

**Answer:**
Based on the specific subshell (orbital) into which the last differentiating valence electron enters, the elements in the periodic table are classified into four distinct structural blocks:

1.  **s-block Elements:** The last differentiating electron enters the outermost s-orbital. Because the s-orbital can hold a maximum of 2 electrons, this block comprises Group 1 (Alkali metals) and Group 2 (Alkaline earth metals). General valence configuration: $ns^{1-2}$. These are highly reactive, electropositive metals.
2.  **p-block Elements:** The last electron enters the outermost p-orbital. It includes Groups 13 through 18. General valence configuration: $ns^2 np^{1-6}$. This is the only block containing a mix of metals, metalloids, and all non-metals (including the noble gases).
3.  **d-block Elements (Transition Elements):** The last electron enters the inner, penultimate (n-1) d-orbital. They lie strategically between the s and p blocks (Groups 3-12). General configuration: $(n-1)d^{1-10} ns^{1-2}$. These are hard metals that typically exhibit variable oxidation states, form colored ions, and act as excellent catalysts.
4.  **f-block Elements (Inner Transition Elements):** The last electron enters the deeply buried anti-penultimate (n-2) f-orbital. They are placed separately at the bottom of the table to maintain its structural shape. They consist of two series: Lanthanides (4f series) and Actinides (5f series, mostly radioactive). General configuration: $(n-2)f^{1-14} (n-1)d^{0-1} ns^2$.

### Q3(c) What is successive ionization potential? (02)

**Answer:**
**Successive Ionization Potential** refers to the sequence of energy amounts required to remove subsequent electrons from an atom one by one.
*   The energy required to remove the very first, most loosely bound electron from a neutral gaseous atom is the First Ionization Potential ($IP_1$).
*   The energy required to remove the second electron from the resulting unipositive ion ($M^+$) is the Second Ionization Potential ($IP_2$), and so forth.
It is always observed that $IP_1 < IP_2 < IP_3 \dots$ because as each electron is removed, the net positive charge on the ion increases, causing the nucleus to pull the remaining electrons much more tightly, requiring significantly more energy to remove the next one.

### (b) Electron affinity and Electronegativity.
**Answer:**

*   **Electron Affinity ($EA$):** It is a measurable, absolute energy value. It is defined as the exact amount of energy released when a single electron is added to the outermost shell of an isolated, neutral gaseous atom to form a stable negative ion (anion). It measures the raw attraction an isolated atom has for an extra electron. Halogens have the highest negative electron affinities.
*   **Electronegativity ($EN$):** It is a relative, dimensionless scale (like the Pauling scale). It is defined as the relative tendency or ability of an atom, *when it is bonded within a molecule*, to attract the shared pair of covalent electrons firmly towards its own nucleus. It measures attraction in a bonded state. Fluorine is the most electronegative element (4.0).
