# Activated Complex Theory
> **Topic:** Chemical Kinetics | **Priority:** 🔴 CRITICAL | **Exam Frequency:** ?/7 exams

## 📌 Quick Summary
> Activated-Complex Theory (Transition State Theory) postulates that reactants must first acquire a specific Activation Energy ($E_a$) to form a highly unstable intermediate called the "Activated Complex" at the peak of a potential energy barrier before they can decompose into final products.

## 📖 Core Theory

### 1. The Core Postulate
*   Formulated by Eyring and Polanyi, this theory provides a detailed microscopic view of reaction kinetics.
*   It dictates that a chemical reaction does **not** occur instantaneously upon the simple collision of reactant molecules.
*   Instead, the colliding reactant molecules must first acquire a specific minimum threshold of energy. This energy allows them to merge and form a highly energetic, unstable, and temporary intermediate structure known as the **Activated Complex** (or Transition State).

### 2. The Energy Barrier & Activation Energy
*   The activated complex represents the absolute peak of the potential energy barrier that separates the reactants from the products.
*   The extra, external energy required by the ground-state reactants to reach this peak is called the **Activation Energy ($E_a$)**. 

### 3. Decomposition
*   Once the activated complex is formed at the peak of the barrier, it exists in a state of delicate equilibrium.
*   It has two highly probable paths: it can either decompose "forward" to form the final, more stable products, or decompose "backward" to reform the original reactants.
*   The overall rate of the chemical reaction is directly proportional to the concentration of the activated complex existing at the peak of the barrier.

### 4. Graphical Representation (Potential Energy Diagram)
When mapping the energy path of a reaction on a 2D graph (Y-axis = Potential Energy, X-axis = Reaction Coordinate):
1.  **Reactant Ground State ($E_R$):** The curve begins at a specific energy level representing the average ground state energy of the reactants.
2.  **Activation Energy ($E_a$):** The curve slopes steeply upward, representing the energy input required to stretch and break initial bonds.
3.  **Transition State Peak:** The absolute highest point of the curve. This is where the short-lived **Activated Complex** exists at its maximum potential energy.
4.  **Product Ground State ($E_P$):** From the peak, the curve slopes rapidly downwards as new bonds form (releasing energy), eventually settling at a final energy level representing the ground state of the products.
5.  **Enthalpy Change ($\Delta H$):** The vertical difference between the starting Reactant energy ($E_R$) and the final Product energy ($E_P$). If $E_P < E_R$, the reaction releases net heat (Exothermic).

### 🎯 2018 Q4(b) State and explain activated-complex theory with graphical representation. (04)
> **Source:** answers/2018.md

**Answer:**

**Activated-Complex Theory (Transition State Theory):**
Formulated by Eyring and Polanyi, this theory provides a detailed microscopic view of reaction kinetics.
*   **Postulate:** A chemical reaction does not occur instantaneously upon collision of reactant molecules. Instead, the colliding reactant molecules must first acquire a specific minimum threshold energy to merge and form a highly energetic, unstable, and temporary intermediate structure called the **Activated Complex** (or Transition State).
*   **Energy Barrier:** The activated complex represents the absolute peak of the potential energy barrier separating reactants from products. The extra energy required by the reactants to reach this peak is called the **Activation Energy ($E_a$)**.
*   **Decomposition:** Once the activated complex is formed at the peak of the barrier, it has two highly probable paths: it can either decompose forward to form the final, more stable products, or decompose backward to reform the original reactants. The rate of the overall reaction is directly proportional to the concentration of the activated complex at the peak of the barrier.

**Graphical Representation (Potential Energy Diagram):**
Imagine a 2D graph mapping the energy path of a reaction.
1.  **Axes:** The Y-axis represents Potential Energy, and the X-axis represents the Reaction Coordinate (the progress of the reaction from start to finish).
2.  **The Curve:** The curve starts at a specific energy level representing the ground state energy of the **Reactants ($E_R$)**. As the reaction progresses, the curve slopes steeply upward, representing the energy input (Activation Energy, $E_a$) required to break initial bonds.
3.  **The Peak:** The highest point of the curve is the **Transition State**, where the short-lived **Activated Complex** exists at its maximum potential energy.
4.  **The Drop:** From the peak, the curve slopes downwards as new bonds form, releasing energy. It settles at a final energy level representing the ground state energy of the **Products ($E_P$)**.
5.  **Enthalpy Change:** The vertical difference between the starting Reactant energy ($E_R$) and final Product energy ($E_P$) is the overall heat of reaction ($\Delta H$). If $E_P < E_R$, the reaction is exothermic.

## ⚡ Exam Tips & Common Mistakes
*   **Drawing the Graph:** If a question asks to "explain with graphical representation," drawing a quick sketch of the Potential Energy vs Reaction Coordinate curve is absolutely mandatory for full marks. Ensure you clearly label $E_a$, $\Delta H$, Reactants, Products, and the Activated Complex at the peak.
*   **Definitions:** Do not confuse "Activation Energy" (the *extra* energy needed) with "Threshold Energy" (the *total* energy needed from absolute zero). $E_{\text{Threshold}} = E_{\text{Reactants}} + E_a$.
