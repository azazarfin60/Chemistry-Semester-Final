# Henrys Law
> **Topic:** Solutions | **Priority:** 🟡 MEDIUM | **Exam Frequency:** ?/7 exams

## 📌 Quick Summary
> Henry's Law dictates the solubility of gases in liquids, stating that the mass of dissolved gas is directly proportional to its partial pressure. This section covers the fundamental theory, the four critical limitations of the law, and the mathematical derivation proving that the *volume* of dissolved gas is completely independent of pressure.

## 📖 Core Theory

### 1. Statement and Explanation
**Henry's Law** states that: *"At a constant temperature, the mass of a gas dissolved in a given volume of a liquid is directly proportional to the partial pressure of that gas present in equilibrium with the liquid."*

Alternatively, expressed in terms of mole fraction: The partial pressure ($p$) of the gas in the vapor phase is proportional to the mole fraction ($x$) of the gas in the solution.
> **$$p = K_H \cdot x$$**
Where $K_H$ is the **Henry's law constant**.

**Physical Meaning:** When a gas is in contact with a liquid, gas molecules continuously enter and leave the liquid phase. If the pressure of the gas above the liquid is increased, more gas molecules are forced to strike the liquid surface and dissolve. This shifts the equilibrium to accommodate a higher concentration of dissolved gas (this is the core principle behind carbonated soda). 
*(Note: A higher value of $K_H$ for a specific gas means it has a lower solubility).*

### 2. Limitations of Henry's Law
Henry's Law is not a universal law; it is strictly valid only for ideal gases and ideal solutions under four specific conditions:
1.  **Moderate Pressure:** At extremely high pressures, gases behave non-ideally and deviate from the law.
2.  **Moderate Temperature:** At very low temperatures, the behavior of the gas also deviates from ideality.
3.  **No Chemical Reaction:** The gas must not react chemically with the solvent. For example, $NH_3$ dissolved in water heavily violates Henry's Law because it reacts to form Ammonium Hydroxide ($NH_4OH$).
4.  **No Dissociation or Association:** The gas molecules must remain intact as single units. They should not dissociate into ions (like $HCl$ separating into $H^+$ and $Cl^-$ in water) or associate with each other in the solvent.

## 🧮 Key Derivations & Equations

### Proof: Dissolved Volume is Independent of Pressure
While Henry's Law proves that the *mass* of dissolved gas increases with pressure, the physical *volume* of that gas (if extracted and measured at that same applied pressure) remains perfectly constant.

**Step 1:** Let $m$ be the mass of the gas dissolved at an applied pressure $P$. From Henry's Law:
$$m = kP \quad \text{--- (Equation 1)}$$

**Step 2:** Assume the dissolved gas behaves ideally. Apply the Ideal Gas Equation:
$$PV = nRT$$

**Step 3:** Substitute moles ($n$) with mass divided by Molar mass ($m/M$):
$$PV = \frac{m}{M} RT$$

**Step 4:** Rearrange the equation to solve for the Volume of the gas ($V$):
$$V = \frac{mRT}{PM}$$

**Step 5:** Substitute the value of $m$ from Equation 1 ($m = kP$) into this volume equation:
$$V = \frac{(kP)RT}{PM}$$

**Final Result:** The pressure terms ($P$) in the numerator and denominator cancel out completely:
$$V = \frac{kRT}{M}$$

Since $k$ (Henry's constant), $R$ (universal gas constant), $T$ (constant temperature), and $M$ (molar mass) are all strict constants, the calculated **Volume ($V$) is a constant**. This mathematically proves that the physical volume of a gas dissolved in a given volume of solvent is entirely independent of the applied pressure.

## ⚡ Exam Tips & Common Mistakes
*   **The Reaction Trap:** This is a very common sub-question. If asked *"Why doesn't Ammonia ($NH_3$) obey Henry's law in water?"*, you must explicitly state that it chemically reacts with the solvent to form Ammonium Hydroxide ($NH_4OH$). This violates limitation #3. The same logic applies to $HCl$ in water (it dissociates into ions, violating limitation #4).
*   **Mass vs Volume Trick:** Be very careful when reading true/false or multiple-choice questions. If they ask about the **mass** of dissolved gas, it is directly proportional to pressure. If they ask about the **volume** of dissolved gas, it is completely independent of pressure.
