# Ideal vs Non Ideal Solutions
> **Topic:** Solutions | **Priority:** 🟡 MEDIUM | **Exam Frequency:** 1/7 exams

## 📌 Quick Summary
> This section contrasts Ideal solutions (which perfectly obey Raoult's law with zero enthalpy and volume changes) against Non-ideal solutions. The defining factor separating the two is whether the intermolecular forces between the mixed components are equal to or different from the forces in the pure components.

## 📖 Core Theory

### 1. Introduction to Raoult's Law
Raoult's law is the fundamental benchmark for solution ideality. It states that the partial vapor pressure of any volatile component in a solution is directly proportional to its mole fraction in that solution.
> **$$P_A = P_A^\circ \cdot x_A$$**
*(Where $P_A^\circ$ is the vapor pressure of the pure liquid A, and $x_A$ is its mole fraction).*

### 2. Characteristics of Ideal Solutions
An ideal solution perfectly obeys Raoult's law under all conditions of temperature and concentration.
*   **The Physics (Intermolecular Forces):** An ideal solution forms when the attractive intermolecular forces between the solute (A) and solvent (B) are absolutely identical to the cohesive forces within the pure components.
    *   $(A-B) \text{ interactions} = (A-A) \text{ and } (B-B) \text{ interactions}$.
    *   Because the forces are exactly the same, molecules escape into the vapor phase just as easily as they did when they were pure liquids.
*   **Thermodynamics:**
    *   $\Delta H_{\text{mix}} = 0$: No heat is absorbed or evolved during mixing because no net change in bond energy occurs.
    *   $\Delta V_{\text{mix}} = 0$: The total volume of the solution is exactly the sum of the individual unmixed volumes.

### 3. Characteristics of Non-Ideal Solutions
A non-ideal solution deviates from Raoult's law (either positive or negative deviation).
*   **The Physics (Intermolecular Forces):** The $(A-B)$ interactions are either significantly stronger or significantly weaker than the pure $(A-A)$ or $(B-B)$ interactions.
*   **Thermodynamics:**
    *   $\Delta H_{\text{mix}} \neq 0$: Mixing is either exothermic (heat released) or endothermic (heat absorbed) because new, differently-strength bonds are formed.
    *   $\Delta V_{\text{mix}} \neq 0$: The total volume either expands or contracts upon mixing.

## 📊 Comparison Tables

### Ideal vs. Non-Ideal Solutions

| Feature | Ideal Solution | Non-Ideal Solution |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Raoult's Law** | Obeys Raoult's law perfectly at all concentrations and all temperatures. | Does not obey Raoult's law; shows positive or negative deviations. |
| **Enthalpy Change** | $\Delta H_{\text{mix}} = 0$ (No heat is absorbed or evolved when mixing). | $\Delta H_{\text{mix}} \neq 0$ (Heat is either evolved or absorbed). |
| **Volume Change** | $\Delta V_{\text{mix}} = 0$ (Total volume is exactly the sum of individual volumes). | $\Delta V_{\text{mix}} \neq 0$ (Volume expands or contracts upon mixing). |
| **Intermolecular Forces** | Solute-Solvent (A-B) attractive forces are perfectly equal to Solute-Solute (A-A) and Solvent-Solvent (B-B) forces. | Solute-Solvent (A-B) forces are either stronger or weaker than the pure component forces. |

## ⚡ Exam Tips & Common Mistakes
*   **The "Why" of Volume Change:** Examiners love to ask conceptual questions about *why* $\Delta V_{\text{mix}} \neq 0$ for non-ideal solutions. If A-B intermolecular forces are **stronger** than the pure forces, the molecules pull closer together, causing the total solution volume to shrink ($\Delta V_{\text{mix}} < 0$). If A-B forces are **weaker**, the molecules don't pack as tightly, causing the volume to expand ($\Delta V_{\text{mix}} > 0$).
