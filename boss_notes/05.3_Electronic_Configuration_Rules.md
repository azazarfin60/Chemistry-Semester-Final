# Electronic Configuration Rules
> **Topic:** Atomic Structure | **Priority:** 🟠 HIGH | **Exam Frequency:** 1/7 exams

## 📌 Quick Summary
> The arrangement of electrons in an atom is strictly governed by three fundamental rules: the Aufbau principle (which dictates filling order based on $(n+l)$ energy), Pauli's Exclusion Principle (which restricts orbital capacity to two electrons of opposite spin), and Hund's Rule (which maximizes unpaired, parallel spins in degenerate orbitals for stability).

## 📖 Core Theory

### 1. The Aufbau Principle & $(n+l)$ Rule
*   **Aufbau Principle:** "Aufbau" is German for "building up." The principle states that in the ground state of an atom or ion, electrons fill atomic orbitals in increasing order of orbital energy levels. The lowest energy orbitals are completely filled before electrons enter higher energy levels.
*   **(n+l) Rule (Madelung's Rule):** This rule mathematically dictates the relative energy order of orbitals.
    1.  Orbitals with a **lower sum of $(n+l)$** possess lower potential energy and are filled first.
        *(Example: $4s$ has $n+l = 4+0=4$. $3d$ has $n+l = 3+2=5$. Thus, $4s$ is lower energy and fills before $3d$).*
    2.  If two distinct orbitals happen to have the **exact same $(n+l)$ value**, the orbital with the **lower principal quantum number ($n$)** has the lower energy and is filled first.
        *(Example: $3p$ has $3+1=4$. $4s$ has $4+0=4$. $3p$ is filled first because $3 < 4$).*

### 2. Pauli's Exclusion Principle
*   **Statement:** "No two electrons in the same atom can have an identical set of all four quantum numbers ($n, l, m_l, m_s$)."
*   **Implication:** If two electrons occupy the exact same orbital, they already share the same $n, l,$ and $m_l$. Therefore, their spin quantum number ($m_s$) must be different ($+1/2$ and $-1/2$). This fundamentally restricts the capacity of any single orbital to a maximum of only **two electrons** with opposed spins.

### 3. Hund's Rule of Maximum Multiplicity
*   **Statement:** This rule governs the filling of **degenerate orbitals** (orbitals with identical energy, such as the three p-orbitals or five d-orbitals). It states that electron pairing will not occur in degenerate orbitals until each orbital of that given subshell is singly occupied.
*   **Parallel Spins:** Furthermore, all these singly occupying electrons must have **parallel spins** (the same spin direction). This arrangement minimizes inter-electronic electrostatic repulsion and maximizes atomic stability.

## 🏆 Golden Questions (Past Exam Archive)
### 🎯 2018 (c) Hund's principle and (n+l) rules
> **Source:** answers/2018.md

**Answer:**
**Hund's Rule of Maximum Multiplicity:** This rule governs the filling of electrons in degenerate orbitals (orbitals with identical energy, such as the three p-orbitals or five d-orbitals). It states that electron pairing will not occur in degenerate orbitals until each orbital of that given subshell is singly occupied with an electron, and all these single electrons must have parallel spins. This arrangement minimizes inter-electronic repulsion and maximizes stability.

**(n+l) Rule (Madelung's Rule):** This rule is used to determine the relative energy order of different atomic orbitals, deciding the sequence in which they are filled according to the Aufbau principle.
1.  Orbitals with a lower sum of $(n+l)$ have lower potential energy and are filled with electrons first. (e.g., $4s$: $n+l = 4+0=4$; $3d$: $n+l = 3+2=5$. Thus, $4s$ is filled before $3d$).
2.  If two distinct orbitals happen to have the exact same $(n+l)$ value, the orbital with the lower principal quantum number '$n$' has the lower energy and is filled first. (e.g., $3p$: $3+1=4$; $4s$: $4+0=4$. The $3p$ orbital is filled first because $3 < 4$).

## ⚡ Exam Tips & Common Mistakes
*   **The Big Exceptions (Cr & Cu):** The most frequently tested concepts regarding electronic configuration are the anomalous behaviors of Chromium ($Z=24$) and Copper ($Z=29$). They violate the standard Aufbau $(n+l)$ sequence.
    *   **Chromium ($Cr$):** $[Ar] \, 4s^1 \, 3d^5$ *(NOT $4s^2 \, 3d^4$)*.
    *   **Copper ($Cu$):** $[Ar] \, 4s^1 \, 3d^{10}$ *(NOT $4s^2 \, 3d^9$)*.
    *   **The Reason:** An exactly half-filled ($d^5$) or fully-filled ($d^{10}$) subshell provides exceptional stability due to perfect symmetrical charge distribution and maximum exchange energy. A $4s$ electron promotes itself to $3d$ to achieve this stable state.
*   **Hund's Rule Vocabulary:** When defining Hund's rule, omitting the words **"degenerate orbitals"** or **"parallel spins"** will cost you marks.
