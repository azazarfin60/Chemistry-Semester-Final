# Faradays Laws
> **Topic:** Electrochemistry | **Priority:** 🟠 HIGH | **Exam Frequency:** ?/7 exams

## 📌 Quick Summary
> Electrolysis involves chemical decomposition driven by electricity. Michael Faraday formulated two quantitative laws governing this process. The First Law links the mass of a deposited substance directly to the total quantity of charge passed ($W=ZIt$), while the Second Law dictates that masses deposited by the same charge across different cells are proportional to their equivalent weights.

## 📖 Core Theory

### 1. What is Electrolysis?
Electrolysis is the process of chemical decomposition of an electrolyte (either in an aqueous solution or a molten state) caused by the continuous passage of a direct electric current through it.
*   It inherently involves **non-spontaneous redox reactions** that are forcibly driven by external electrical energy.
*   During this process, cations are reduced at the cathode, and anions are oxidized at the anode.

### 2. Faraday's Laws of Electrolysis
In 1833, Michael Faraday formulated two fundamental, quantitative laws governing the exact extent of chemical change (specifically the mass of substance deposited or liberated) that occurs during electrolysis.

**A) Faraday's First Law:**
*   **Statement:** The mass ($W$) of any substance deposited or liberated at a specific electrode during electrolysis is directly proportional to the total quantity of electricity/charge ($Q$) passed through the electrolyte.
*   **Mathematical Expression:** Since electric charge $Q = I \times t$ (Current in Amperes $\times$ time in seconds), the proportionality can be written as:
    $$W \propto Q \implies W \propto It$$
    > **$$W = Z \cdot I \cdot t$$**
*   **Electrochemical Equivalent ($Z$):** The proportionality constant $Z$ is specific to the substance. It is formally defined as the exact mass of a substance deposited when exactly 1 Ampere of current flows for 1 second (which is equivalent to 1 Coulomb of charge). Its unit is generally $g/C$.

**B) Faraday's Second Law:**
*   **Statement:** When the exact same quantity of electricity is passed through different electrolytic cells connected in series, the masses of the respective substances deposited at the electrodes are directly proportional to their chemical equivalent weights ($E$).
*   **Mathematical Expression:** If $W_1$ and $W_2$ are the masses of two different metals deposited, and $E_1$ and $E_2$ are their respective equivalent weights, then $W \propto E$, leading to:
    > **$$\frac{W_1}{W_2} = \frac{E_1}{E_2}$$**
*   **Significance:** This law mathematically establishes the concept of the Faraday constant. It proves that **1 Faraday of charge** ($96,485 \text{ Coulombs}$, which is the charge of one mole of electrons) will deposit exactly one gram-equivalent weight of any substance.

## 🔢 Solved Numericals

### 🎯 2019 Q.8(c) First Law Application (04 marks)
**Question:** $0.1978 \text{ g}$ of Cu is deposited by a current of $0.2 \text{ amp}$ for $50 \text{ min}$. What is the electrochemical equivalent of Cu?

**Given Data:**
*   Mass of Copper deposited ($W$) = $0.1978 \text{ g}$
*   Current passed ($I$) = $0.2 \text{ Ampere}$
*   Time duration ($t$) = $50 \text{ minutes}$ (Must convert to seconds: $50 \times 60 = 3000 \text{ seconds}$)

**To Find:**
*   Electrochemical equivalent ($Z$) of Cu.

**Solution:**
According to Faraday's First Law of Electrolysis, the mass deposited is proportional to the current and time:
$$W = Z \times I \times t$$

Rearranging the formula to specifically solve for the electrochemical equivalent ($Z$):
$$Z = \frac{W}{I \times t}$$

Substitute the given values into the equation:
$$Z = \frac{0.1978}{0.2 \times 3000}$$
$$Z = \frac{0.1978}{600}$$
$$Z = 0.0003296 \text{ g/C}$$

**Final Answer:** 
The electrochemical equivalent of Copper (Cu) is **$3.296 \times 10^{-4} \text{ g/C}$**.

## ⚡ Exam Tips & Common Mistakes
*   **The Time Conversion Trap:** In First Law numericals ($W = ZIt$), the time ($t$) MUST be explicitly converted to **seconds**. Examiners frequently give the time in minutes or hours specifically to trap students who forget to convert before plugging the number into the formula.
*   **Faraday's Constant Relation:** Remember that $Z = \frac{E}{F}$, where $E$ is the equivalent weight and $F$ is the Faraday constant ($96485 \text{ C/mol}$). If you are given $E$ but need $Z$, use this relation.
