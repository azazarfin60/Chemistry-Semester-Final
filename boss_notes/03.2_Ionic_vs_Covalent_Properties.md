# Ionic vs Covalent Properties
> **Topic:** Chemical Bonding | **Priority:** 🔴 CRITICAL | **Exam Frequency:** ?/7 exams

## 📌 Quick Summary
> Ionic compounds form rigid 3D crystal lattices, resulting in high melting points, brittleness, and electrical conductivity in molten/aqueous states. Covalent compounds form discrete neutral molecules with weak intermolecular forces, leading to lower melting points and poor conductivity. The solubility of ionic compounds depends entirely on Hydration Energy overcoming Lattice Energy.

## 📖 Core Theory

### 1. Fundamental Structural Differences
*   **Ionic Compounds:** Consist of an alternating array of positively and negatively charged ions. These ions are held together by strong, **non-directional** electrostatic forces, forming a giant, rigid 3D lattice structure.
*   **Covalent Compounds:** Consist of discrete, electrically neutral molecules. The atoms within the molecule are held tightly by shared electrons (**directional** bonds), but the separate molecules are held together externally by very weak intermolecular forces (like van der Waals forces).

### 2. The Thermodynamics of Dissolution
When an ionic compound is placed in a polar solvent (like water), two opposing energetic forces dictate whether it will dissolve:
1.  **Lattice Energy:** The massive energy that holds the ions tightly together in the solid crystal. It is the energy barrier that must be *overcome* (energy absorbed) to break the solid apart into isolated gaseous ions.
2.  **Hydration Energy:** The energy *released* when these isolated ions are surrounded and stabilized by polar water molecules (forming ion-dipole interactions).

**The Solubility Rule:**
*   **Soluble:** If **Hydration Energy > Lattice Energy**, the energy released by hydration is enough to shatter the crystal lattice. The net process is energetically favorable, and the compound dissolves (e.g., $NaCl$ in water).
*   **Insoluble:** If **Hydration Energy < Lattice Energy**, the solvation energy cannot compensate for the massive energy required to break the strong ionic bonds. The solid remains intact (e.g., $BaSO_4$ in water).

| :--- | :--- | :--- |

| **Directional Nature** | Non-directional (electrostatic force is uniform in all directions). | Highly directional (shared electron pairs are localized, creating specific molecular geometries). |

> **Source:** answers/2024.md

**Answer:**

| Property | Ionic Compounds | Covalent Compounds |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **State at Room Temp.** | Mostly hard, crystalline solids due to strong electrostatic forces. | Exist as gases, liquids, or relatively soft solids. |
| **Melting & Boiling Points**| Very high, since a large amount of thermal energy is needed to break the strong ionic lattice. | Generally low, as only weak intermolecular forces (van der Waals) need to be overcome. |
| **Solubility** | Soluble in polar solvents (like water) due to ion-dipole interactions, but insoluble in non-polar solvents (like benzene). | Soluble in non-polar organic solvents (like benzene, ether) but generally insoluble in polar solvents like water. |

| **Directional Nature** | Ionic bonds are non-directional (electrostatic force is uniform in all directions). | Covalent bonds are rigid and directional, leading to specific molecular geometries (e.g., tetrahedral, linear). |

### 🎯 2019 Q.2(b) Explain the dissolution of ionic compounds based on the relative magnitudes of lattice energy and hydration energy. (04)
> **Source:** answers/2019.md

**Answer:**

When an ionic compound is placed in a polar solvent like water, two opposing energetic forces govern whether it will dissolve:

1.  **Lattice Energy:** This is the energy that holds the ions tightly together in the solid crystal lattice. It is the energy barrier that must be overcome (absorbed) to break the solid apart into isolated gaseous ions.
2.  **Hydration Energy:** When the isolated ions are surrounded by polar water molecules (ion-dipole interactions), they become hydrated. This solvation process releases energy, known as Hydration Energy.

**Mechanism of Dissolution:**
*   **If Hydration Energy > Lattice Energy:** The energy released by the hydration of the separated ions is greater than the energy required to break the ionic bonds in the crystal lattice. The net process is energetically favorable (exothermic or entropically driven), and the **ionic compound dissolves**. (e.g., NaCl in water).
*   **If Hydration Energy < Lattice Energy:** The energy released by hydrating the ions is not sufficient to compensate for the massive energy required to break apart the strong crystal lattice. The forces holding the solid together "win," and the **ionic compound remains insoluble**. (e.g., $BaSO_4$ in water).

## ⚡ Exam Tips & Common Mistakes
*   **State the Reason:** In distinction tables, never just state the property (e.g., "High MP"). Always provide the structural reason in parentheses ("due to strong electrostatic lattice"). Examiners specifically look for the "Why".
*   **The Conductivity Catch:** A classic trick question asks why solid NaCl doesn't conduct electricity. You must explicitly state that ionic compounds do *not* conduct in the solid state because the ions are fixed in the lattice; they only conduct when melted or dissolved, allowing the ions to become mobile.
*   **Solubility Mechanism:** For the 4-mark solubility question (like 2019 Q2b), you must explicitly define both Lattice Energy and Hydration Energy and state the clear mathematical inequality ($\text{Hydration} > \text{Lattice}$) for dissolution to occur.
