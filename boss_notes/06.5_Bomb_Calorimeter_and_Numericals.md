# Bomb Calorimeter and Numericals
> **Topic:** Thermochemistry | **Priority:** 🟠 HIGH | **Exam Frequency:** 3/7 exams

## 📌 Quick Summary
> A Bomb Calorimeter is the standard laboratory apparatus used to accurately measure the heat of combustion at constant volume ($\Delta U$). This section details its operation procedure, the mathematical derivation to convert its readings to standard enthalpy, and consolidates heavily tested thermochemistry numericals.

## 📖 Core Theory

### The Bomb Calorimeter
A Bomb Calorimeter is a heavily insulated, sealed device used specifically to measure the heat of combustion of a solid or liquid substance (like fuels or foods) at a constant volume.

**Operation Procedure (Tested in 2023):**
1.  **Sample Preparation:** A known mass ($m$) of the combustible substance is weighed accurately and placed in a small platinum crucible.
2.  **Assembling the "Bomb":** The crucible is placed inside a strong, heavy-walled steel vessel called the "bomb." A fine platinum ignition wire is connected to the crucible, touching the sample.
3.  **Pressurization:** The bomb is sealed air-tight and filled with excess pure Oxygen gas at a high pressure (usually 20-25 atmospheres) to guarantee complete combustion.
4.  **Water Bath:** The sealed bomb is completely immersed in a larger, heavily insulated outer vessel containing a precisely known mass of water ($W_w$). The outer vessel is equipped with a mechanical stirrer and a highly sensitive Beckman thermometer (capable of reading up to $0.01^\circ C$).
5.  **Initial Reading:** The stirrer is turned on to ensure uniform temperature. The initial temperature ($t_1$) of the water is carefully noted.
6.  **Ignition:** An electric current is passed through the platinum wire, which glows and ignites the sample. The sample burns completely in the pressurized oxygen.
7.  **Final Reading:** The intense heat evolved from the combustion is transferred through the steel walls of the bomb to the surrounding water bath. The water temperature rises. The maximum temperature reached by the water ($t_2$) is recorded.

*(Note: In the exam, accompany this description with a neat, labeled sketch showing the inner bomb, sample cup, ignition wires, surrounding water bath, thermometer, stirrer, and insulated outer jacket).*

## 🧮 Key Derivations & Equations

### Mathematical Equation of Enthalpy of Combustion (Tested in 2023)
Because the combustion inside the calorimeter occurs in a closed, rigid steel vessel, the volume is constant ($\Delta V = 0$). Therefore, the heat measured is the heat at constant volume ($q_v$), which is exactly equal to the change in internal energy ($\Delta U$).

**Step 1: Calculate Internal Energy Change ($\Delta U$)**
Let $W_w$ = mass of water in the calorimeter
Let $w_c$ = water equivalent of the calorimeter setup (bomb, stirrer, thermometer)
Let $\Delta T = (t_2 - t_1)$ = Rise in temperature
Let $s$ = specific heat of water (usually $4.184 \text{ J/g}^\circ\text{C}$ or $1 \text{ cal/g}^\circ\text{C}$)

The total heat absorbed by the calorimeter and the water is:
$$q_v = (W_w + w_c) \times s \times \Delta T$$
This heat corresponds to the burning of $m$ grams of the substance. For exactly 1 mole (Molar mass $M$) of the substance, the internal energy change ($\Delta U$) is:
$$\Delta U = - \frac{(W_w + w_c) \times s \times \Delta T}{m} \times M$$
*(The negative sign is added because combustion is strictly an exothermic process).*

**Step 2: Calculate Enthalpy of Combustion ($\Delta H_c$)**
The thermodynamic relationship between enthalpy change ($\Delta H$) and internal energy change ($\Delta U$) is:
$$\Delta H_c = \Delta U + P\Delta V$$
For reactions involving ideal gases at constant temperature and pressure, we substitute $P\Delta V = \Delta n_g RT$:
> **$$\Delta H_c = \Delta U + \Delta n_g RT$$**

Where:
*   $\Delta n_g = (\text{Total moles of gaseous products}) - (\text{Total moles of gaseous reactants})$
*   $R$ = Universal gas constant ($8.314 \text{ J/(mol K)}$ or $2 \text{ cal/(mol K)}$)
*   $T$ = Absolute initial temperature in Kelvin.

## 🔢 Solved Numericals

### 🎯 2024 Q.4(c) Hess's Law Calculation (03 marks)
**Question:** The heat of combustion of $CH_4$ is $212.0 \text{ kcal}$. If the heat of formation of $CO_2$ and $H_2O$ is $94.3 \text{ kcal}$ and $68.5 \text{ kcal}$ respectively. Calculate the heat of formation of $CH_4$.

**Solution:**
**1. Given Data:**
*   $\Delta H_c(CH_4) = -212.0 \text{ kcal/mol}$ *(combustion is exothermic, sign must be negative)*
*   $\Delta H_f(CO_2) = -94.3 \text{ kcal/mol}$ *(formation is exothermic)*
*   $\Delta H_f(H_2O) = -68.5 \text{ kcal/mol}$ *(formation is exothermic)*

**2. Chemical Equation:**
$$CH_4(g) + 2O_2(g) \rightarrow CO_2(g) + 2H_2O(l)$$

**3. Application of Hess's Law:**
$$\Delta H_{rxn} = \sum \Delta H_f(\text{Products}) - \sum \Delta H_f(\text{Reactants})$$
For combustion, $\Delta H_{rxn} = \Delta H_c(CH_4)$. Also, $\Delta H_f(O_2) = 0$.
$$\Delta H_c(CH_4) = [\Delta H_f(CO_2) + 2 \times \Delta H_f(H_2O)] - [\Delta H_f(CH_4) + 2 \times 0]$$
$$-212.0 = [-94.3 + 2(-68.5)] - \Delta H_f(CH_4)$$
$$-212.0 = [-94.3 - 137.0] - \Delta H_f(CH_4)$$
$$-212.0 = -231.3 - \Delta H_f(CH_4)$$
$$\Delta H_f(CH_4) = -231.3 + 212.0$$
$$\Delta H_f(CH_4) = \mathbf{-19.3 \text{ kcal/mol}}$$

### 🎯 2017 Q.2(c) Heat of Reaction from Combustion Data (03 marks)
**Question:** Calculate the enthalpy of $C_2H_4(g) + H_2(g) \rightarrow C_2H_6(g)$ reaction at $25^\circ C$. The heat of combustion of ethylene, hydrogen and ethane are $-1410 \text{ kJ}$, $-286.2 \text{ kJ}$ and $-1560.6 \text{ kJ}$ respectively at $25^\circ C$.

**Solution:**
**1. Target Reaction:**
$$C_2H_4(g) + H_2(g) \rightarrow C_2H_6(g) \quad \Delta H_{\text{reaction}} = ?$$

**2. Application of Hess's Law:**
When using Heats of Combustion data instead of Formation data, the formula reverses:
$$\Delta H_{\text{reaction}} = \sum \Delta H_c (\text{Reactants}) - \sum \Delta H_c (\text{Products})$$
$$\Delta H_{\text{reaction}} = [\Delta H_c(C_2H_4) + \Delta H_c(H_2)] - [\Delta H_c(C_2H_6)]$$
$$\Delta H_{\text{reaction}} = [(-1410) + (-286.2)] - [-1560.6]$$
$$\Delta H_{\text{reaction}} = [-1696.2] + 1560.6$$
$$\Delta H_{\text{reaction}} = \mathbf{-135.6 \text{ kJ/mol}}$$

### 🎯 2020 Q.5(c) Constant Volume to Constant Pressure Conversion (04 marks)
**Question:** Heat of combustion of ethylene at $17^\circ C$ and at constant volume is $-332.19 \text{ KCals}$. Calculate the heat of combustion at constant volume considering water to be in the liquid state. [$R = 2 \text{ Cal/}^\circ\text{C-mole}$]

*(Note: The question has a typo. It asks for heat at constant volume but provides it. It intends to ask for the heat of combustion at **constant pressure** ($\Delta H$)).*

**Solution:**
**1. Given Data:**
*   Heat at constant volume ($\Delta E$) = $-332.19 \text{ kcal}$
*   Temperature ($T$) = $17 + 273 = 290 \text{ K}$
*   $R = 2 \text{ cal K}^{-1} \text{mol}^{-1} = 2 \times 10^{-3} \text{ kcal K}^{-1} \text{mol}^{-1}$

**2. Calculate $\Delta n_g$:**
$$C_2H_4(g) + 3O_2(g) \rightarrow 2CO_2(g) + 2H_2O(l)$$
*   Moles of gaseous products = $2$ *(Ignore liquid water)*
*   Moles of gaseous reactants = $1 + 3 = 4$
$$\Delta n_g = 2 - 4 = -2$$

**3. Apply Thermodynamic Relation:**
$$\Delta H = \Delta E + \Delta n_g RT$$
$$\Delta H = -332.19 + [(-2) \times (2 \times 10^{-3}) \times 290]$$
$$\Delta H = -332.19 + [-4 \times 10^{-3} \times 290]$$
$$\Delta H = -332.19 + [-1.160]$$
$$\Delta H = \mathbf{-333.35 \text{ kcal}}$$


## ⚡ Exam Tips & Common Mistakes
*   **The 2020 Typo:** The 2020 question asks to "calculate the heat of combustion at constant volume" while providing the heat at constant volume. Recognize this classic typo! It wants you to calculate heat at constant *pressure* ($\Delta H$).
*   **$\Delta n_g$ Calculation:** Remember to strictly ignore liquid water when calculating $\Delta n_g$ for combustion reactions (like the $C_2H_4$ or $CH_4$ combustion). It is $n_{\text{gas(products)}} - n_{\text{gas(reactants)}}$.
*   **Sign Conventions:** If a question states "heat of combustion is 212 kcal", you MUST add the negative sign yourself ($\Delta H_c = -212 \text{ kcal/mol}$) before plugging it into Hess's law formulas. Combustion is always exothermic.
