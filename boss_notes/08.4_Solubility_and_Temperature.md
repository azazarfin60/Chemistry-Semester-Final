## 🏆 Golden Questions (Past Exam Archive)
### 🎯 2024 Q.8(b) Discuss the temperature effect on the solubility of gas in liquid. (03)
> **Source:** answers/2024.md

**Answer:**

**Effect of Temperature on Gas Solubility:**
The solubility of a gas in a liquid generally **decreases with an increase in temperature**.

**Discussion/Reasoning:**
1.  **Exothermic Nature:** The dissolution of a gas in a liquid is an exothermic process. According to Le Chatelier's principle, for an exothermic equilibrium process, increasing the temperature shifts the equilibrium backward. Therefore, the dissolved gas escapes from the solution, lowering its solubility.
    $$\text{Gas (solute)} + \text{Liquid (solvent)} \rightleftharpoons \text{Solution} + \text{Heat}$$
2.  **Kinetic Energy:** Non-polar gases have very weak intermolecular attractive forces with polar liquids like water. As temperature increases, the kinetic energy of the dissolved gas molecules increases. This added thermal energy allows the gas molecules to easily overcome the weak solute-solvent interactions and escape into the vapor phase above the liquid. 
3.  *(Example: This is why soda goes "flat" quickly on a hot day, and why aquatic life survives better in colder waters where dissolved oxygen concentration is higher).*

### 🎯 2020 Q.1(c) What happens when a tiny crystal of the solute is added to super saturated solution. (03)
> **Source:** answers/2020.md

**Answer:**

When a tiny crystal of the solute (called a **"seed crystal"**) is added to a supersaturated solution, it provides a nucleation site or template for the excess dissolved solute to deposit upon. 
Because a supersaturated solution is in a highly unstable, metastable state (containing more dissolved solute than it normally could at that temperature), the introduction of the seed crystal disrupts this fragile state. This causes an immediate and rapid precipitation (crystallization) of all the excess solute out of the solution. The crystallization continues until the concentration of the remaining solution drops to exactly the saturation point, restoring a stable equilibrium.

### 🎯 2017 Q7(c) Define exothermic reaction. Discuss the effect of temperature on solubility of gas in liquid. (04)
> **Source:** answers/2017.md

**Answer:**
**Exothermic Reaction:** A chemical reaction or physical process that is accompanied by the release or evolution of heat energy into the surroundings. The enthalpy change ($\Delta H$) is negative.

**Effect of Temperature on Solubility of a Gas:**
The dissolution of a gas in a liquid solvent is almost universally an **exothermic process**. It is akin to condensation, where the gaseous molecules lose their high kinetic energy and settle into the liquid phase, releasing heat.
$$\text{Gas (solute)} + \text{Liquid (solvent)} \rightleftharpoons \text{Solution} + \text{Heat}$$
According to **Le Chatelier's Principle**, if a dynamic equilibrium is subjected to a stress (like an increase in temperature), the system will shift to counteract that stress. Since dissolving is exothermic, supplying external heat acts as a stress on the product side. To absorb this excess heat, the equilibrium violently shifts in the backward, endothermic direction.
Therefore, the dissolved gas molecules are driven out of the solution back into the vapor phase. **Conclusion: The solubility of any gas in a liquid always decreases as the temperature increases.** (This is why boiling water forcefully expels all dissolved oxygen).

### 🎯 ClassTests Q.4 Discuss briefly the effect of temperature on solubility of solid in liquid. (4)
> **Source:** answers/ClassTests.md

**Answer:**
The effect of temperature on the solubility of a solid solute in a liquid solvent depends entirely on the enthalpy of solution ($\Delta H_{\text{sol}}$), governed by Le Chatelier's Principle:
1.  **Endothermic Dissolution ($\Delta H > 0$):** If the solid absorbs heat when dissolving (e.g., $KNO_3$, $NH_4Cl$, $KCl$), raising the temperature shifts the equilibrium forward to absorb the added heat. Thus, **solubility increases with an increase in temperature**.
2.  **Exothermic Dissolution ($\Delta H < 0$):** If the solid releases heat when dissolving (e.g., $Li_2SO_4$, $NaOH$, $Ca(OH)_2$), raising the temperature shifts the equilibrium backward to get rid of the excess heat. Thus, **solubility decreases with an increase in temperature**.
