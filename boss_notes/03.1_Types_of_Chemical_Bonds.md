# Types of Chemical Bonds
> **Topic:** Chemical Bonding | **Priority:** 🔴 CRITICAL | **Exam Frequency:** 5/7 exams

## 📌 Quick Summary
> A chemical bond is the attractive force holding atoms together to achieve a stable, low-energy noble gas configuration. Bonds are broadly classified into Strong Bonds (Ionic, Covalent, Coordinate, Metallic) and Weak Intermolecular Bonds (Hydrogen, van der Waals).

## 📖 Core Theory

### 1. What is a Chemical Bond?
*   **Definition:** The attractive physical force that holds various constituents (atoms, ions, or molecules) together in different chemical species.
*   **The "Why":** Under normal conditions, atoms (except noble gases) have incomplete valence electron shells, making them highly energetic and unstable. By completely transferring or mutually sharing electrons, atoms attain a highly stable, noble-gas-like electronic configuration (following the octet or duplet rule), significantly lowering their overall potential energy.

### 2. Classification of Chemical Bonds
**A. Strong (Primary) Bonds:**
1.  **Ionic (Electrovalent) Bond:** Formed by the complete transfer of one or more valence electrons from an electropositive atom (metal) to an electronegative atom (non-metal).
2.  **Covalent Bond:** Formed by the mutual sharing of valence electrons between two non-metal atoms.
3.  **Coordinate (Dative) Bond:** A specialized covalent bond where the shared pair of electrons is provided entirely by only one of the participating atoms.
4.  **Metallic Bond:** The electrostatic attraction between a lattice of positive metal ions (kernels) and a delocalized "sea" of mobile electrons.

**B. Weak (Secondary) Bonds:**
1.  **Hydrogen Bond:** A strong dipole-dipole attraction between a highly electronegative atom (F, O, N) and a hydrogen atom that is covalently bonded to another highly electronegative atom.
2.  **van der Waals Forces:** Weak intermolecular dispersion forces existing between all molecules.

### 3. Conditions for Bond Formation
**Conditions Favoring Ionic Bonds:**
1.  **Low Ionization Energy (Metal):** The metal must easily lose electrons to form a cation.
2.  **High Electron Affinity (Non-metal):** The non-metal must readily accept electrons to form an anion, releasing energy.
3.  **High Lattice Energy:** The massive amount of energy released when gaseous ions pack into a rigid 3D crystal lattice must be high enough to compensate for the ionization energy.
4.  **Large Electronegativity Difference:** Generally $\Delta EN > 1.7$.

**Conditions Favoring Covalent Bonds:**
1.  **High Ionization Energy:** Neither atom is willing to completely give up an electron.
2.  **High Electron Affinity:** Both atoms are eager to gain electrons, so sharing is the only compromise.
3.  **Small Electronegativity Difference:** Generally $\Delta EN < 1.7$.

## 📊 Comparison Tables

### Ionic vs. Covalent Compounds
| Property | Electrovalent (Ionic) Compounds | Covalent Compounds | Why they differ (The Reasoning) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Physical State** | Hard, crystalline solids at room temperature. | Gases, liquids, or relatively soft solids. | Ionic compounds form giant 3D lattice structures held by strong electrostatic forces. Covalent compounds consist of discrete molecules held by weak van der Waals forces. |
| **Melting & Boiling Pts** | Very high. | Generally low. | Huge thermal energy is required to break the strong ionic lattice. Little energy is needed to separate weak intermolecular covalent forces. |
| **Solubility** | Soluble in polar solvents (water); insoluble in non-polar. | Soluble in non-polar organic solvents; insoluble in water. | Polar water molecules pull apart charged ions (ion-dipole). Neutral covalent molecules dissolve in neutral non-polar solvents ("like dissolves like"). |
| **Electrical Conductivity** | Non-conductors in solid state, but excellent in molten/aqueous state. | Generally poor conductors or insulators in all states. | In solid ionic lattices, ions are fixed. When melted/dissolved, ions become mobile. Covalent compounds lack free ions or electrons to carry current. |

## 🏆 Golden Questions (Past Exam Archive)
### 🎯 Explain the conditions of formation of ionic bond and covalent bond.
> **Appeared:** 2024 Q.1(a) — (06 marks)

**Answer:**

**Conditions for the Formation of an Ionic Bond:**
An ionic bond is formed by the complete transfer of one or more electrons from an electropositive atom (metal) to an electronegative atom (non-metal). The primary conditions favoring its formation are:
1. **Low Ionization Energy of the Metal:** The metal atom must have a low ionization energy so that it can easily lose electrons to form a cation. (e.g., Alkali metals like Na, K).
2. **High Electron Affinity of the Non-metal:** The non-metal atom should possess a high electron affinity so that it can readily accept electrons to form an anion and release a large amount of energy. (e.g., Halogens like Cl, F).
3. **High Lattice Energy:** The electrostatic attraction between the newly formed cations and anions must be strong enough to release a high amount of lattice energy when they pack into a crystal lattice. The higher the lattice energy, the greater the stability of the ionic compound.
4. **Large Electronegativity Difference:** The difference in electronegativity between the two bonding atoms must be large (typically > 1.7 on the Pauling scale) to ensure a complete transfer of electrons rather than sharing.

**Conditions for the Formation of a Covalent Bond:**
A covalent bond is formed by the mutual sharing of valence electrons between two atoms to achieve a stable noble gas configuration. The conditions favoring its formation are:
1. **High Ionization Energy:** Both interacting atoms should have high ionization energies, meaning neither is willing to completely give up an electron to form a cation.
2. **High Electron Affinity:** Both atoms should have high or comparable electron affinities, making them eager to gain electrons. Thus, sharing is the only viable compromise.
3. **Small or Zero Electronegativity Difference:** The electronegativity difference between the two atoms must be very small or zero (typically < 1.7). If it is zero (e.g., $H_2$, $Cl_2$), a purely non-polar covalent bond forms. If it is small, a polar covalent bond forms (e.g., $HCl$, $H_2O$).
4. **Equal Requirement of Electrons:** Both atoms should require 1, 2, or 3 electrons to complete their respective octets (or duplets).

### 🎯 Describe the factors favoring the formation of ionic bond.
> **Appeared:** 2019 Q.2(a) — (04 marks)

**Answer:**

An ionic bond is formed by the complete transfer of electrons from a metal to a non-metal. The formation of a stable ionic bond is favored by three main thermodynamic factors:

1.  **Low Ionization Energy of the Metal:** For a metal atom to easily lose its valence electron(s) and form a stable positive ion (cation), it must have a low ionization energy. (e.g., Alkali metals naturally form ionic bonds because they have very low ionization energies).
2.  **High Electron Affinity of the Non-Metal:** The non-metal atom must readily accept the transferred electron(s) to form a stable negative ion (anion). A high, negative electron affinity means a large amount of energy is released when the non-metal gains an electron, stabilizing the system. (e.g., Halogens).
3.  **High Lattice Energy:** The most crucial factor is the lattice energy, which is the massive amount of energy released when the gaseous cations and anions come together and pack into a rigid, 3D electrostatic crystal lattice. If the lattice energy is highly negative (highly exothermic), it fully compensates for the energy required to ionize the metal, ensuring the overall process results in a highly stable ionic compound.

### 🎯 What is Electrovalent bond? How and why do electrovalent compounds differ from covalent compounds in their properties?
> **Appeared:** 2021 Q.8(a) — (05 marks)

**Answer:**

**Electrovalent (Ionic) Bond:**
An electrovalent bond is a type of chemical bond formed by the complete transfer of one or more valence electrons from an electropositive atom (a metal) to an electronegative atom (a non-metal). This transfer creates oppositely charged ions (cations and anions) which are then held together by strong electrostatic forces of attraction.

**Differences in Properties (How and Why):**

| Property | Electrovalent (Ionic) Compounds | Covalent Compounds | Why they differ (Reasoning) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Physical State** | Hard, crystalline solids at room temperature. | Gases, liquids, or relatively soft solids. | Ionic compounds form giant 3D lattice structures held by very strong non-directional electrostatic forces. Covalent compounds consist of discrete, neutral molecules held by weak intermolecular van der Waals forces. |
| **Melting & Boiling Pts** | Very high. | Generally low. | A huge amount of thermal energy is required to break the strong ionic lattice. Little energy is needed to overcome the weak intermolecular forces between covalent molecules. |
| **Solubility** | Soluble in polar solvents (like water); insoluble in non-polar solvents. | Soluble in non-polar organic solvents (like benzene); insoluble in water. | Polar water molecules surround and pull apart the charged ions via ion-dipole interactions. Neutral covalent molecules interact better with non-polar solvents (like dissolves like). |
| **Electrical Conductivity** | Non-conductors in solid state, but excellent conductors in molten/aqueous state. | Generally poor conductors or insulators in all states. | In solid ionic lattices, ions are fixed. When melted or dissolved, ions become mobile and carry charge. Covalent compounds lack free ions or electrons to carry current. |

### 🎯 What is Chemical bond? Define and classify them.
> **Appeared:** 2020 Q.8(a) · 2017 Q1(a) — (04 marks)

**Answer:**

**Chemical Bond:**
A chemical bond is defined as the attractive physical force that holds various constituents (atoms, ions, or molecules) together in different chemical species to form a stable, independent entity.

**Why Bonds Form:**
Under normal conditions, atoms of most elements (except noble gases) do not exist as independent entities in nature because they have incomplete valence electron shells, making them highly energetic and unstable. To achieve maximum stability (lowest potential energy), atoms interact with each other. A chemical bond is formed when atoms either completely transfer valence electrons (Ionic bond), mutually share valence electrons (Covalent bond), or pool their electrons in a sea (Metallic bond). This redistribution of electrons allows each participating atom to attain a stable, noble-gas-like electronic configuration (usually an octet), thereby lowering the overall energy of the resulting molecule compared to the isolated atoms.

**Classification of Chemical Bonds:**
Chemical bonds are broadly classified into strong (primary) bonds and weak (secondary) bonds:

**Strong (Primary) Bonds:**
1.  **Ionic (Electrovalent) Bond:** Formed by the complete transfer of one or more valence electrons from a metal to a non-metal.
2.  **Covalent Bond:** Formed by the mutual sharing of valence electrons between two non-metal atoms.
3.  **Coordinate (Dative) Bond:** A specialized covalent bond where the shared pair of electrons is provided entirely by only one of the bonding atoms.
4.  **Metallic Bond:** The electrostatic attraction between a lattice of positive metal ions (kernels) and a delocalized "sea" of mobile electrons.

**Weak (Secondary) Bonds:**
1.  **Hydrogen Bond:** A dipole-dipole attraction between a highly electronegative atom (F, O, N) and a hydrogen atom covalently bonded to another highly electronegative atom.
2.  **van der Waals Forces:** Weak intermolecular dispersion forces existing between all molecules (e.g., London dispersion forces).

## ⚡ Exam Tips & Common Mistakes
*   **The Lattice Energy Factor:** When asked for the factors favoring ionic bonds (like in 2019 Q.2a), DO NOT forget to explicitly mention "High Lattice Energy." Many students only mention Ionization Energy and Electron Affinity, but Lattice Energy is the thermodynamic driving force for ionic stability.
*   **The "Why" in Differences:** When asked to distinguish ionic and covalent compounds (like in 2021 Q.8a), don't just state the differences (e.g., "Ionic has a high melting point"). Always provide the *reasoning* (e.g., "Because of the strong 3D electrostatic lattice").
