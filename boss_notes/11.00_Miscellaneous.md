## 📖 Core Theory

### 1. Crystal Structure
Unlike amorphous solids (like glass or plastic) which have a random internal arrangement, crystalline solids are highly organized. 
The **crystal structure** of a solid refers to the highly ordered, symmetrical, and continuously repeating 3D geometric arrangement of its constituent particles (atoms, ions, or molecules) in space. 
It is defined by two fundamental concepts:
*   **Lattice:** The mathematical, regular grid of points extending infinitely in space.
*   **Basis:** The actual physical atoms/ions attached to each lattice point.
*   **Unit Cell:** The smallest repeating geometric unit that displays the full macroscopic symmetry of the crystal. By stacking unit cells infinitely in three dimensions, the entire crystal lattice is formed.

### 2. Isomorphism
**Isomorphism** (from Greek *iso* = same, *morphe* = form) is a crystallographic phenomenon where two or more chemically distinct, entirely different compounds possess the exact identical crystal structure. They crystallize in the exact same geometric form.
*   Such substances are termed **isomorphous**.
*   **Conditions:** This phenomenon usually occurs only when the differing compounds have the exact same chemical formula type (e.g., identical valency ratios) and their constituent interchangeable atoms/ions have very similar ionic radii/sizes.
*   **Classic Example:** Zinc sulfate heptahydrate ($ZnSO_4 \cdot 7H_2O$, known as White vitriol) and Magnesium sulfate heptahydrate ($MgSO_4 \cdot 7H_2O$, known as Epsom salt) are perfectly isomorphous.

## ⚡ Exam Tips & Common Mistakes
*   **The Isomorphism Definition:** Whenever defining isomorphism, explicitly mention that the compounds must be *chemically distinct* (different chemicals) but *structurally identical* (same 3D lattice). 
*   **Examples are Mandatory:** A short note on isomorphism will likely lose marks if you do not provide an example. Memorize the White vitriol ($ZnSO_4 \cdot 7H_2O$) and Epsom salt ($MgSO_4 \cdot 7H_2O$) pair, as they are the classic textbook examples.
