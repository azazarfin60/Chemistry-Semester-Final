### 🎯 2018 (a) Crystal structure and isomorphism
> **Source:** answers/2018.md

**Crystal Structure:** The crystal structure of a solid refers to the highly ordered, symmetrical, and continuously repeating 3D geometric arrangement of its constituent particles (atoms, ions, or molecules) in space. It is defined by two concepts: a "lattice" (the regular grid of points in space) and a "basis" (the atoms attached to each lattice point). The smallest repeating unit that displays the full symmetry of the crystal is called the **unit cell**.
**Isomorphism:** Isomorphism is the crystallographic phenomenon where two or more chemically distinct, entirely different compounds possess the exact same identical crystal structure (they crystallize in the identical geometric form). Such substances are termed "isomorphous." This usually occurs when the compounds have the same chemical formula type and their constituent atoms/ions have very similar sizes.
*   *Example:* Zinc sulfate heptahydrate ($ZnSO_4 \cdot 7H_2O$, White vitriol) and Magnesium sulfate heptahydrate ($MgSO_4 \cdot 7H_2O$, Epsom salt) are perfectly isomorphous.
