# MO Diagrams Specific Molecules
> **Topic:** Chemical Bonding | **Priority:** 🔴 CRITICAL | **Exam Frequency:** ?/7 exams

## 📌 Quick Summary
> Molecular Orbital Theory (MOT) is practically applied by constructing energy level diagrams to determine a molecule's electronic configuration, bond order, and magnetic properties. The presence of unpaired electrons in these diagrams definitively proves whether a species is paramagnetic (like $O_2$) or diamagnetic (like $N_2$), and whether a hypothetical molecule can exist at all (like $He_2$).

## 📖 Core Theory

### 1. Constructing MO Diagrams
To construct an MO diagram and derive molecular properties:
1.  Sum the total valence electrons of the interacting atoms.
2.  Fill the Molecular Orbitals starting from the lowest energy ($\sigma_{1s}$) to the highest, strictly obeying the Aufbau principle, Pauli's exclusion principle, and Hund's rule.

### 2. The Crucial $s-p$ Mixing Rule
The energy sequence of molecular orbitals changes depending on the total number of electrons due to $s-p$ orbital mixing:
*   **For molecules with $\le 14$ electrons** (e.g., $N_2, CO, C_2$): The energy gap between $2s$ and $2p$ atomic orbitals is small, causing significant mixing. This pushes the $\sigma_{2p_z}$ orbital **higher** in energy than the degenerate $\pi_{2p_x}$ and $\pi_{2p_y}$ orbitals.
    *Sequence:* $\sigma_{1s} < \sigma^*_{1s} < \sigma_{2s} < \sigma^*_{2s} < \mathbf{\pi_{2p_x} = \pi_{2p_y}} < \mathbf{\sigma_{2p_z}} < \pi^*_{2p_x} = \pi^*_{2p_y} < \sigma^*_{2p_z}$
*   **For molecules with $> 14$ electrons** (e.g., $O_2, F_2, NO$): The energy gap is large, so $s-p$ mixing is negligible. The $\sigma_{2p_z}$ orbital remains **lower** in energy than the $\pi_{2p_x}$ and $\pi_{2p_y}$ orbitals.
    *Sequence:* $\sigma_{1s} < \sigma^*_{1s} < \sigma_{2s} < \sigma^*_{2s} < \mathbf{\sigma_{2p_z}} < \mathbf{\pi_{2p_x} = \pi_{2p_y}} < \pi^*_{2p_x} = \pi^*_{2p_y} < \sigma^*_{2p_z}$

### 3. Calculating Bond Order and Magnetism
*   **Bond Order (B.O.):** Calculated as $\frac{N_b - N_a}{2}$. It indicates bond strength (single, double, triple). A B.O. of exactly zero mathematically proves the molecule cannot exist (e.g., $He_2$).
*   **Diamagnetic:** All electrons in the MO diagram are paired (repelled by magnetic fields).
*   **Paramagnetic:** Contains one or more unpaired electrons in the MO diagram (attracted by magnetic fields).

## 🏆 Golden Questions (Past Exam Archive)
### 🎯 2024 Q.3(b) Explain the energy level diagram of $O_2$ and $N_2$ molecule using MOT. (04)
> **Source:** answers/2024.md

**Answer:**

**Energy Level Diagram of Nitrogen ($N_2$) Molecule:**
*   **Electronic Configuration of N (Z=7):** $1s^2 2s^2 2p^3$
*   Total valence electrons for $N_2$ = $5 + 5 = 10$.
*   In $N_2$, the $2s-2p$ mixing is significant. This pushes the energy of the $\sigma_{2p_z}$ orbital higher than the $\pi_{2p_x}$ and $\pi_{2p_y}$ orbitals.
*   **MO Configuration:**
    $KK \ (\sigma_{2s})^2 \ (\sigma^*_{2s})^2 \ (\pi_{2p_x})^2 = (\pi_{2p_y})^2 \ (\sigma_{2p_z})^2$
*   **Bond Order:** $\frac{N_b - N_a}{2} = \frac{8 - 2}{2} = 3$ (Triple bond).
*   **Magnetic Property:** Diamagnetic, as there are no unpaired electrons.

**Energy Level Diagram of Oxygen ($O_2$) Molecule:**
*   **Electronic Configuration of O (Z=8):** $1s^2 2s^2 2p^4$
*   Total valence electrons for $O_2$ = $6 + 6 = 12$.
*   In $O_2$, the energy gap between 2s and 2p is large, so $2s-2p$ mixing is negligible. Therefore, the $\sigma_{2p_z}$ orbital is lower in energy than the $\pi_{2p_x}$ and $\pi_{2p_y}$ orbitals.
*   **MO Configuration:**
    $KK \ (\sigma_{2s})^2 \ (\sigma^*_{2s})^2 \ (\sigma_{2p_z})^2 \ (\pi_{2p_x})^2 = (\pi_{2p_y})^2 \ (\pi^*_{2p_x})^1 = (\pi^*_{2p_y})^1$
*   **Bond Order:** $\frac{N_b - N_a}{2} = \frac{8 - 4}{2} = 2$ (Double bond).
*   **Magnetic Property:** Paramagnetic, because it contains two unpaired electrons in the degenerate $\pi^*$ antibonding orbitals (one in $\pi^*_{2p_x}$ and one in $\pi^*_{2p_y}$), governed by Hund's rule.

*(Note: In an exam, sketching the actual diagram with vertical energy axes, showing atomic orbitals on the sides and molecular orbitals in the middle is highly recommended).*

### 🎯 2023 Q.3(b) Draw the molecular orbital of $He_2$ gas and proof that it doesn't exist. (03)
> **Source:** answers/2023.md

**Answer:**

**Proof of Non-existence of $He_2$ via MOT:**
1.  **Electronic Configuration of He atom (Z=2):** $1s^2$
2.  **Total valence electrons in hypothetial $He_2$ molecule:** $2 + 2 = 4$ electrons.
3.  **Molecular Orbital Diagram:**
    The two $1s$ atomic orbitals of the two Helium atoms will combine to form two molecular orbitals: a bonding $\sigma_{1s}$ and an antibonding $\sigma^*_{1s}$.

    *   **Energy Level Diagram sketch:**
        He(Atom A) -------- $He_2$ Molecule -------- He(Atom B)
        $1s$ [ ↑↓ ] ----------------------------------- $1s$ [ ↑↓ ]
                     \           $\sigma^*_{1s}$ [ ↑↓ ]         /
                      \                             /
                       \--- $\sigma_{1s}$ [ ↑↓ ] ---/

4.  **MO Electronic Configuration:** $(\sigma_{1s})^2 \ (\sigma^*_{1s})^2$
5.  **Bond Order Calculation:**
    $$\text{Bond Order (B.O.)} = \frac{N_b - N_a}{2}$$
    Where $N_b$ = electrons in bonding MOs = 2
    Where $N_a$ = electrons in antibonding MOs = 2
    $$\text{B.O.} = \frac{2 - 2}{2} = 0$$

**Conclusion:** A bond order of zero implies that there is no net attractive force between the two Helium atoms. The destabilizing effect of the 2 electrons in the antibonding orbital completely cancels out the stabilizing effect of the 2 electrons in the bonding orbital. Therefore, the $He_2$ molecule cannot exist.

### 🎯 2023 Q.3(c) Draw the molecular orbitals of CO and NO and also find the bond order and magnetic properties of each molecules. (04)
> **Source:** answers/2023.md

**Answer:**

**(I) Nitric Oxide (NO) Molecule:**
*   Total valence electrons: N (7) + O (8) = 15 electrons.
*   **MO Electronic Configuration:**
    Because NO is a heteronuclear molecule with more than 14 electrons, its energy filling order is similar to $O_2$.
    $\sigma_{1s}^2 \ \sigma_{1s}^{*2} \ \sigma_{2s}^2 \ \sigma_{2s}^{*2} \ \sigma_{2p_z}^2 \ \pi_{2p_x}^2 \equiv \pi_{2p_y}^2 \ \pi_{2p_x}^{*1}$
*   **Bond Order (B.O.):** 
    $$B.O. = \frac{N_b - N_a}{2} = \frac{10 - 5}{2} = \frac{5}{2} = 2.5$$
    *(Implies a bond between double and triple).*
*   **Magnetic Property:** It contains one unpaired electron in the antibonding $\pi_{2p_x}^*$ orbital. Therefore, NO is **Paramagnetic**.

**(II) Carbon Monoxide (CO) Molecule:**
*   Total valence electrons: C (6) + O (8) = 14 electrons.
*   **MO Electronic Configuration:**
    For molecules with 14 or fewer electrons (like $N_2$ or CO), significant $s-p$ mixing occurs, pushing the $\sigma_{2p_z}$ orbital higher in energy than the $\pi_{2p_x}$ and $\pi_{2p_y}$ orbitals.
    $\sigma_{1s}^2 \ \sigma_{1s}^{*2} \ \sigma_{2s}^2 \ \sigma_{2s}^{*2} \ \pi_{2p_x}^2 \equiv \pi_{2p_y}^2 \ \sigma_{2p_z}^2$
*   **Bond Order (B.O.):**
    $$B.O. = \frac{N_b - N_a}{2} = \frac{10 - 4}{2} = \frac{6}{2} = 3$$
    *(Implies a strong triple bond, $C \equiv O$).*
*   **Magnetic Property:** All electrons are paired in the molecular orbitals. Therefore, CO is **Diamagnetic**.

### 🎯 ClassTests 2. "$N_2$ is diamagnetic but $O_2$ is paramagnetic" justify this statement with M.O.T.
> **Source:** answers/ClassTests.md

**Answer:**
According to Molecular Orbital Theory, a molecule is diamagnetic if all its electrons are paired, and paramagnetic if it contains unpaired electrons.
1.  **For $N_2$ (14 electrons):** The MO configuration is $KK (\sigma_{2s})^2 (\sigma^*_{2s})^2 (\pi_{2p_x})^2 = (\pi_{2p_y})^2 (\sigma_{2p_z})^2$. Every single molecular orbital is filled with exactly two electrons (spin-paired). Because there are absolutely **zero unpaired electrons**, the $N_2$ molecule is completely **diamagnetic**.
2.  **For $O_2$ (16 electrons):** The MO configuration is $KK (\sigma_{2s})^2 (\sigma^*_{2s})^2 (\sigma_{2p_z})^2 (\pi_{2p_x})^2 = (\pi_{2p_y})^2 (\pi^*_{2p_x})^1 = (\pi^*_{2p_y})^1$. According to Hund's rule, the two highest energy electrons occupy the degenerate $\pi^*$ antibonding orbitals singly before pairing. Because it contains **two unpaired electrons**, the $O_2$ molecule is **paramagnetic**.

## ⚡ Exam Tips & Common Mistakes
*   **The $O_2$ vs $N_2$ Magnetism Question:** This is the most famous, heavily tested MOT question (appearing in finals and class tests). You must explicitly write the configuration ending in $(\pi^*_{2p_x})^1 = (\pi^*_{2p_y})^1$ to definitively prove $O_2$ has two unpaired electrons.
*   **The $s-p$ Mixing Flip:** A massive common mistake is using the exact same orbital sequence for $N_2$ and $O_2$. Do not forget to flip the order of $\sigma_{2p_z}$ and the $\pi_{2p}$ orbitals between $N_2$ ($\le 14e^-$) and $O_2$ ($> 14e^-$). Examiners actively check this sequence.
*   **$He_2$ Non-existence:** When asked to prove $He_2$ doesn't exist, you must explicitly show the calculation $B.O. = \frac{2-2}{2} = 0$. Writing "electrons cancel out" is not enough for full marks.
