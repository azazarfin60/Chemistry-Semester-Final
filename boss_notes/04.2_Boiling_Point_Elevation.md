# Boiling Point Elevation
> **Topic:** Colligative Properties | **Priority:** 🟠 HIGH | **Exam Frequency:** ?/7 exams

## 📌 Quick Summary
> A liquid boils when its vapor pressure equals atmospheric pressure. Because adding a non-volatile solute lowers the vapor pressure, the solution must be heated to a higher temperature to boil. This increase in temperature ($\Delta T_b$) is directly proportional to the molality of the solution.

## 📖 Core Theory

### 1. Mechanism of Boiling Point Elevation
*   **Definition of Boiling Point:** The temperature at which the vapor pressure of a liquid becomes exactly equal to the external atmospheric pressure ($1 \text{ atm}$).
*   **The Cause:** According to Raoult's Law, adding a non-volatile solute lowers the vapor pressure of the solvent at any given temperature. Consequently, at the pure solvent's normal boiling point, the solution's vapor pressure is now *below* atmospheric pressure. To force the vapor pressure back up to $1 \text{ atm}$, additional thermal energy (a higher temperature) is required.
*   **Elevation ($\Delta T_b$):** The difference between the boiling point of the solution ($T_s$) and the boiling point of the pure solvent ($T_o$).
    $$\Delta T_b = T_s - T_o$$

### 2. Molal Elevation Constant ($K_b$)
Experimental evidence shows that for dilute solutions, the elevation in boiling point is directly proportional to the **molal concentration (molality, $m$)** of the solute.
$$\Delta T_b \propto m$$
$$\Delta T_b = K_b \cdot m$$
*   **Ebullioscopic Constant ($K_b$):** Also known as the molal elevation constant. It is defined as the elevation in boiling point when exactly 1 mole of non-volatile solute is dissolved in 1000 g (1 kg) of the solvent. Its units are $K \cdot kg \cdot mol^{-1}$.

## 🧮 Key Derivations & Equations

### Determination of Molecular Weight ($M_2$)
The colligative property formula can be expanded to determine the unknown molecular weight of a solute.
1.  Start with the core relation:
    $$\Delta T_b = K_b \cdot m$$
2.  Expand molality ($m$), which is moles of solute ($n$) per kg of solvent ($w_1$ in grams):
    $$m = \frac{n}{w_1 / 1000} = \frac{n \times 1000}{w_1}$$
3.  Substitute $n = \frac{w_2}{M_2}$ (where $w_2$ = mass of solute, $M_2$ = MW of solute):
    $$m = \frac{w_2 \times 1000}{M_2 \times w_1}$$
4.  Substitute this back into the $\Delta T_b$ equation:
    $$\Delta T_b = \frac{1000 \cdot K_b \cdot w_2}{M_2 \cdot w_1}$$
5.  Rearrange to isolate $M_2$:
    > $$M_2 = \frac{1000 \cdot K_b \cdot w_2}{\Delta T_b \cdot w_1}$$

## ⚡ Exam Tips & Common Mistakes
*   **Molality vs Molarity:** A very common mistake is using Molarity ($M$) instead of Molality ($m$) in the $\Delta T_b$ formula. Colligative properties rely on molality because it is mass-based and therefore entirely independent of temperature fluctuations, whereas molarity (volume-based) changes with temperature.
*   **The Link:** Always be prepared to explain *why* the BP elevates. The examiner is looking for you to explicitly state: "Because vapor pressure is lowered by the solute, a higher temperature is needed to reach atmospheric pressure."
