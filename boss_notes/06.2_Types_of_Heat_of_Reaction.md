# Types of Heat of Reaction
> **Topic:** Thermochemistry | **Priority:** 🟠 HIGH | **Exam Frequency:** ?/7 exams

## 📌 Quick Summary
> Thermochemistry measures enthalpy changes during chemical and physical processes. Different types of reactions have specific enthalpy definitions, with Heat of Combustion (always exothermic) and Heat of Neutralization (constant for strong acid/base) being the most commonly tested definitions in exams.

## 📖 Core Theory

### 1. Introduction to Thermochemistry
Thermochemistry is the branch of physical chemistry dealing with the quantitative study of thermal energy (heat) changes that accompany chemical reactions and physical transformations (like melting or boiling).
**Importance of Thermochemistry:**
1.  **Bond Energies:** It provides the experimental data necessary to calculate precise bond dissociation energies.
2.  **Theoretical Heats of Formation:** It allows the theoretical calculation of the heat of formation for complex compounds that cannot be prepared directly in the lab (by applying Hess's Law).
3.  **Calorific Values:** It is heavily used in engineering to calculate the calorific values of fuels, determining their efficiency.
4.  **Reaction Feasibility:** Along with entropy, thermochemical data ($\Delta H$) is a key component in calculating Gibbs Free Energy ($\Delta G$), which dictates the thermodynamic feasibility of industrial reactions.

### 2. Types of Heat of Reaction

**A) General Heat of Reaction ($\Delta H$)**
The total amount of heat evolved or absorbed when molar quantities of reactants, exactly as represented by a balanced chemical equation, react completely. It indicates the difference in total enthalpy between products and reactants.
*   *Example:* $C(s) + O_2(g) \rightarrow CO_2(g); \quad \Delta H = -393.5 \text{ kJ/mol}$

**B) Heat of Combustion ($\Delta H_c$) & Calorific Value**
*   **Heat of Combustion:** The total amount of heat evolved when exactly **1 mole** of a substance is completely oxidized or burnt in the presence of excess oxygen at standard conditions. It is always an exothermic process (negative $\Delta H$).
    *   *Example:* $CH_4(g) + 2O_2(g) \rightarrow CO_2(g) + 2H_2O(l); \quad \Delta H_c = -890.3 \text{ kJ/mol}$
    *   *Applications:* Calculating heats of formation, determining calorific values, and elucidating structural stability between isomers (less stable isomers release more heat).
*   **Calorific Value:** The heat energy released when a **unit mass** (1 gram or 1 kg) of a fuel is completely burnt. Expressed in $kJ/g$ or $kcal/kg$, it directly compares fuel efficiency.

**C) Heat of Neutralization**
The amount of heat evolved when exactly **one gram-equivalent** of an acid is completely neutralized by one gram-equivalent of a base in a dilute aqueous solution.
*   *The Strong Acid/Base Constant:* Because strong acids and strong bases ionize completely in water, their neutralization is simply the net ionic reaction between $H^+$ and $OH^-$ to form water. Therefore, the heat of neutralization for *any* strong acid/base pair is a constant value of approximately **$-13.7 \text{ kcal/mol}$** (or $-57.1 \text{ kJ/mol}$).

**D) Heat of Sublimation ($\Delta H_{sub}$)**
The total amount of heat absorbed required to convert exactly **1 mole** of a solid directly into its gaseous state at a constant temperature, without passing through the liquid phase.
*   *Example:* $I_2(s) \rightarrow I_2(g); \quad \Delta H_{sub} = +62.4 \text{ kJ/mol}$

## ⚡ Exam Tips & Common Mistakes
*   **The "One Mole" Keyword:** The definitions for Heat of Combustion and Heat of Sublimation absolutely require the phrase *"exactly 1 mole."* Missing this nullifies the definition and will cost you the mark.
*   **The "One Gram-Equivalent" Keyword:** Conversely, the definition of Heat of Neutralization must use *"one gram-equivalent"* of acid/base instead of "one mole". 
*   **The Neutralization Constant:** You are frequently asked why the heat of neutralization of strong acids/bases is constant. Be ready to write the net ionic equation: $H^+ + OH^- \rightarrow H_2O$ and explicitly state the $-13.7 \text{ kcal/mol}$ value.
