# Solution Definitions and Types
> **Topic:** Solutions | **Priority:** 🟡 MEDIUM | **Exam Frequency:** ?/7 exams

## 📌 Quick Summary
> A solution is a homogeneous mixture of a solute dissolved in a solvent. This foundational section covers the 9 classifications of solutions based on physical states, defines critical concentration terms (ppm, Normality, Molality), and explains the vital distinction between temperature-dependent (volume-based) and temperature-independent (mass-based) concentration units.

## 📖 Core Theory

### 1. Definition of a Solution
A **solution** is defined as a perfectly homogeneous physical mixture of two or more chemically non-reacting substances, the composition of which can be varied within certain limits.
*   **Solvent:** The component present in the physically larger amount. It dictates the final physical state of the bulk solution.
*   **Solute:** The component that is dissolved into the solvent, typically present in a smaller amount.

### 2. Concentration Units and Temperature Dependence
Chemists use various units to express the concentration of a solute in a solution:
1.  **ppm (parts per million):** A unit used for extremely dilute solutions (like trace pollutants in water). It represents the mass of the solute in milligrams ($mg$) present in exactly one kilogram ($kg$) of the solution. 
    $\text{ppm} = \frac{\text{Mass of solute}}{\text{Total mass of solution}} \times 10^6$
2.  **Molarity (M):** The number of moles of solute dissolved in exactly one liter ($1 \text{ dm}^3$) of the *solution*.
3.  **Normality (N):** The number of gram-equivalents of solute dissolved in exactly one liter ($1 \text{ dm}^3$) of the *solution*.
4.  **Molality (m):** The number of moles of solute dissolved in exactly one kilogram ($1000 \text{ g}$) of the *pure solvent*.

**The Temperature Rule (Highly Tested):**
*   **Molality is independent of temperature:** Molality is defined strictly by the *mass* of the solvent. Because the physical mass of a substance does not expand or contract when heated or cooled, its molality remains perfectly constant regardless of temperature changes.
*   **Molarity and Normality are dependent on temperature:** These are defined by the *volume* of the solution. The physical volume of a liquid expands upon heating and contracts upon cooling. Therefore, as temperature changes, the denominator (volume) changes, causing the concentration value to fluctuate.

## 📊 Comparison Tables

### 9 Classifications of Binary Solutions
Depending on the physical state (solid, liquid, or gas) of the solute and the solvent, there are 9 possible classifications. The physical state of the **solvent** always determines the physical state of the resulting solution.

| Type of Solution | Solute State | Solvent State | Common Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Gaseous Solutions** | Gas | **Gas** | Air (Mixture of $O_2$, $N_2$, etc.) |
| | Liquid | **Gas** | Water vapor in air (Humidity), Chloroform in $N_2$ gas |
| | Solid | **Gas** | Camphor vapors in $N_2$ gas, Iodine vapors in air |
| **2. Liquid Solutions** | Gas | **Liquid** | Aerated drinks ($CO_2$ dissolved in water), Oxygen in water |
| | Liquid | **Liquid** | Ethanol dissolved in water |
| | Solid | **Liquid** | Sugar or Salt dissolved in water |
| **3. Solid Solutions** | Gas | **Solid** | Solution of Hydrogen gas in Palladium metal |
| | Liquid | **Solid** | Amalgam of mercury with sodium (Na-Hg) |
| | Solid | **Solid** | Alloys like brass (Cu + Zn) or gold alloyed with copper/silver |

## 🔢 Solved Numericals

### 🎯 2024 Q.8(c) & 2021 Q.1(c): Molality of Toluene (02-03 marks)
**Question:** $5.0 \text{ g}$ of toluene is dissolved in $225 \text{ g}$ of benzene. What is the molality of toluene in the solution?

**Given Data:**
*   Mass of solute (Toluene, $C_7H_8$), $W_B = 5.0 \text{ g}$
*   Mass of solvent (Benzene), $W_A = 225 \text{ g} = \mathbf{0.225 \text{ kg}}$

**To Find:**
*   Molality ($m$) of toluene in the solution.

**Solution:**
**Step 1: Calculate the molar mass of the solute (Toluene)**
The chemical formula of Toluene is $C_6H_5CH_3$, which combines to $C_7H_8$.
Molar mass ($M_B$) = $(7 \times 12) + (8 \times 1) = 84 + 8 = 92 \text{ g/mol}$

**Step 2: Calculate the number of moles of solute ($n_B$)**
$$n_B = \frac{\text{Mass of Toluene}}{\text{Molar mass of Toluene}} = \frac{5.0 \text{ g}}{92 \text{ g/mol}} = \mathbf{0.05435 \text{ moles}}$$

**Step 3: Calculate the molality ($m$)**
Molality is defined as the number of moles of solute per kilogram of solvent.
$$m = \frac{\text{Moles of solute } (n_B)}{\text{Mass of solvent in kg } (W_A)}$$
$$m = \frac{0.05435 \text{ mol}}{0.225 \text{ kg}}$$
$$m = 0.2415 \text{ mol/kg}$$

**Final Answer:** 
The molality of toluene in the solution is **$0.2415 \text{ m}$** (or $\text{mol kg}^{-1}$).

### 🎯 ClassTests Q.5: Molality of Antifreeze (03 marks)
**Question:** Calculate the molality of a solution prepared by dissolving $10 \text{ g}$ of the antifreeze ethylene glycol ($C_2H_6O_2$) in $450 \text{ g}$ of water.

**Solution:**
1.  **Molar Mass of Solute:** Ethylene glycol ($C_2H_6O_2$) = $(2 \times 12) + (6 \times 1) + (2 \times 16) = 24 + 6 + 32 = 62 \text{ g/mol}$.
2.  **Moles of Solute:** $n = \frac{10 \text{ g}}{62 \text{ g/mol}} = 0.1613 \text{ moles}$.
3.  **Mass of Solvent in kg:** $450 \text{ g} = 0.45 \text{ kg}$.
4.  **Calculate Molality ($m$):** 
    $$m = \frac{0.1613 \text{ moles}}{0.45 \text{ kg}} = \mathbf{0.358 \text{ mol/kg}}$$

## ⚡ Exam Tips & Common Mistakes
*   **Definition Traps:** When defining Molarity, it is per liter of **solution**. When defining Molality, it is per kilogram of **solvent**. Swapping these two words is an automatic zero.
*   **The Gram-to-Kilogram Trap:** In molality numericals, the mass of the solvent is almost always given in grams ($g$) to trick you. Always immediately divide by $1000$ to convert it to kilograms ($kg$) before plugging it into the formula.
