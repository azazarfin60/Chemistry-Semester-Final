# Orbit vs Orbital
> **Topic:** Atomic Structure | **Priority:** 🟠 HIGH | **Exam Frequency:** 2/7 exams

## 📌 Quick Summary
> The distinction between an "Orbit" and an "Orbital" represents the fundamental shift from classical physics (Bohr's model) to modern quantum mechanics. An orbit is a fixed 2D circular path, while an orbital is a 3D mathematical probability cloud defined by the Schrödinger wave equation.

## 📖 Core Theory

### The Conceptual Shift in Atomic Structure
*   **Orbit (Bohr's Classical Concept):** This is a deterministic view where an electron is treated strictly as a particle revolving in a perfectly known, fixed circular path around the nucleus (like planets around the sun). Because it assumes we can know the exact path and velocity simultaneously, the concept of an orbit fundamentally violates Heisenberg's Uncertainty Principle.
*   **Orbital (Quantum Mechanical Concept):** This is a probabilistic view arising from the wave-particle duality of electrons (de Broglie) and the Schrödinger wave equation. Quantum mechanics acknowledges that an electron's exact trajectory cannot be known. Instead, an orbital defines a 3D region of space around the nucleus where the probability of finding the electron is mathematically maximum (usually $>90\%$).

## 📊 Comparison Tables

### 🎯 Orbit vs. Orbital (Directly Tested in 2023)
| Feature | Orbit (Bohr Model) | Orbital (Quantum Model) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Definition** | A well-defined circular path around the nucleus in which an electron revolves. | A 3D region in space around the nucleus where the probability of finding an electron is maximum. |
| **Shape** | Orbits are strictly circular or elliptical (2D planar motion). | Orbitals have complex 3D shapes (e.g., s-orbital is spherical, p-orbital is a dumbbell). |
| **Directional Nature**| Orbits are non-directional as they simply represent a planar path. | Except for the spherical s-orbital, all other orbitals (p, d, f) have directional characteristics (e.g., along x, y, z axes). |
| **Heisenberg's Principle** | Violates Heisenberg's Uncertainty Principle because it implies exact position and momentum are known simultaneously. | Conforms strictly to Heisenberg's Uncertainty Principle, representing only a "probability cloud" rather than a fixed trajectory. |
| **Maximum Electron Capacity** | Can accommodate a maximum of **$2n^2$** electrons (where $n$ is the shell number). | Can accommodate a maximum of only **2** electrons (with opposite spins, per Pauli's principle). |

## ⚡ Exam Tips & Common Mistakes
*   **The Easiest Point:** When asked to distinguish the two, always include the "Maximum Electron Capacity" point. It's the easiest mark to secure and mathematically objective: Orbits hold $2n^2$, Orbitals hold exactly 2.
*   **Heisenberg Keyword:** Examiners specifically scan your answer for the name "Heisenberg's Uncertainty Principle." You must state that orbits *violate* it, while orbitals *conform* to it.
