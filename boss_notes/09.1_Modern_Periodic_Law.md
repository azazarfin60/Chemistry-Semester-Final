### 🎯 2024 Q.2(a) Define modern periodic law. Discuss the periodic properties of long form of periodic table. (06)
> **Source:** answers/2024.md

**Answer:**

**Modern Periodic Law:**
The Modern Periodic Law states that *"The physical and chemical properties of the elements are periodic functions of their atomic numbers."* This implies that when elements are arranged in increasing order of their atomic number, elements with similar properties recur at regular intervals or periods.

**Periodic Properties of the Long Form of the Periodic Table:**
Periodic properties are those characteristics of elements that are directly related to their electronic configuration and show a regular gradation as we move across a period (left to right) or down a group (top to bottom).
1. **Atomic Radius:**
   * *Across a period:* Decreases from left to right. The effective nuclear charge increases, pulling the valence shell closer to the nucleus.
   * *Down a group:* Increases. New electron shells are added, increasing the distance between the nucleus and the outermost electrons, despite the increase in nuclear charge.
2. **Ionization Potential (Energy):**
   * *Across a period:* Increases. Due to the decreasing atomic radius and increasing effective nuclear charge, more energy is required to remove an electron.
   * *Down a group:* Decreases. The atomic size increases, and the shielding effect of inner electrons makes the outermost electrons less tightly held.
3. **Electron Affinity:**
   * *Across a period:* Increases (becomes more negative). Non-metals on the right more readily accept electrons to complete their octet.
   * *Down a group:* Decreases. The incoming electron goes into a higher energy level further from the nucleus.
4. **Electronegativity:**
   * *Across a period:* Increases. Elements on the right have a stronger tendency to attract shared pairs of electrons.
   * *Down a group:* Decreases. The bonding electrons are further from the attractive pull of the nucleus.
5. **Metallic/Non-metallic Character:**
   * *Across a period:* Metallic character decreases and non-metallic character increases.
   * *Down a group:* Metallic character increases.

### 🎯 2020 Q.7(a) Modern periodic table is more clear than Mendeleev's periodic table. Explain why? (04)
> **Source:** answers/2020.md

**Answer:**

The Modern Periodic Table is conceptually clearer and scientifically more rigorous than Mendeleev's Periodic Table for the following fundamental reasons:

1.  **Basis of Classification:** Mendeleev based his table on **Atomic Mass**. This led to anomalies where he had to break his own rule to group elements with similar properties (e.g., placing Argon (mass 39.9) before Potassium (mass 39.1)). The Modern table is based on **Atomic Number** (number of protons), which is a much more fundamental and precise property of an element. This naturally resolves all the mass anomalies.
2.  **Position of Isotopes:** Because Mendeleev's table was based on atomic mass, isotopes of the same element (having different masses) technically required different positions in the table, which confused the chemical grouping. In the Modern table, since all isotopes of an element have the identical atomic number, they naturally and correctly occupy the exact same single position.
3.  **Correlation with Electronic Configuration:** The Modern periodic table perfectly aligns with quantum mechanics and the Aufbau principle. The arrangement into s, p, d, and f blocks directly correlates with the outermost electronic configuration of the atoms. This provides a clear, logical explanation for *why* periodicity occurs (elements in the same group have the same valence electron configuration), which Mendeleev's table could not explain mathematically.

### 🎯 2017 Q3(a) What is periodic table? Why the long form of periodic Table is more clear than that of Mendeleef's periodic Table? (06)
> **Source:** answers/2017.md

**Answer:**

**Periodic Table:**
The periodic table is a tabular arrangement of all known chemical elements, organized systematically in order of their increasing atomic numbers, electron configurations, and recurring chemical properties. It groups elements with similar characteristics into vertical columns (Groups) and horizontal rows (Periods), serving as a unified reference framework for chemistry.

**Why the Long Form (Modern) is clearer than Mendeleev's:**
The Long Form (Modern) Periodic Table is conceptually clearer and scientifically more rigorous for three fundamental reasons:

1.  **Basis of Classification (Atomic Number vs. Mass):** Mendeleev based his table on **Atomic Mass**. This led to unexplainable anomalies where he had to break his own rule to group elements correctly (e.g., placing Argon, mass 39.9, before Potassium, mass 39.1). The Modern table is based on **Atomic Number** (number of protons). This is a much more fundamental property that naturally resolves all of Mendeleev's mass anomalies without exception.
2.  **Position of Isotopes:** Because Mendeleev's table relied on atomic mass, isotopes of the exact same element (which have different masses) technically required entirely different positions in the table, destroying chemical groupings. In the Modern table, since all isotopes of an element have the identical atomic number, they naturally and correctly occupy the exact same single slot.
3.  **Correlation with Electronic Configuration:** The Modern table's structure perfectly aligns with quantum mechanics and the Aufbau principle. The arrangement into s, p, d, and f blocks directly and visibly correlates with the outermost electronic configuration of the atoms. This provides a clear, logical, and visual explanation for *why* periodicity occurs (elements in the same group have the exact same valence electron configuration), something Mendeleev's table could not mathematically explain.

### 🎯 2024 Q.2(b) Classify the elements as per their electronic configuration. Give examples. (04)
> **Source:** answers/2024.md

**Answer:**

Based on their electronic configuration, elements in the long form of the periodic table are classified into four main blocks depending on the subshell into which the last differentiating electron enters:

1. **s-block elements:**
   * **Definition:** Elements in which the last electron enters the s-orbital of their outermost shell. They have the general valence electronic configuration $ns^{1-2}$.
   * **Characteristics:** They are highly reactive, soft, electropositive metals.
   * **Examples:** Alkali metals (e.g., Sodium, $Na: [Ne]3s^1$) and Alkaline earth metals (e.g., Calcium, $Ca: [Ar]4s^2$).

2. **p-block elements:**
   * **Definition:** Elements in which the last electron enters the p-orbital of the outermost shell. General valence configuration is $ns^2 np^{1-6}$.
   * **Characteristics:** Includes metals, non-metals, and metalloids. Together with s-block elements, they are called representative elements.
   * **Examples:** Carbon ($C: [He]2s^2 2p^2$), Oxygen ($O: [He]2s^2 2p^4$), Chlorine ($Cl: [Ne]3s^2 3p^5$).

3. **d-block elements (Transition Elements):**
   * **Definition:** Elements in which the last electron enters the d-orbital of the penultimate (n-1) shell. General configuration is $(n-1)d^{1-10} ns^{1-2}$.
   * **Characteristics:** Hard metals, show variable oxidation states, form colored ions, and act as good catalysts.
   * **Examples:** Iron ($Fe: [Ar]3d^6 4s^2$), Copper ($Cu: [Ar]3d^{10} 4s^1$).

4. **f-block elements (Inner Transition Elements):**
   * **Definition:** Elements in which the last electron enters the f-orbital of the anti-penultimate (n-2) shell. General configuration is $(n-2)f^{1-14} (n-1)d^{0-1} ns^2$.
   * **Characteristics:** Heavy metals, many are radioactive (Actinides). They are divided into Lanthanides and Actinides.
   * **Examples:** Uranium ($U: [Rn]5f^3 6d^1 7s^2$), Cerium ($Ce: [Xe]4f^1 5d^1 6s^2$).

### 🎯 2020 Q.7(b) Classify the elements according to their electronic configuration. Give examples of each. (04)
> **Source:** answers/2020.md

**Answer:**

Based on the subshell (orbital) into which the last differentiating electron enters, elements are classified into four blocks:

1.  **s-block Elements:** The last electron enters the outermost s-orbital. They include Group 1 (Alkali metals) and Group 2 (Alkaline earth metals). General configuration: $ns^{1-2}$.
    *   *Example:* Sodium (Na, Z=11): $1s^2 2s^2 2p^6 3s^1$
2.  **p-block Elements:** The last electron enters the outermost p-orbital. They include Groups 13 to 18 (comprising metals, non-metals, and metalloids). General configuration: $ns^2 np^{1-6}$.
    *   *Example:* Chlorine (Cl, Z=17): $1s^2 2s^2 2p^6 3s^2 3p^5$
3.  **d-block Elements (Transition Elements):** The last electron enters the penultimate (n-1) d-orbital. They lie between the s and p blocks. General configuration: $(n-1)d^{1-10} ns^{1-2}$.
    *   *Example:* Iron (Fe, Z=26): $[Ar] 4s^2 3d^6$
4.  **f-block Elements (Inner Transition Elements):** The last electron enters the anti-penultimate (n-2) f-orbital. They are placed at the bottom of the table (Lanthanides and Actinides). General configuration: $(n-2)f^{1-14} (n-1)d^{0-1} ns^2$.
    *   *Example:* Uranium (U, Z=92): $[Rn] 7s^2 5f^3 6d^1$

### 🎯 2017 Q3(b) Explain the types of elements on the basis of electronic configuration. (04)
> **Source:** answers/2017.md

**Answer:**
Based on the specific subshell (orbital) into which the last differentiating valence electron enters, the elements in the periodic table are classified into four distinct structural blocks:

1.  **s-block Elements:** The last differentiating electron enters the outermost s-orbital. Because the s-orbital can hold a maximum of 2 electrons, this block comprises Group 1 (Alkali metals) and Group 2 (Alkaline earth metals). General valence configuration: $ns^{1-2}$. These are highly reactive, electropositive metals.
2.  **p-block Elements:** The last electron enters the outermost p-orbital. It includes Groups 13 through 18. General valence configuration: $ns^2 np^{1-6}$. This is the only block containing a mix of metals, metalloids, and all non-metals (including the noble gases).
3.  **d-block Elements (Transition Elements):** The last electron enters the inner, penultimate (n-1) d-orbital. They lie strategically between the s and p blocks (Groups 3-12). General configuration: $(n-1)d^{1-10} ns^{1-2}$. These are hard metals that typically exhibit variable oxidation states, form colored ions, and act as excellent catalysts.
4.  **f-block Elements (Inner Transition Elements):** The last electron enters the deeply buried anti-penultimate (n-2) f-orbital. They are placed separately at the bottom of the table to maintain its structural shape. They consist of two series: Lanthanides (4f series) and Actinides (5f series, mostly radioactive). General configuration: $(n-2)f^{1-14} (n-1)d^{0-1} ns^2$.
