# Equilibrium Numericals
> **Topic:** Chemical Equilibrium | **Priority:** 🔴 CRITICAL | **Exam Frequency:** 2/7 exams

## 📌 Quick Summary
> Equilibrium numericals heavily rely on the thermodynamic relation $\Delta G^\circ = -RT \ln K_p$. A classic, repeated exam question requires calculating the equilibrium constant from a given Standard Gibbs Free Energy, demanding careful attention to units (converting kcal to cal) and selecting the correct gas constant ($R$).

## 📖 Core Theory
The primary mathematical relationship tested in these numericals is the Reaction Isotherm Equation:
**$$\Delta G^\circ = -2.303 RT \log K_p$$**
Where:
*   $\Delta G^\circ$ = Standard Gibbs Free Energy Change
*   $R$ = Universal Gas Constant
*   $T$ = Absolute temperature in Kelvin ($^\circ C + 273.15$)
*   $K_p$ = Equilibrium constant (dimensionless in thermodynamic terms)

## 🔢 Solved Numericals

**Problem 1 (Exam 2019, 2018):** Calculate the value of $K_p$ for a reaction which has a $\Delta G^\circ$ value of $-20 \text{ kcal/mole}$ at $25^\circ C$.

**1. Given Data:**
*   $\Delta G^\circ = -20 \text{ kcal/mole} = \mathbf{-20,000 \text{ cal/mole}}$ *(CRITICAL: Convert to calories!)*
*   $T = 25^\circ C = 25 + 273.15 = \mathbf{298.15 \text{ K}}$
*   $R = \mathbf{1.987 \text{ cal K}^{-1}\text{mol}^{-1}}$ *(Because $\Delta G^\circ$ is in calories)*

**2. Find:**
*   Equilibrium Constant ($K_p$)

**3. Solution:**
Using the standard free energy equation:
$$\Delta G^\circ = -2.303 RT \log K_p$$
Substitute the given values into the equation:
$$-20,000 = -2.303 \times 1.987 \times 298.15 \times \log K_p$$
$$-20,000 = -1364.3 \times \log K_p$$
Rearranging to solve for $\log K_p$:
$$\log K_p = \frac{-20,000}{-1364.3}$$
$$\log K_p = 14.659$$
Now, calculate $K_p$ by taking the antilog:
$$K_p = 10^{14.659}$$
$$K_p = 10^{0.659} \times 10^{14}$$
$$K_p = 4.56 \times 10^{14}$$

**4. Final Answer:**
The value of the equilibrium constant $K_p$ is **$4.56 \times 10^{14}$**.

## 🏆 Golden Questions (Past Exam Archive)
### 🎯 Calculate the value of $K_p$ for a reaction which has $\Delta G^\circ$ value -20 kcal/mole at $25^\circ C$.
> **Appeared:** 2019 Q.4(b) · 2018 Q6(c) — (04 marks)

**Answer:**

**Given Data:**
*   Standard Gibbs Free Energy Change ($\Delta G^\circ$) = $-20 \text{ kcal/mole} = -20,000 \text{ cal/mole}$
*   Temperature ($T$) = $25^\circ C = 25 + 273.15 = 298.15 \text{ K}$
*   Universal gas constant ($R$) = $1.987 \text{ cal K}^{-1}\text{mole}^{-1}$

**Solution:**
The fundamental thermodynamic relationship between standard free energy change and the equilibrium constant ($K_p$) is:
$$\Delta G^\circ = -RT \ln K_p$$
or, using base 10 logarithms:
$$\Delta G^\circ = -2.303 RT \log K_p$$

Substitute the given values into the equation:
$$-20,000 = -2.303 \times 1.987 \times 298.15 \times \log K_p$$
$$-20,000 = -1364.3 \times \log K_p$$

Rearranging to solve for $\log K_p$:
$$\log K_p = \frac{-20,000}{-1364.3}$$
$$\log K_p = 14.659$$

Now, calculate $K_p$ by taking the antilog:
$$K_p = 10^{14.659}$$
$$K_p = 10^{0.659} \times 10^{14}$$
$$K_p = 4.56 \times 10^{14}$$

**Final Answer:** The value of the equilibrium constant $K_p$ is **$4.56 \times 10^{14}$**.

## ⚡ Exam Tips & Common Mistakes
*   **The Unit Trap (CRITICAL):** If $\Delta G^\circ$ is given in `kcal`, you MUST convert it to `cal` (multiply by 1000) before using $R = 1.987 \text{ cal/(K mol)}$. If you forget this, your final power of 10 will be completely wrong (you'd get $10^{14}$ instead of $10^{0.014}$ if you messed up).
*   **The R-Value Trap:** Ensure you use the correct value of $R$ matching your energy units.
    *   If $\Delta G^\circ$ is in Joules ($J$), use $R = 8.314 \text{ J/(K mol)}$.
    *   If $\Delta G^\circ$ is in calories ($cal$), use $R = 1.987 \text{ cal/(K mol)}$.
*   **Antilog Execution:** Practice calculating $10^{14.659} = 10^{0.659} \times 10^{14}$ on your specific scientific calculator.
