# Intermolecular Forces
> **Topic:** Chemical Bonding | **Priority:** 🔴 CRITICAL | **Exam Frequency:** ?/7 exams

## 📌 Quick Summary
> Intermolecular forces are weak attractive forces acting between discrete neutral molecules. The primary category is van der Waals forces, which are subdivided into dipole-dipole, dipole-induced dipole, and London dispersion forces, depending on the permanent or temporary polarity of the interacting molecules.

## 📖 Core Theory

### 1. Van der Waals Forces
Van der Waals forces are short-range, weak intermolecular attractive forces. They are the fundamental reason non-polar gases can be liquefied under high pressure/low temperature, and they dictate the boiling and melting points of covalent compounds. 
They are strictly categorized into three types based on the electrostatic interactions of the electron clouds:

**A. Dipole-Dipole Interactions (Keesom Forces)**
*   **Mechanism:** Occurs between two molecules that have *permanent* dipole moments (polar molecules). The partially positive end ($\delta^+$) of one permanent dipole is electrostatically attracted to the partially negative end ($\delta^-$) of a neighboring permanent dipole.
*   **Examples:** $HCl \dots HCl$, $H_2S \dots H_2S$.
*   **Strength:** The strongest of the three van der Waals forces because the dipoles are permanent.

**B. Dipole-Induced Dipole Interactions (Debye Forces)**
*   **Mechanism:** Occurs when a permanent polar molecule approaches a completely non-polar molecule. The strong electric field of the polar molecule distorts the symmetrical electron cloud of the non-polar molecule, "inducing" a temporary dipole within it. The two now-polar molecules attract.
*   **Examples:** $H_2O$ (polar) dissolving a small amount of $O_2$ (non-polar).

**C. Instantaneous Dipole-Induced Dipole (London Dispersion Forces)**
*   **Mechanism:** The *only* force acting between completely non-polar molecules or isolated noble gas atoms. Due to the constant, random motion of electrons, a molecule's electron cloud can momentarily shift to one side. This creates an "instantaneous, temporary dipole." This fleeting dipole instantly distorts the electron cloud of an adjacent molecule, creating an "induced dipole," leading to mutual attraction.
*   **Examples:** $He \dots He$, $CH_4 \dots CH_4$, $Cl_2 \dots Cl_2$.
*   **Strength:** The weakest intermolecular force. However, its strength increases dramatically with the size (polarizability) of the electron cloud (which is why $I_2$ is a solid but $Cl_2$ is a gas).

## 🏆 Golden Questions (Past Exam Archive)
### 🎯 ClassTests 1. Explain different types of van-der-Waals attraction forces using proper figure.
> **Source:** answers/ClassTests.md

**Answer:**
Van der Waals forces are weak, short-range intermolecular attractive forces that exist between neutral molecules. They are classified into three main types based on the polarity of the interacting molecules:

1.  **Dipole-Dipole Interactions (Keesom Forces):**
    These occur between two permanently polar molecules (e.g., $HCl$). The partially positive end ($\delta^+$) of one permanent dipole is electrostatically attracted to the partially negative end ($\delta^-$) of a neighboring permanent dipole.
    *(Mental Figure: Two oval molecules, each with a $\delta^+$ on the left and $\delta^-$ on the right, aligning end-to-end so $\delta^-$ attracts $\delta^+$).*
2.  **Dipole-Induced Dipole Interactions (Debye Forces):**
    These occur when a permanent polar molecule approaches a completely non-polar molecule (e.g., $H_2O$ and $O_2$). The strong electric field of the permanent dipole distorts the symmetrical electron cloud of the non-polar molecule, "inducing" a temporary dipole within it. The two molecules then attract each other.
    *(Mental Figure: A permanent polar molecule distorting a perfectly spherical non-polar electron cloud into a temporary oval shape, causing attraction).*
3.  **Instantaneous Dipole-Induced Dipole Interactions (London Dispersion Forces):**
    These are the only forces that exist between completely non-polar molecules or noble gas atoms (e.g., $He, N_2, CH_4$). Due to the constant, random motion of electrons, the electron cloud can briefly shift to one side, creating an "instantaneous, temporary dipole." This fleeting dipole instantly distorts the electron cloud of an adjacent molecule, creating an "induced dipole," and they attract. These are the weakest forces but exist in all molecules.
    *(Mental Figure: A symmetrical atom suddenly having more electrons on one side, creating a temporary $\delta^-$ pole, which pushes away electrons on a neighboring atom, inducing a matching $\delta^+$ pole).*

## ⚡ Exam Tips & Common Mistakes
*   **Diagrams are Mandatory:** When asked to explain van der Waals forces (like in the Class Test), you **must** draw simple diagrams alongside your text. The prompt explicitly says "using proper figure." Draw simple ovals with $\delta^+$ and $\delta^-$ to represent the shifting electron clouds.
*   **Universal London Forces:** Remember that London dispersion forces exist between *all* molecules, whether they are polar or non-polar. However, in polar molecules, dipole-dipole forces are much stronger and dominate the physical properties, so London forces are usually only highlighted for non-polar molecules.
*   **Polarizability:** If asked why boiling points increase down the halogen group ($F_2 < Cl_2 < Br_2 < I_2$), the answer is purely due to London Dispersion forces increasing as the electron cloud becomes larger and more easily distorted (more polarizable).
