# Conductance and Kohlrausch
> **Topic:** Electrochemistry | **Priority:** 🟠 HIGH | **Exam Frequency:** ?/7 exams

## 📌 Quick Summary
> Conductance measures an electrolyte's ability to carry electrical current. This section covers the fundamental definitions of specific, molar, and equivalent conductance and analyzes the paradoxical effect of dilution on them. It also formally introduces Kohlrausch’s Law of Independent Migration of Ions and details its powerful application in deriving the ionic product of water ($K_w$).

## 📖 Core Theory

### 1. Types of Conductance
*   **Specific Conductance ($\kappa$):** The exact conductance offered by exactly $1 \text{ cm}^3$ of the electrolyte solution. It depends directly on the number of current-carrying ions per unit volume.
*   **Molar Conductance ($\Lambda_m$):** The total conductance of all the ions produced by dissolving exactly *one mole* of an electrolyte in a volume ($V$) of solution. Mathematically: $\Lambda_m = \kappa \times \frac{1000}{M}$, where $M$ is molarity.
*   **Equivalent Conductance ($\Lambda_{eq}$):** The total conductance of all the ions produced by dissolving exactly *one gram-equivalent weight* of an electrolyte in a volume ($V$) of solution. Mathematically: $\Lambda_{eq} = \kappa \times \frac{1000}{N}$, where $N$ is normality.

### 2. The Effect of Dilution on Conductance (Tested in 2017)
When water (solvent) is added to an electrolyte solution, the total volume increases. This creates two contrasting effects:

1.  **Specific Conductance ($\kappa$) Decreases:** 
    When diluted, the total number of ions remains relatively similar, but they are spread over a much larger volume. Consequently, the **number of current-carrying ions per unit volume ($1 \text{ cm}^3$) decreases drastically**. Therefore, specific conductance ($\kappa$) always drops upon dilution.
2.  **Equivalent/Molar Conductance ($\Lambda_{eq}, \Lambda_m$) Increases:** 
    Recall the formula: $\Lambda_{eq} = \kappa \times V$. Upon dilution, $\kappa$ drops, but the volume ($V$) containing that 1 gram-equivalent increases tremendously. The massive increase in volume overwhelmingly overcompensates for the small drop in $\kappa$.
    *   *Mechanism for Weak Electrolytes:* Dilution forces them to dissociate more (according to Ostwald's dilution law), significantly increasing the total number of mobile ions.
    *   *Mechanism for Strong Electrolytes:* They are already fully ionized, but dilution separates the crowded ions further apart. This greatly reduces inter-ionic electrostatic attractions (retardation forces), allowing the ions to migrate much faster.

### 3. Kohlrausch’s Law of Independent Migration of Ions
At **infinite dilution**, dissociation is 100% complete, and inter-ionic electrostatic forces drop to exactly zero. Kohlrausch's law states that at this point, each individual ion migrates completely independently and makes a definite, fixed contribution to the total equivalent conductance of the electrolyte, regardless of the nature of the other ion it happens to be associated with.
> **$$\Lambda^\circ_{\text{eq}} = \lambda^\circ_+ + \lambda^\circ_-$$**

## 🧮 Key Derivations & Equations

### Derivation of the Ionic Product of Water ($K_w$) using Kohlrausch's Law
Water is an extremely weak electrolyte that ionizes slightly:
$$H_2O \rightleftharpoons H^+ + OH^-$$

Let the degree of dissociation of water be $\alpha$. According to Arrhenius theory, the degree of dissociation is the ratio of molar conductance at a given concentration ($\Lambda_v$) to the molar conductance at infinite dilution ($\Lambda^\circ$):
$$\alpha = \frac{\Lambda_v}{\Lambda^\circ}$$

**Step 1: Find $\Lambda^\circ$ using Kohlrausch's Law**
The molar conductance of water at infinite dilution is the sum of its independent ionic mobilities:
$$\Lambda^\circ_{H_2O} = \lambda^\circ_{H^+} + \lambda^\circ_{OH^-}$$

**Step 2: Find $\Lambda_v$ using Specific Conductance**
If $C$ is the molar concentration of pure water ($1000 \text{ g/L} / 18 \text{ g/mol} = 55.55 \text{ mol/L}$), then its molar conductance is:
$$\Lambda_v = \kappa \times \frac{1000}{C}$$

**Step 3: Substitute into the $\alpha$ equation**
$$\alpha = \frac{\kappa \times \frac{1000}{C}}{\lambda^\circ_{H^+} + \lambda^\circ_{OH^-}} = \frac{1000 \cdot \kappa}{C (\lambda^\circ_{H^+} + \lambda^\circ_{OH^-})}$$

**Step 4: Calculate $K_w$**
The ionic product of water is defined as $K_w = [H^+][OH^-]$.
From the ionization equation, $[H^+] = C\alpha$ and $[OH^-] = C\alpha$.
$$K_w = (C\alpha) \times (C\alpha) = C^2 \alpha^2$$

Substitute the full expression for $\alpha$ into the $K_w$ equation:
$$K_w = C^2 \left[ \frac{1000 \cdot \kappa}{C (\lambda^\circ_{H^+} + \lambda^\circ_{OH^-})} \right]^2$$
$$K_w = C^2 \frac{(1000 \cdot \kappa)^2}{C^2 (\lambda^\circ_{H^+} + \lambda^\circ_{OH^-})^2}$$

The $C^2$ terms perfectly cancel out, yielding the final equation:
> **$$K_w = \left[ \frac{1000 \cdot \kappa}{\lambda^\circ_{H^+} + \lambda^\circ_{OH^-}} \right]^2$$**

*Conclusion: This derived equation elegantly allows the determination of the fundamental constant $K_w$ by simply measuring the specific conductance ($\kappa$) of ultra-pure water.*

## ⚡ Exam Tips & Common Mistakes
*   **The Dilution Paradox:** Examiners love asking why $\kappa$ decreases while $\Lambda_{eq}$ increases on dilution. To get full marks, you MUST explicitly state that the massive increase in **Volume ($V$)** overcompensates for the drop in specific conductance ($\kappa$), referencing the formula $\Lambda_{eq} = \kappa \times V$.
*   **$K_w$ Derivation Step:** In the $K_w$ derivation, do not forget to explicitly state the value of the molar concentration of pure water ($C = 55.55 \text{ mol/L}$). Leaving $C$ undefined loses marks.
