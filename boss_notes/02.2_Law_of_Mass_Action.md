# Law of Mass Action
> **Topic:** Chemical Equilibrium | **Priority:** 🔴 CRITICAL | **Exam Frequency:** ?/7 exams

## 📌 Quick Summary
> Formulated by Guldberg and Waage, the Law of Mass Action states that the rate of a chemical reaction is proportional to the product of the active masses of its reactants. Applying this law to the forward and backward rates of a reversible reaction at equilibrium allows us to mathematically derive the Equilibrium Constant ($K_c$).

## 📖 Core Theory

### 1. Statement of the Law
*   Formulated by Norwegian chemists Cato Guldberg and Peter Waage in 1864.
*   **The Law States:** *"At a given constant temperature, the rate of a chemical reaction at any instant is directly proportional to the product of the active masses of the reacting substances, with each concentration term raised to a power equal to its stoichiometric coefficient in the balanced chemical equation."*

### 2. What is "Active Mass"?
The term "active mass" represents the effective concentration of a substance participating in the reaction.
*   For dilute aqueous solutions, the active mass is simply the **molar concentration** (denoted by square brackets $[C]$, expressed in $mol/L$).
*   For gaseous reactions, the active mass is often more conveniently expressed as **partial pressure** (denoted by $p$, expressed in $atm$).
*   For pure solids and pure liquids involved in a heterogeneous equilibrium, the active mass is taken as **unity (1)** because their concentration (derived from density) is essentially constant and does not change during the reaction.

## 🧮 Key Derivations & Equations

### Derivation of the Equilibrium Constant ($K_c$)
Consider a general reversible reaction at constant temperature:
$$aA + bB \rightleftharpoons cC + dD$$

According to the precise definition of the Law of Mass Action:
1.  **Rate of Forward Reaction ($R_f$):**
    $$R_f \propto [A]^a [B]^b \implies R_f = k_f [A]^a [B]^b$$
    *(where $k_f$ is the velocity constant for the forward reaction)*
2.  **Rate of Backward Reaction ($R_b$):**
    $$R_b \propto [C]^c [D]^d \implies R_b = k_b [C]^c [D]^d$$
    *(where $k_b$ is the velocity constant for the backward reaction)*
3.  **At Chemical Equilibrium:**
    By definition of equilibrium, the velocity of the forward reaction perfectly equals the velocity of the backward reaction ($R_f = R_b$):
    $$k_f [A]^a [B]^b = k_b [C]^c [D]^d$$
    Rearranging the equation to group the constants on one side and variables on the other:
    $$\frac{k_f}{k_b} = \frac{[C]^c [D]^d}{[A]^a [B]^b}$$
    Since the ratio of two constants ($k_f / k_b$) yields a new constant, we define this as the **Equilibrium Constant ($K_c$)**:
    $$K_c = \frac{[C]^c [D]^d}{[A]^a [B]^b}$$

## 🏆 Golden Questions (Past Exam Archive)
### 🎯 2024 Q.6(b) State and explain the law of mass action. (03)
> **Source:** answers/2024.md

**Answer:**

**Statement of the Law of Mass Action:**
The Law of Mass Action states that *"At a given constant temperature, the rate of a chemical reaction is directly proportional to the product of the active masses (molar concentrations or partial pressures) of the reacting substances, with each concentration term raised to a power equal to its stoichiometric coefficient in the balanced chemical equation."*

**Explanation:**
Consider a general reversible reaction:
$$aA + bB \rightleftharpoons cC + dD$$

According to the law of mass action, the rate of the forward reaction ($R_f$) is proportional to the active masses of reactants A and B:
$$R_f \propto [A]^a [B]^b \implies R_f = k_f [A]^a [B]^b$$
where $k_f$ is the rate constant for the forward reaction.

Similarly, the rate of the backward reaction ($R_b$) is proportional to the active masses of products C and D:
$$R_b \propto [C]^c [D]^d \implies R_b = k_b [C]^c [D]^d$$
where $k_b$ is the rate constant for the backward reaction.

At equilibrium, $R_f = R_b$:
$$k_f [A]^a [B]^b = k_b [C]^c [D]^d$$
$$\frac{k_f}{k_b} = \frac{[C]^c [D]^d}{[A]^a [B]^b} = K_c$$

Here, $K_c$ is the **Equilibrium Constant** expressed in terms of concentration. This derivation shows how the law of mass action quantitatively defines the equilibrium state of a system.

### 🎯 2020 Q.3(a) What is chemical equilibrium? State and explain law of mass action. (04)
> **Source:** answers/2020.md

**Answer:**

**Chemical Equilibrium:**
Chemical equilibrium is the dynamic state of a reversible reaction in a closed system where the rate of the forward reaction becomes exactly equal to the rate of the backward (reverse) reaction. At this state, there is no net change in the measurable macroscopic properties of the system, such as concentrations, pressure, or color, over time.

**Law of Mass Action:**
*   **Statement:** *"At a given constant temperature, the rate of a chemical reaction is directly proportional to the product of the active masses (molar concentrations or partial pressures) of the reacting substances, with each concentration term raised to a power equal to its stoichiometric coefficient in the balanced chemical equation."*
*   **Explanation:** For a general reversible reaction: $aA + bB \rightleftharpoons cC + dD$
    The rate of the forward reaction ($R_f$) is proportional to $[A]^a [B]^b \implies R_f = k_f [A]^a [B]^b$
    The rate of the backward reaction ($R_b$) is proportional to $[C]^c [D]^d \implies R_b = k_b [C]^c [D]^d$
    At equilibrium, $R_f = R_b$:
    $k_f [A]^a [B]^b = k_b [C]^c [D]^d$
    $\frac{k_f}{k_b} = \frac{[C]^c [D]^d}{[A]^a [B]^b} = K_c$ (Equilibrium Constant).

## ⚡ Exam Tips & Common Mistakes
*   **The Crucial Phrase:** When writing the definition, if you forget to include the phrase *"raised to a power equal to its stoichiometric coefficient"*, you will lose marks. The rate depends not just on concentration, but on the stoichiometry of the balanced equation.
*   **Answering "Explain":** When a question asks to "State and explain the Law of Mass Action" (as seen in 2024, 2018, 2020), always provide the theoretical definition *first*, and then write out the short $aA + bB \rightleftharpoons cC + dD$ derivation leading to $K_c$. This mathematical derivation is exactly what the examiner wants for the "explain" portion.
