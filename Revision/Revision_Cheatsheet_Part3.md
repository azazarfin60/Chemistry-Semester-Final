# 🟢 PHASE 09 — PERIODIC TABLE (43% exam frequency)

## 9.1 Modern Periodic Law

**Mendeleev's Periodic Law:** Properties are periodic functions of **atomic weight** (now obsolete).
**Modern Periodic Law (Moseley):** Properties are periodic functions of **atomic number**.

**Why Modern Table is superior:**
1. Based on atomic number (fundamental property), not weight
2. Resolves the anomalous pair problem (Ar/K, Co/Ni, Te/I)
3. Clear placement of isotopes (same atomic number → same position)

## 9.2 Periodic Properties ⭐ (asked 2024)

| Property | Across Period (→) | Down Group (↓) |
|:---------|:-----------------|:---------------|
| **Atomic Radius** | ↓ Decreases (↑ nuclear charge, same shell) | ↑ Increases (new shell added) |
| **Ionization Potential** | ↑ Increases (harder to remove e⁻) | ↓ Decreases (e⁻ farther from nucleus) |
| **Electron Affinity** | ↑ Increases (general trend) | ↓ Decreases |
| **Electronegativity** | ↑ Increases (F is highest) | ↓ Decreases |
| **Metallic Character** | ↓ Decreases | ↑ Increases |

## 9.3 Ionization Potential ⭐

**Definition:** Minimum energy required to remove the most loosely bound electron from an isolated, gaseous atom in its ground state.

**Successive IP:** $IP_1 < IP_2 < IP_3$ (each successive electron is harder to remove — less shielding, more nuclear pull)

**Factors affecting IP:**
1. **Nuclear charge** (↑ Z → ↑ IP)
2. **Atomic size** (↑ size → ↓ IP, electron farther away)
3. **Shielding effect** (↑ inner electrons → ↓ effective nuclear charge → ↓ IP)
4. **Orbital penetration** (s > p > d > f in penetration → s electrons are harder to remove)

**Effective Nuclear Charge:** $Z_{eff} = Z - \sigma$ (where $\sigma$ = shielding constant)
- **Why 4s fills before 3d:** 4s electrons penetrate closer to nucleus → experience higher $Z_{eff}$ → lower energy → fills first

## 9.4 Classification of Elements by Electronic Configuration ⭐ (3/7 exams)

| Block | Outer Config | Elements | Properties |
|:------|:-------------|:---------|:-----------|
| **s-block** | ns¹⁻² | Groups 1–2 (alkali, alkaline earth) | Highly electropositive, strong reducing agents |
| **p-block** | ns² np¹⁻⁶ | Groups 13–18 | Contains metals, nonmetals, metalloids |
| **d-block** | (n-1)d¹⁻¹⁰ ns⁰⁻² | Groups 3–12 (transition metals) | Variable oxidation states, colored compounds, catalysts |
| **f-block** | (n-2)f¹⁻¹⁴ | Lanthanides and Actinides | Inner transition, radioactive (actinides) |

**Redox from electronic config:**
- Elements with **1–3 valence electrons** → tend to LOSE electrons → **reducing agents** (oxidized)
- Elements with **5–7 valence electrons** → tend to GAIN electrons → **oxidizing agents** (reduced)
- Noble gas config (8 valence electrons) → inert

**Pauling Electronegativity Scale:**
$$X_A - X_B = 0.208\sqrt{\Delta}$$
where $\Delta = E_{A-B} - \sqrt{E_{A-A} \times E_{B-B}}$ (bond energy values in kcal/mol)

# 🟢 PHASE 10 — ACID-BASE & BUFFER

## 10.1 Acid-Base Theories ⭐

| Theory | Acid | Base |
|:-------|:-----|:-----|
| **Arrhenius** | Gives H⁺ in water | Gives OH⁻ in water |
| **Brønsted-Lowry** | Proton (H⁺) **donor** | Proton (H⁺) **acceptor** |
| **Lewis** ⭐ | Electron pair **acceptor** | Electron pair **donor** |

**Lewis Theory** (most general):
- **Lewis Acid:** Species with empty orbital to accept e⁻ pair (e.g., BF₃, AlCl₃, H⁺, metal cations)
- **Lewis Base:** Species with lone pair to donate (e.g., NH₃, H₂O, OH⁻, halides)
- Coordinate bond forms between Lewis acid and base

**Conjugate Acid-Base Pairs** (Brønsted-Lowry):
- Every acid has a conjugate base (loses H⁺)
- Every base has a conjugate acid (gains H⁺)
- Strong acid → weak conjugate base (and vice versa)

## 10.2 pH & Ionic Equilibrium

**pH Scale:**
$$pH = -\log[H^+] \qquad pOH = -\log[OH^-]$$
$$\boxed{pH + pOH = 14} \quad \text{(at 25°C)}$$

- pH < 7 → Acidic
- pH = 7 → Neutral
- pH > 7 → Basic

**Ionic Product of Water:**
$$K_w = [H^+][OH^-] = 10^{-14} \quad \text{at 25°C}$$

## 10.3 Buffer Solutions ⭐ (Rising trend — asked 2023)

**Definition:** A solution that resists significant changes in pH upon addition of small amounts of strong acid or strong base.

**Classification:**
- **Acidic buffer:** Weak acid + its salt with strong base (e.g., CH₃COOH + CH₃COONa)
- **Basic buffer:** Weak base + its salt with strong acid (e.g., NH₄OH + NH₄Cl)

**Henderson-Hasselbalch Equation:**
$$\boxed{pH = pK_a + \log\frac{[\text{Salt}]}{[\text{Acid}]}}$$
$$pOH = pK_b + \log\frac{[\text{Salt}]}{[\text{Base}]}$$

**Buffer Action mechanism** (Acidic buffer: CH₃COOH + CH₃COONa):
- **Add strong acid (HCl):** H⁺ reacts with CH₃COO⁻ → CH₃COOH (weak acid formed, pH barely drops)
- **Add strong base (NaOH):** OH⁻ reacts with CH₃COOH → CH₃COO⁻ + H₂O (neutralized, pH barely rises)

**Common Ion Effect:** Adding a common ion (e.g., CH₃COO⁻ from salt) to a weak acid solution suppresses its ionization → shifts equilibrium backward.

## 10.4 Precision & Accuracy

| Term | Meaning |
|:-----|:--------|
| **Precision** | How close repeated measurements are to **each other** (reproducibility) |
| **Accuracy** | How close a measurement is to the **true value** |

**Dartboard analogy:**
- High precision, low accuracy → clustered shots, but off-center
- High accuracy, low precision → scattered, but average near bullseye
- High both → clustered AND on bullseye ✓

# 🟢 PHASE 11 — MISCELLANEOUS

## 11.00 Crystal Structure & Isomorphism

**Crystal:** Homogeneous solid with atoms/ions arranged in a definite, repeating 3D geometric pattern (lattice).

**7 Crystal Systems:** Cubic, Tetragonal, Orthorhombic, Monoclinic, Triclinic, Hexagonal, Rhombohedral
- Distinguished by axes lengths ($a, b, c$) and angles ($\alpha, \beta, \gamma$)

**Isomorphism:** Different chemical substances that crystallize in the **same crystal form** with similar chemical compositions.
- Example: NaF and MgO (both have rock-salt structure)

**Polymorphism:** Same chemical substance crystallizing in **different crystal forms**.
- Example: Carbon → Diamond (cubic) AND Graphite (hexagonal)
