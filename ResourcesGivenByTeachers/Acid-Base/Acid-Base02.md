## Slide 06: Calculation of Relative Strength of Weak Acids from $K_a$

For an acid in aqueous solution:
$$HA \rightleftharpoons H^+ + A^-$$
The acid dissociation constant is:
$$K_a = \frac{[H^+][A^-]}{[HA]}$$

Let $C$ moles per litre be the concentration of the acid ($HA$) and $\alpha$ be its degree of dissociation. Then:
- $[H^+] = C\alpha$
- $[A^-] = C\alpha$
- $[HA] = C(1 - \alpha)$

Substituting these values into the equilibrium expression:
$$K_a = \frac{C\alpha \times C\alpha}{C(1 - \alpha)} = \frac{C\alpha^2}{(1 - \alpha)}$$

For weak acids, $(1 - \alpha) \approx 1$. Therefore:
$$K_a \approx C\alpha^2$$

**Comparing Two Weak Acids:**
For two different acids, acid-1 and acid-2, with degrees of dissociation $\alpha_1$ and $\alpha_2$ and constants $K_1$ and $K_2$:
- For acid-1: $K_1 = C\alpha_1^2 \dots (1)$
- For acid-2: $K_2 = C\alpha_2^2 \dots (2)$

Dividing equation (1) by (2):
$$\frac{\alpha_1}{\alpha_2} = \sqrt{\frac{K_1}{K_2}}$$

Since $[H^+]$ is a measure of acid strength and it depends on the degree of dissociation $\alpha$, we can write:
$$\frac{\text{Strength of acid-1}}{\text{Strength of acid-2}} = \sqrt{\frac{K_1}{K_2}}$$

**Conclusion:**
The square root of the ratio of the dissociation constants gives the relative strengths of the two weak acids.

## Slide 07: Practice Questions

**Question -1:**
The $pH$ of an $HCl$ solution is 2. Calculate the amount ($g/L$) of the acid present in the solution.

**Solution:**
Here, $-\log[H^+] = 2$
So, $[H^+] = 10^{-2}\text{ M}$
Amount of $HCl$ in $1\text{ L} = 10^{-2}\text{ mol/L} \times 36.5\text{ g/mol} = 0.365\text{ g/L}$ (Ans)

## Slide 08: Periodic Trends in Acidity

### 1. Relative acidity of $CH_4, NH_3, H_2O, HF$:
**Conjugate bases:** $CH_3^-, NH_2^-, OH^-, F^-$

- **Trend Analysis:** As we move from $CH_3^-$ to $F^-$, the volume available to the electron increases (represented as $1/4, 1/3, 1/2, 1/1$ in the slide).
- **Effect:** Electron delocalization increases $\rightarrow$ Electron density decreases $\rightarrow$ Attraction for protons decreases $\rightarrow$ Basicity decreases.
- **Basicity Order:** $CH_3^- (\text{Strongest base}) > NH_2^- > OH^- > F^- (\text{Weakest base})$
- **Acidity Order:** $CH_4 (\text{Weakest acid}) < NH_3 < H_2O < HF (\text{Strongest acid})$

### 2. Relative acidity of $HF, HCl, HBr, HI$:
**Conjugate bases:** $F^-, Cl^-, Br^-, I^-$

- **Trend Analysis (Descending the Group):**
  - Size increases $\rightarrow$
  - Volume available to electron increases $\rightarrow$
  - Electron delocalization increases $\rightarrow$
  - Electron density decreases $\rightarrow$
  - Attraction for proton decreases $\rightarrow$
  - Basicity decreases.
- **Basicity Order:** $F^- (\text{Strongest base}) > Cl^- > Br^- > I^- (\text{Weakest base})$
- **Acidity Order:** $HF (\text{Weakest acid}) < HCl < HBr < HI (\text{Strongest acid})$

## Slide 10: Cady-Esley Concept: General Theory of Solvent System

**Definitions:**
- **Acid:** A solute that, either by direct association or by reaction with the solvent, gives the **cation** characteristics of the solvent.
- **Base:** A solute that, either by direct association or by reaction with the solvent, gives the **anion** characteristics of the solvent.

**Auto-ionization Examples:**
1. **Water:** $2H_2O \rightleftharpoons H_3O^+ + OH^-$
   - Acid gives $H_3O^+$ ion.
   - Base gives $OH^-$ ion.
2. **Dinitrogen Tetroxide:** $N_2O_4 \rightleftharpoons NO^+ + NO_3^-$
   - Acid gives $NO^+$.
   - Base gives $NO_3^-$.

**Protonic vs. Non-protonic Solvents:**
- **Protonic:** $2NH_3 \rightleftharpoons NH_4^+ + NH_2^-$
- **Non-protonic:** $2SO_2 \rightleftharpoons SO^{2+} + SO_3^{2-}$

### Utility of the Concept:
This concept can explain acid-base reactions in both aqueous and non-aqueous solvents, including those that are non-protonic.

### Limitations:
(i) Does not consider certain reactions included in the protonic definition.
(ii) Limits phenomena to solvent systems (cannot explain reactions in the absence of a solvent).
(iii) Cannot explain neutralization reactions occurring without the presence of ions.

**Summary:** This theory is essentially an extension of the Arrhenius water-ion system.
