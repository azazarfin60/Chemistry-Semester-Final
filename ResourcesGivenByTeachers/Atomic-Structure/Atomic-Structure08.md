# Atomic Structure: Slides 036-040

## Slide 36

### **Numerical Problem**

**Problem:** The radius of first Bohr orbit of hydrogen atom is 0.529 $\mathring{A}$. Calculate the radii of (i) the third orbit of $He^+$ ion and (ii) the second orbit of $Li^{2+}$ ion.

**Solution:**
Radius of nth Bohr orbit, $r_n = \frac{n^2 h^2}{4\pi^2 m Ze^2}$

For hydrogen atom Z = 1, first orbit n = 1
$r_1 = \frac{h^2}{4\pi^2 me^2} = 0.529 \mathring{A}$

**(i) For $He^+$ ion, Z = 2, third orbit, n = 3**
$r_3(He^+) = \frac{3^2 h^2}{4\pi^2 m \times 2 \times e^2} = \frac{9}{2} [\frac{h^2}{4\pi^2 me^2}] = \frac{9}{2} \times 0.529 = 2.380 \mathring{A}$

**(ii) For $Li^{2+}$ ion, Z = 3, second orbit, n = 2**
$r_2(Li^{2+}) = \frac{2^2 h^2}{4\pi^2 m \times 3 \times e^2} = \frac{4}{3} [\frac{h^2}{4\pi^2 me^2}] = \frac{4}{3} \times 0.529 = 0.7053 \mathring{A}$

## Slide 37

### **Energy of Electron in H- Atom**

**Energy of electron in Bohr model**
The energy of an electron moving in one particular energy level of the hydrogen atom in the Bohr models can be calculated by the total energy or the sum of the kinetic energy and the potential energy of an electron.

**Energy of electron in hydrogen atom**
- **Kinetic energy (T)** = $\frac{1}{2} mv^2$
- **Potential energy for moving electron (V)** = $\int_\infty^r \frac{e^2}{r^2} dr = -\frac{e^2}{r}$

If the total energy for the nth level of hydrogen atom = E
$E = \frac{1}{2} mv^2 - \frac{e^2}{r} = \frac{1}{2} mv^2 - mv^2$ (where, $\frac{e^2}{r} = mv^2$)
$= -\frac{1}{2} mv^2 = -\frac{1}{2} \frac{e^2}{r}$

When we putting, $r = \frac{n^2 h^2}{4\pi^2 m e^2}$ in the above equation
**$E = -\frac{2\pi^2 m e^4}{n^2 h^2}$**

## Slide 38

### **Heisenberg Uncertainty Principle**

According to this principle, **"Position and momentum of an electron cannot be determined simultaneously with absolute accuracy"**

If $\Delta x$ be uncertainty in position, and $\Delta p$ be uncertainty in momentum, then according to Heisenberg Uncertainty principle:
**$\Delta x \cdot \Delta p \ge \frac{h}{4\pi}$**

Since, momentum (p) of a particle is equal to the product of its mass and velocity,
$\Delta x \cdot \Delta(mv) \ge \frac{h}{4\pi}$

As the mass of a particle remains constant,
$\Delta x \cdot m \Delta v \ge \frac{h}{4\pi}$
**$\Delta x \cdot \Delta v \ge \frac{h}{4\pi m}$**

This is the mathematical formula or equation which clears that the accuracy in the measurement of one quantity (say velocity) causes uncertainty/error in other quantity (momentum), and vice-versa.

## Slide 39

### **Orbit & Orbital**

**Orbit**
- It represent well defined circular path followed by electrons around nucleus.
- It is also known as the **shell**.
- It represents two dimensional motion of electrons.
- Max. no of electrons in an orbit is **$2n^2$**
- Always Circular in shape ex K, L, M, N
- Orbits are non directional

**Orbital**
- It is three dimensional area around the nucleus where probability if finding of electrons are maximum.
- It represents three dimensional motion of electrons.
- Shapes are not fixed, can be spherical (s-orbital) dumbbell shape(p-orbital) or double dumbbell(d-orbital) or complex.
- Maximum no of electrons in an orbital is **2**.
- Orbitals are directional in nature except s orbital.
