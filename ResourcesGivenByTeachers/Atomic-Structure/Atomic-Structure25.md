# Atomic Structure: Slides 121-122

## Slide 121

### **De Broglie Wave and Kinetic Energy**

**Relation between the de-Broglie Wavelength and Kinetic Energy**

The expression for the kinetic energy of a moving body is:
**$k = \frac{1}{2} m v^2$**  ...(4)

Multiplying mass m on both sides of equation (4), we get:
**$2 k m = m^2 v^2$**

The momentum of the particle is:
**$m v = \sqrt{2 k m}$**  ...(5)

From equations (4) and (5), the de-Broglie wavelength is:
**$\lambda = \frac{h}{\sqrt{2 m k}}$**  ...(6)

## Slide 122

### **De Broglie Equation**

**Question 4: What is the wavelength of an electron moving at $5.31 \times 10^6 m/sec$?**

**Given:** mass of electron = $9.11 \times 10^{-31} kg$, h = $6.626 \times 10^{-34} J \cdot s$

**Answer:**

de Broglie’s equation is
**$\lambda = h/mv$**

$\lambda = 6.626 \times 10^{-34} J \cdot s / (9.11 \times 10^{-31} kg \times 5.31 \times 10^6 m/sec)$

$\lambda = 6.626 \times 10^{-34} J \cdot s / 4.84 \times 10^{-24} kg \cdot m/sec$

**$\lambda = 1.37 \times 10^{-10} m$**

**$\lambda = 1.37 \AA$**

The wavelength of an electron moving $5.31 \times 10^6 m/sec$ is $1.37 \times 10^{-10} m$ or $1.37 \AA$.
