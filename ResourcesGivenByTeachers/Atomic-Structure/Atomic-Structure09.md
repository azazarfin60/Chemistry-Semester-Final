## Slide 43

### **Characteristics of Orbits**

- **Orbits are quantized** means there are an infinite number of orbits around the nucleus but electrons are only present in that orbits in which their angular momentum is an integral multiple of $h/2\pi$.
- **While revolving around the nucleus** electrons neither lose nor gain energy therefore these orbitals are also called **stationary orbits**.
- **Electrons can easily move** from one orbit to another by absorbing or releasing energy equal to the energy difference b/w the two energy levels.
- **The arrangement of electrons** in orbits determines the chemical behavior and reactivity of the atom.
- **The different numbers of electrons** in orbits decide the position of the element in the periodic table.

## Slide 45

### **Orbital**

![Orbit to Orbital Mapping](file:///home/azaz/Downloads/Documents/Atomic-Structure/Atomic-Structure-045.png)

**Diagram Analysis:**
This slide bridges the gap between Bohr's orbits and the quantum mechanical subshells (orbitals).
- **Subshell Representation:** Orbitals are represented by the letters **s, p, d, f**.
- **Mapping Table:**
    - **n = 1 (K Orbit):** Contains the **1s** orbital.
    - **n = 2 (L Orbit):** Contains **2s** and **2p** orbitals.
    - **n = 3 (M Orbit):** Contains **3s**, **3p**, and **3d** orbitals.
    - **n = 4 (N Orbit):** Contains **4s**, **4p**, **4d**, and **4f** orbitals.
- **Shapes:** 
    - **S Orbital:** Spherical shape.
    - **P Orbital:** Dumbbell shape.
- **Key Note:** "Different Orbitals have different Shapes".
