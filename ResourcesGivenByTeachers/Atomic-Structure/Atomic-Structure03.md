# Atomic Structure: Slides 011-015

## Slide 11

### **Terminology**

- **Isotope:** Any of two or more forms of an element where the atoms have the same number of protons, but a different number of neutrons within their nuclei.
- **Half-life:** The time it takes for half of the original concentration of an isotope to decay back to its more stable form.
- **Carbon-12 (or $^{12}C$)** contains six protons, six neutrons, and six electrons; therefore, it has a mass number of 12 amu (six protons and six neutrons).
- **Carbon-14 (or $^{14}C$)** contains six protons, eight neutrons, and six electrons; its atomic mass is 14 amu (six protons and eight neutrons).
- **While the mass of individual isotopes is different,** their physical and chemical properties remain mostly unchanged.

## Slide 12

### **Atomic Model**

Many scientists used atomic models to understand the structure of the atom in the early centuries. Each of these models had advantages and disadvantages of its own and played a significant role in the development of the modern atomic model. Scientists like John Dalton, J.J. Thomson, Ernest Rutherford, and Niels Bohr made the most noteworthy contributions to science.

**The following theories regarding atomic structure**
- Dalton's Atomic Theory
- Thomson's Atomic Model
- Rutherford's Atomic Model
- Bohr's Atomic Model
- Quantum Mechanical Model (Electron cloud model)

## Slide 13

### **History of Atomic Model**

**ATOMIC MODELS**

![Timeline of Atomic Models](file:///home/azaz/Downloads/Documents/Atomic-Structure/Atomic-Structure-013.png)

**Diagram Analysis:**
This slide presents a visual timeline of the evolution of atomic theory:
- **1807 | J. Dalton | Solid sphere model:** Atoms are indivisible solid spheres.
- **1897 | J.J Thomson | Plum pudding model:** Discovery of electrons embedded in a positive sphere.
- **1911 | E. Rutherford | Nuclear model:** Discovery of a small, dense, positive nucleus with electrons around it.
- **1913 | N. Bohr | Planetary model:** Electrons move in specific circular orbits (energy levels) around the nucleus.
- **1926 | E. Schrodinger | Quantum model:** Electrons are found in "clouds" (orbitals) where their position is probabilistic.

### **Rutherford Gold Foil Experiment**

![Rutherford Gold Foil Experiment Setup](file:///home/azaz/Downloads/Documents/Atomic-Structure/Atomic-Structure-014.png)

**Diagram Analysis:**
The diagram illustrates the experimental setup used by Ernest Rutherford:
- **Alpha particle emitter:** Fires a beam of positive alpha particles through a **Slit**.
- **Gold foil:** A very thin sheet of gold placed in the path of the beam.
- **Zinc Sulphide / Fluorescent screen:** A circular detector that glows when hit by alpha particles.
- **Observations:** Most particles pass through (**Most particles undeflected**), but some are scattered at various angles (**Deflected particles**).

## Slide 15

### **Rutherford Gold Foil Experiment**

**SCATTERING OF ALPHA PARTICLES**

![Microscopic view of Alpha Scattering](file:///home/azaz/Downloads/Documents/Atomic-Structure/Atomic-Structure-015.png)

**Observations of Rutherford's alpha ray scattering experiment:**
1. Most of the $\alpha$-particles passed straight through the gold foil without any deviation.
2. Some of the $\alpha$-particles were deflected by the foil by some angles.
3. Interestingly one out of every 12,000 alpha particles appeared to rebound.

**Diagram Analysis:**
This microscopic view shows alpha particles (green circles with '++') interacting with gold atoms (orange circles).
- **Nucleus:** Represented as a tiny pink dot with a '+' sign.
- **Small deflection:** Occurs when alpha particles pass near the nucleus.
- **Large deflection / Turned back:** Occurs when alpha particles get very close to or hit the nucleus directly, due to electrostatic repulsion.
- **Empty space:** Most particles pass straight through the gaps between nuclei, proving the atom is mostly empty space.
