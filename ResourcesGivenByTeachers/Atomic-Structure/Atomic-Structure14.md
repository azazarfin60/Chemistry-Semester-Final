## Slide 67

### **Energy Level**

**Emission** occurs when the electron falls from an excited (high energy) to the ground, or in general, a lower energy level.

> 📷 **[Diagram]** Absorption and Emission Synthesis

**Diagram Analysis:**
This slide synthesizes both transitions into a comparative visualization:
- **Absorption (Left):** Incoming light (blue wavy arrow) hits a ground-state electron, causing it to jump to the "Higher Energy level" (indicated by a blue dashed upward arrow).
- **Emission (Right):** An excited-state electron falls to the "Lower Energy level" (indicated by a red dashed downward arrow), releasing energy as light (red wavy arrow).
- **Conclusion:** "Absorbing light with sufficient energy moves the electron in a higher energy level."

## Slide 68

### **Frequency and Wavelength Relation**

The frequency and wavelength are related to each other as follows:

**$c = \lambda \nu$**

where:
- **‘c’** is the velocity of light,
- **‘$\lambda$’** is the wavelength,
- and **‘$\nu$’** is the frequency.

We can also write this equation as:
- **$\lambda = c / \nu$**
- or, **$\nu = c / \lambda$**

It implies that the wavelength and the frequency are inversely proportional to each other.

## Slide 69

### **Spectrum**

- **A spectrum** is defined as the characteristic wavelengths of electromagnetic radiation (or a portion thereof) that is emitted or absorbed by an object or substance, atom, or molecule.
- **Atomic Spectra:** The atomic spectra are defined as the spectrum of frequencies of electromagnetic radiation emitted or absorbed during transitions of electrons between energy levels within an atom.
- **Emission spectrum:** Spectrum produced by the emitted radiation is known as emission spectrum. This spectrum corresponds to the radiation emitted (energy evolved) when an excited electron returns back to the ground state.
- **Absorption spectrum:** Spectrum produced by the absorbed radiations is called absorption spectrum.
