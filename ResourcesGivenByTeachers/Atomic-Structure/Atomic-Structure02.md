# Atomic Structure: Slides 006-010

## Slide 6

### **Structure of Water**

![Structure of Water and Hydrogen Bonds](file:///home/azaz/Downloads/Documents/Atomic-Structure/Atomic-Structure-006.png)

**Diagram Analysis:**
The diagram illustrates the molecular interaction between water molecules ($H_2O$).
- **Molecular Components:** Red spheres represent Oxygen (O) atoms, and white spheres represent Hydrogen (H) atoms.
- **Polarity:** The symbols $\delta^+$ and $\delta^-$ indicate partial positive and partial negative charges, respectively. Oxygen is more electronegative ($\delta^-$), while Hydrogen is more electropositive ($\delta^+$).
- **Hydrogen Bonds:** Indicated by dotted blue lines, these bonds form between the partially negative oxygen of one water molecule and the partially positive hydrogen of another.

## Slide 8

### **Structure of Water**

**THE POLAR COVALENT BONDS OF WATER MOLECULES**

![Polar Covalent Bonds in Water](file:///home/azaz/Downloads/Documents/Atomic-Structure/Atomic-Structure-008.png)

**Diagram Analysis:**
The diagram depicts the formation of a water molecule from its constituent atoms.
- **Atoms:** Shows two Hydrogen (H) atoms (light blue) and one Oxygen (O) atom (red).
- **Bonding Process:** The arrows indicate the sharing of electrons between the Oxygen and Hydrogen atoms.
- **Result:** A stable water molecule is formed where the Oxygen atom's outer shell is filled by sharing electrons with the two Hydrogen atoms. The "Polar Covalent" nature refers to the unequal sharing of these electrons.

### **Structure of Carbon**

**Atomic number**
- **Box 1:** $^6$C$_{12}$
- **Box 2:** $^6$C$_{13}$
**Chemical symbol**
**Mass number**

- **Carbon** has an atomic number of six, and two stable isotopes with mass numbers of twelve and thirteen, respectively. Its relative atomic mass is 12.011.
- **Mass number:** The sum of the number of protons and the number of neutrons in an atom.
- **Atomic number:** The number of protons in an atom.
- **Atomic mass:** The average mass of an atom, taking into account all its naturally occurring isotopes.
