## Slide 006

### Postulates of Arrhenius theory of ionization

*   Any electrolyte whenever dissolved in water or any polar solvent, undergoes dissociation or ionization and produces charged species called ions capable of free movement in the bulk.
*   **Electro-neutrality of an electrolytic solution:** in an electrolytic solution total number of positive charges are equal to the total number of negative charges.
*   These ions tend to recombine to form unionized electrolyte and thus there exists a dynamic equilibrium between ionized and unionized form of electrolyte. For an electrolyte AB:
    $$AB \rightleftharpoons A^+ + B^-$$
    $$K = \frac{[A^+][B^-]}{[AB]} \quad [\text{Applying law of mass action}]$$
    Here, K = equilibrium constant = dissociation constant
*   The physical and chemical properties of the electrolyte in the solution are due to the ions it furnishes in the solution. Example: electrical conductivity of electrolytic solution depends on nature and number of ions present. Also, color, magnetic, optical properties depend on the nature of ions.
*   **Extent of ionization:** the fraction of total number of molecules present as free ions in the solution is called **degree of dissociation (α)**. Degree of dissociation of an electrolyte determines whether it is weak or strong electrolyte.
    $$\alpha = \frac{\text{num of molecules dissociated into ions}}{\text{total num of molecules}}$$
    $$= \frac{\text{amount dissociated (mol/L)}}{\text{initial concentration (mol/L)}}$$

## Slide 007

### Limitations of Arrhenius theory of ionization.

Although Arrhenius theory of ionization has explained the important concept of ionization for strong and weak electrolytes, it has some limitations which are described below.

*   Strong electrolytes are almost dissociated in to its ions even at moderate concentration. Therefore, there is no chance of existence of ionic equilibrium.
*   The value of degree of ionization obtained from conductance measurement and **colligative properties**, agree well for the weak electrolytes but not for the strong electrolytes.
*   The Arrhenius theory does not consider the interionic attraction between the ions. This is the main reason of failure of it in the case of strong electrolytes. In real, there exists certain forces of interaction between the ions.

## Slide 008

### The theory of strong electrolytes stated by Debye-Huckel

(1) The strong electrolyte is completely ionized at all dilutions i.e., their degree of ionization is 100%.
(2) Since oppositely charged ions attract each other, it suggests that anions and cations are not uniformly distributed in the solution of an electrolyte but that the cations tend to be found in the vicinity of anions and vice-versa (Fig.)
(3) Decrease in equivalent conductance with increase in concentration is due to fall in mobilities of the ions due to greater inter-ionic effect and vice-versa.
(4) The ratio $\lambda_v / \lambda_{\infty}$ does not correctly give the degree of dissociation (α) for strong electrolytes but only the conductance or conductance coefficient.
(5) The two ions present in the solution obey the Coulomb’s law
    $$F \propto \frac{Q_1 \times Q_2}{r^2}$$

#### Diagram Analysis:
The figure illustrates the concept of **ionic atmospheres** in strong electrolytes:
*   **Visualization:** A group of pink anions (-) and green cations (+) are shown.
*   **Inter-ionic Attraction:** Dashed red lines connect oppositely charged ions, particularly showing a central cation (+) surrounded by four anions (-).
*   **Key Concept:** The diagram reinforces the point that ions are not randomly distributed; instead, each ion is surrounded by a "cloud" of oppositely charged ions.
*   **Caption Explanation:** "Fig. The ions of a particular charge are surrounded by more ions of the opposite charge and solvent molecules. Because of the large number of ions in concentrated solution, the ion activity is reduced due to hindered movement of the ions." This explains why conductivity decreases with concentration even if ionization is 100%.

## Slide 010

### Transport number

During electrolysis the current is carried by the anions and the cations.
The fraction of the total current carried by the cation or the anion is termed its **Transport number** or **Hittorf’s number**.

If $v_+$ represents the speed of migration of the cation and $v_-$ that of the anion,
the transport number of cation $= \frac{v_+}{v_+ + v_-}$
the transport number of anion $= \frac{v_-}{v_+ + v_-}$

The transport number of the cation is represented by $t_+$ and that of the anion by $t_-$.
$$t_+ = \frac{v_+}{v_+ + v_-} \quad \text{and} \quad t_- = \frac{v_-}{v_+ + v_-}$$
$$\frac{t_+}{t_-} = \frac{v_+}{v_-} \quad \text{and} \quad t_+ + t_- = 1$$

If the speed ratio $v_+ / v_-$ be denoted by $r$, we have,
$$r = \frac{t_+}{t_-} = \frac{t_+}{1 - t_+}$$
$$t_- = \frac{1}{1 + r}$$
