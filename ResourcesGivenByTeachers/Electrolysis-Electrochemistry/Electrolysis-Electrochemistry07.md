## Slide 031

### The zinc electrode (Zn, Zn$^{2+}$) coupled with hydrogen electrode

The half-cell whose potential is desired, is combined with the hydrogen electrode and the emf of the complete cell determined with a voltmeter. The emf of the cell is the emf of the half-cell.

$$E_{\text{cell}}^\circ = E_R^\circ - E_L^\circ$$
$$= 0 - 0.76 = - 0.76 \text{ V}$$

For example, it is desired to determine the emf of the zinc electrode, $Zn \mid Zn^{2+}$. It is connected with the SHE as shown in Fig. The complete electrochemical cell may be represented as:
$$Zn \mid Zn^{2+} \parallel H^+ \mid H_2 (1 \text{ atm}), Pt$$

#### Diagram Analysis:
The diagram illustrates a complete galvanic cell for measuring electrode potential:
*   **Anode Half-Cell (Left):** Zinc electrode ($Zn$) in 1 M $Zn^{2+}$ solution at 25°C. Reaction: $Zn \rightarrow Zn^{2+} + 2e^-$.
*   **Cathode Half-Cell (Right):** Standard Hydrogen Electrode (SHE). Platinum ($Pt$) electrode in 1 M $H^+$ solution at 25°C with $H_2$ gas at 1 atm. Reaction: $2H^+ + 2e^- \rightarrow H_2$.
*   **Circuitry:** A salt bridge connects the two compartments. A voltmeter in the external circuit measures a potential difference of 0.76V. Electrons ($e^-$) flow from the negative Zinc anode to the positive Hydrogen cathode.

## Slide 034

### Methods for pH Determination

#### i. Using hydrogen electrode
**Diagram Analysis:** Shows a concentration cell setup with two hydrogen electrodes.
*   **Left:** Reference SHE (1 M $HCl$).
*   **Right:** Test hydrogen electrode in a "Test solution" of unknown pH.
*   Connected by a salt bridge and voltmeter to measure the concentration-dependent potential.

#### ii. Using SCE instead of SHE
**Diagram Analysis:** Shows a cell where the test half-cell is coupled with a Saturated Calomel Electrode (SCE).
*   **Left (Test Electrode):** $H_2$ gas inlet and $Pt$ electrode in the "Test solution".
*   **Right (SCE Reference):** Saturated Calomel Electrode containing $Hg$, $Hg_2Cl_2$ paste, and saturated $KCl$ solution.

**Formulas:**
$$E_{\text{cell}} = E_{\text{right}} - E_{\text{left}}$$
$$= 0.2415 - (- 0.0591 \times \text{pH})$$
$$E_{\text{cell}} = 0.2415 + 0.0591 \times \text{pH}$$
$$\text{pH} = \frac{E_{\text{cell}} - 0.2415}{0.0591}$$

## Slide 035

### iii. Using glass electrode

#### Diagram Analysis:
The diagram shows the setup for measuring pH using a modern glass electrode probe:
*   **Left (Glass Electrode):** Consists of an $Ag/AgCl$ wire in 1 M $HCl$, enclosed in a thin glass bulb. It is dipped into a "Solution of unknown pH".
*   **Right (SCE Reference):** A standard Saturated Calomel Electrode ($Hg/Hg_2Cl_2/KCl$) providing a stable reference potential.
*   **Measurement:** A voltmeter connects the two to measure the total cell potential.

#### Theoretical Calculation:
We know that
$$E_{\text{cell}} = E_R - E_L$$
i.e., $E_{\text{cell}} = E_{\text{SCE}} - E_G$

Substituting the value of $E_G$:
$$E_{\text{cell}} = E_{\text{SCE}} - (E_G^\circ - 0.0591 \times \text{pH})$$
$$E_{\text{cell}} = E_{\text{SCE}} - E_G^\circ + 0.0591 \times \text{pH}$$

Rearranging for pH:
$$\text{pH} = \frac{E_{\text{cell}} - E_{\text{SCE}} + E_G^\circ}{0.0591} \text{ (or rearranged based on sign conventions)}$$
*Note: The slide formula shows:*
$$\text{pH} = \frac{E_{\text{SCE}} - E_G^\circ - E_{\text{cell}}}{0.0591}$$
