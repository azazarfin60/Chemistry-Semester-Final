### **Determination of Order of Reaction**

**Half Life Method**

*   This method is used only when the rate law involves only one concentration term.
*   $t_{1/2} \propto a^{1-n}$
*   $t_{1/2} = k' \frac{1}{a^{n-1}}$
*   $\log t_{1/2} = \log k' + (1-n) \log a$

*   Graph of $\log t_{1/2}$ vs $\log a$ gives a straight line with slope $(1-n)$, where '$n$' is the order of the reaction.
*   Determining the slope, we can find the order $n$.
*   If half-life at different concentrations is given then:
    $$(t_{1/2})_1 \propto \frac{1}{a_1^{n-1}} \text{ and } (t_{1/2})_2 \propto \frac{1}{a_2^{n-1}}$$

### **Determination of Order of Reaction**

$$\therefore \frac{(t_{1/2})_1}{(t_{1/2})_2} = \left[ \frac{a_2}{a_1} \right]^{n-1}$$

Taking logarithm and rearranging:
$$n = 1 + \frac{\log(t_{1/2})_1 - \log(t_{1/2})_2}{\log a_2 - \log a_1}$$

**Plots of half-lives vs concentration ($t_{1/2} \propto a^{1-n}$):**

*   **1st order:** $t_{1/2}$ vs Conc. (Horizontal line)
*   **2nd order:** $t_{1/2}$ vs $1/a$ (Straight line through origin)
*   **3rd order:** $t_{1/2}$ vs $1/a^2$ (Straight line through origin)

### **Arrhenius Equation**

An equation describing the mathematical relationship between temperature and the rate of a chemical reaction.

**Arrhenius equation:** mathematical expression that describes the effect of temperature on the velocity of a chemical reaction, the basis of all predictive expressions used for calculating reaction-rate constants. In the Arrhenius equation, $k$ is the reaction-rate constant, $A$ and $E$ are numerical constants characteristic of the reacting substances, $R$ is the thermodynamic gas constant, and $T$ is the absolute temperature. The equation is commonly given in the form of an exponential function:

$$k = A \exp(-E/RT)$$

It predicts that a small increase in reaction temperature will produce a marked increase in the magnitude of the reaction-rate constant.

### **Arrhenius Equation**

From Vant-Hoff’s equation:
$$\frac{d \ln K}{dT} = \frac{\Delta E}{RT^2} \quad \dots (i) \quad [K \text{- Equilibrium constant}]$$

Hence, $K = k_1/k_2$ [Ratio of rate constant of forward and backward reactions respectively]

Or, $\frac{d \ln (k_1/k_2)}{dT} = \frac{\Delta E}{RT^2}$

$$\frac{d \ln k_1}{dT} - \frac{d \ln k_2}{dT} = \frac{\Delta E}{RT^2} \quad \dots (ii)$$

The eq- (ii) can be divided into two, such as:
$$\frac{d \ln k_1}{dT} = \frac{\Delta E_1}{RT^2} + \text{constant, and } \frac{d \ln k_2}{dT} = \frac{\Delta E_2}{RT^2} + \text{constant}$$

## Slide 55

![Arrhenius Equation Derivation - Final Form](file:///home/azaz/Downloads/Documents/Chemical-Kinetics/Chemical-Kinetics-055.png)

### **Arrhenius Equation**

Here, $\Delta E = E_1 - E_2$

It is found in experimental results that the change in specific reaction rate with temperature can be expressed considering the value of constant is zero. So,

$$\frac{d \ln K}{dT} = \frac{E}{RT^2} \quad \dots (iii)$$

$[E \text{- Energy of activation}]$

The eq- (iii) is known as **Arrhenius Equation**. From this equation, we get:

$$\ln k = -\frac{E}{RT} + a (\text{constant}) \quad \dots (iv)$$

Or, $$k = A \exp(-E/RT) \quad \dots (v)$$

Here, **$A$** – frequency factor, **$T$** – kelvin temperature and **$R$** – molar gas constant.
