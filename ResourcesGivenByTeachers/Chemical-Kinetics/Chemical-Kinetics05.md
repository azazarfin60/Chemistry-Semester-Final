### **Kinetics of First Order Reaction**

$$A \rightarrow \text{Product}$$

Suppose, the initial concentration of A is "a" mole/liter and after time $t$, $x$ mole/liter of A is converted to product.

Therefore, $$\frac{dx}{dt} = k (a - x) \quad \dots (i)$$

Or, $$\int \frac{dx}{dt} = \int k(a - x)$$

Or, $$\int \frac{dx}{(a-x)} = \int kdt$$

$$-\ln (a - x) = kt + \text{constant} \quad \dots (ii)$$

When, $t = 0$, then $x = 0$, From eq- (ii)

## Slide 22

![Derivation of First Order Rate Law - Part 2](file:///home/azaz/Downloads/Documents/Chemical-Kinetics/Chemical-Kinetics-022.png)

### **Kinetics of First Order Reaction**

$$\text{Constant} = -\ln a \quad \dots (iii)$$

From eq- (ii) and (iii)

$$-\ln (a - x) = kt - \ln a$$

$$k = \frac{1}{t} \ln \frac{a}{a-x}$$

$$k = \frac{2.303}{t} \log \frac{a}{a-x} \quad \dots (iv)$$

This is the integrated rate expression for first order reaction.

## Slide 23

![Alternative Derivation - Part 1](file:///home/azaz/Downloads/Documents/Chemical-Kinetics/Chemical-Kinetics-023.png)

### **Kinetics of First Order Reaction- Alternative Method**

$$A \rightarrow \text{Product}$$

During reaction, if the concentration of A is decreased-

$$-\frac{dc}{dt} = kc \quad \dots (i)$$

Here, $c$ = concentration of reactant in mole/liter at time $t$, and $k$ = rate constant

From eq- (i)
$$-\frac{dc}{c} = kdt \quad \dots (ii)$$

By integration,
$$-\int \frac{dc}{c} = \int kdt$$

or, $-\ln c = kt + \text{constant} \quad \dots (iii)$

When $t = 0$ then, $c = c_0$

From eq- (iii)
$-\ln c_0 = 0 + \text{constant}$
$-\ln c_0 = \text{constant} \quad \dots (iv)$

## Slide 24

![Alternative Derivation - Part 2](file:///home/azaz/Downloads/Documents/Chemical-Kinetics/Chemical-Kinetics-024.png)

### **Kinetics of First Order Reaction- Alternative Method**

From eq- (iii) and (iv),
$-\ln c = kt - \ln c_0$

Or, $\ln c_0 - \ln c = kt$

$$\ln \frac{c_0}{c} = kt \quad \dots (v)$$

$$k = \frac{2.303}{t} \log \frac{c_0}{c} \quad \dots (vi)$$

This is the integrated rate expression for first order reaction.

## Slide 25

![Examples of First Order Reactions](file:///home/azaz/Downloads/Documents/Chemical-Kinetics/Chemical-Kinetics-025.png)

### **Example of First Order Reaction**

* Decomposition of $H_2O_2$ in aqueous solution
  $$H_2O_2 \rightarrow H_2O + \frac{1}{2} O_2$$

* Hydrolysis of methyl acetate in presence of mineral acids.
  $$CH_3COOCH_3 + H_2O \rightarrow CH_3COOH + CH_3OH$$

* Inversion of cane sugar in presence of mineral acids.
  $$C_{12}H_{22}O_{11} + H_2O \xrightarrow{H^+} C_6H_{12}O_6 + C_6H_{12}O_6$$

* Decomposition of ammonium nitrite in aqueous solution.
  $$NH_4NO_2 \rightarrow N_2 + 2H_2O$$

* Hydrolysis of diazo derivatives.
  $$C_5H_5N = NCl + H_2O \rightarrow C_6H_5OH + N_2 + HCl$$
  *(Note: The slide likely refers to Benzenediazonium chloride, usually $C_6H_5N_2Cl$)*
