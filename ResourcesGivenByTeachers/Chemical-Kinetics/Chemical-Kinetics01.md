## Slide 2

> 📷 **[Diagram]** Kinetics - Background

### **Kinetics - Background**

* To understand & predict behaviour of a chemical system one must consider both Thermodynamics & Kinetics

> **Thermodynamics :** does a reaction takes place ???
>
> **Kinetics :** how fast does a reaction proceed ???

* Factors to be considered when predicting whether or not a change will take place

1. Gibbs Free Energy $\Delta G$
2. Entropy Change $\Delta S$
3. The **RATE** of the change

**Diagram Analysis:**
The slide features a flowchart-like mapping that distinguishes between thermodynamic and kinetic factors:
* **Thermodynamics:** Is mapped to **Gibbs Free Energy ($\Delta G$)** and **Entropy Change ($\Delta S$)**. These factors determine the spontaneity and feasibility of a reaction.
* **Kinetics:** Is mapped to **The RATE of the change**. This focuses on the speed and mechanism by which a reaction occurs.

## Slide 3

> 📷 **[Diagram]** Rate of Reaction Equations

### **Rate of Reaction**

The **rate of reaction** is the change in the amount of a reactant or product per unit time.

If the amount of product is $dx$ at the time interval $dt$, the rate of reaction will be $\frac{dx}{dt}$

If the reduced amount of reactant is $dc$ at that time interval $dt$, the rate of reaction will be $-\frac{dc}{dt}$

The -ve sign is used because the concentration of reactant is reduced with time.

Suppose, $A \rightarrow B$

If the molar concentration of A is $c$; than according to law of mass action,

$\frac{dx}{dt} \propto c$ or, $\frac{dx}{dt} = kc$ or, $k = \frac{dx/dt}{c}$
[ $k$ = proportionality constant known as **rate constant** ]

If $c = 1$ mole/litre; then $k = \frac{dx}{dt}$ From this equation, we can define the rate constant

## Slide 4

> 📷 **[Diagram]** Rate of Reaction Visual Representation

### **Rate of Reaction**

**Diagram Analysis:**
This diagram provides a visual progression of a chemical reaction $A \rightarrow B$ over a span of 40 seconds using three spherical flasks:
* **At 0 s:** The flask is filled entirely with red spheres, representing 1.00 mol of reactant **A**. There are 0 mol of product **B**.
* **At 20 s:** A significant portion of the red spheres has been converted into blue spheres. The concentrations are now 0.54 mol **A** and 0.46 mol **B**.
* **At 40 s:** Further conversion has occurred, with blue spheres dominating the flask. The concentrations are 0.30 mol **A** and 0.70 mol **B**.
* **Key Concept:** The visualization clearly shows the depletion of reactant molecules and the simultaneous accumulation of product molecules as time elapses.

The slide presents a quantitative graph and a series of beakers illustrating the kinetics of the reaction $A \rightarrow B$:
* **The Graph:**
