# Slide 11

![Why Molarity changes with Temperature](Solution-011.png)

## **Why**

**Molarity is a function of temperature.**

### **Pedagogical Analysis:**
This slide poses a conceptual "Why" question.
- **Concept:** Molarity ($M$) is defined as moles of solute per volume of solution ($V$). Since liquids undergo thermal expansion or contraction, the volume of a solution changes with temperature.
- **Implication:** Because the denominator ($V$) in the molarity formula is temperature-dependent, the molarity of a solution will change as temperature changes. This is contrasted with units like molality ($m$), which depend on mass and are therefore temperature-independent.

# Slide 12

![Numerical Problem - Molarity 1 & 2](Solution-012.png)

## **Numerical Problem**

**Formula:** $\text{Molarity} = \frac{\text{moles of solute}}{\text{Liter of solution}}$

### **Example 1:**
**Question:** What is the molarity of a solution containing 1.4 mol of acetic acid in 250 mL of solution?
- **Conversion:** $250 \text{ mL} \times \frac{1 \text{ L}}{1000 \text{ mL}} = 0.25 \text{ L}$
- **Calculation:** $\text{Molarity} = \frac{1.4 \text{ mol of acetic acid}}{0.25 \text{ L of solution}} = 5.6 \text{ M}$

### **Example 2:**
**Question:** What is the molarity of a solution prepared by dissolving 60.0 g of NaOH in 0.250 L of solution?
- **Step 1 (Moles):** $60.0 \text{ g of NaOH} \times \frac{1 \text{ mol}}{40.0 \text{ g}} = 1.50 \text{ mol of NaOH}$
- **Step 2 (Molarity):** $\text{M} = \frac{1.50 \text{ mol of NaOH}}{0.250 \text{ L of solution}} = 6.00 \text{ M}$

# Slide 13

![Numerical Problem - Molarity 4 & 5](Solution-013.png)

## **Numerical Problem**

### **Example 4:**
**Question:** What volume (L) of 1.5 M HCl solution contains 6.0 moles of HCl?
- **Calculation:** $6.0 \text{ mol HCl} \times \frac{1 \text{ L solution}}{1.5 \text{ mol HCl}} = 4.0 \text{ L of solution}$

### **Example 5:**
**Question:** What volume (mL) of 2.0 M NaOH solution contains 20.0 g of NaOH?
- **Step 1 (Moles):** $20.0 \text{ g NaOH} \times \frac{1 \text{ mol}}{40.0 \text{ g}} = 0.500 \text{ mol of NaOH}$
- **Step 2 (Volume in L):** $0.500 \text{ mol NaOH} \times \frac{1 \text{ L solution}}{2.0 \text{ mol NaOH}} = 0.25 \text{ L of solution}$
- **Step 3 (Volume in mL):** $0.25 \text{ L} \times \frac{1000 \text{ mL}}{1 \text{ L}} = 250 \text{ mL}$

# Slide 14

![Numerical Problem - H2SO4 Molarity & Molality](Solution-014.png)

## **Numerical Problem**

**Problem:** Calculate molarity and molality of the sulphuric acid solution of density $1.198 \text{ g cm}^{-3}$ containing 27 % by mass of sulphuric acid.

- **Given:**
  - Density of the solution = $1.198 \text{ g cm}^{-3}$
  - % mass of sulphuric acid = $27\%$
- **To Find:** Molarity ($M$) = ? and Molality ($m$) = ?

### **Solution:**
1.  **Consider 100 g of solution:**
    - Mass of $H_2SO_4 = 27 \text{ g}$
    - Mass of $H_2O = 100 - 27 \text{ g} = 73 \text{ g} = 0.073 \text{ kg}$
2.  **Molecular mass calculation:**
    - $H_2SO_4 = (1 \times 2) + 32 + (16 \times 4) = 98 \text{ g mol}^{-1}$
3.  **Number of moles ($n_B$):**
    - $n_B = \frac{27 \text{ g}}{98 \text{ g mol}^{-1}} = 0.2755 \text{ mol}$
4.  **Volume of solution ($V$):**
    - $V = \frac{\text{Mass}}{\text{Density}} = \frac{100 \text{ g}}{1.198 \text{ g cm}^{-3}} = 83.47 \text{ cm}^3 = 0.08347 \text{ L}$
5.  **Molarity ($M$):**
    - $M = \frac{0.2755 \text{ mol}}{0.08347 \text{ L}} = 3.301 \text{ M}$
6.  **Molality ($m$):**
    - $m = \frac{0.2755 \text{ mol}}{0.073 \text{ kg}} = 3.774 \text{ mol kg}^{-1}$

**Ans:** The molarity of solution is 3.301 M and the molality is 3.774 m.

# Slide 15

![Numerical Problem - KCl Molality](Solution-015.png)

## **Numerical Problem**

**Problem:** 7.45 g of potassium chloride (KCl) was dissolved in 100 g of water. Calculate the molality of the solution.

- **Given:**
  - Mass of solute (KCl) = $7.45 \text{ g}$
  - Mass of solvent (water) = $100 \text{ g} = 0.1 \text{ kg}$
- **To Find:** Molality of solution = ?

### **Solution:**
1.  **Molecular mass of KCl:**
    - $KCl = 39 + 35.5 = 74.5 \text{ g mol}^{-1}$
2.  **Number of moles of solute:**
    - $n = \frac{7.45 \text{ g}}{74.5 \text{ g mol}^{-1}} = 0.1 \text{ mol}$
3.  **Molality ($m$):**
    - $m = \frac{\text{Number of moles of solute}}{\text{Mass of solvent in kg}}$
    - $m = \frac{0.1 \text{ mol}}{0.1 \text{ kg}} = 1 \text{ mol kg}^{-1}$

**Ans:** The molality of solution is $1 \text{ mol kg}^{-1}$ or 1 m.
