# Slide 16

![Solubilization Process](Solution-016.png)

## **Solubilization Process**

- **Breaking of inter-ionic or inter-molecular bonds in the solute**
- **The separation of the molecules of the solvent to provide space in the solvent for the solute**
- **Interaction between the solvent and solute molecule or ion**

### **Pedagogical Analysis of Diagrams:**
The slide breaks down the dissolving process into three fundamental molecular steps using colored sphere models:
1.  **Solute Breakdown (Top):** Shows a bulk solid represented by green paired spheres breaking away into individual pairs. This illustrates the energy required to overcome lattice energy or intermolecular forces.
2.  **Solvent Preparation (Middle):** Shows a dense arrangement of red spheres (solvent) expanding to create "holes" or gaps. This represents the energy needed to overcome solvent-solvent attractions.
3.  **Integration (Bottom):** Shows the green solute molecules fitting perfectly into the gaps between the red solvent molecules. This represents the formation of new solute-solvent interactions (solvation), which releases energy.

# Slide 17

![Solubilization Process - Macro to Micro](Solution-017.png)

## **Solubilization Process**

### **Pedagogical Analysis of Diagrams:**
This slide provides several visual examples of solubility and the dissolution process across different scales:
- **Immiscible Liquids (Top Left):** A photograph of a beaker containing vegetable oil and water. They form two distinct layers (oil on top, water on bottom), illustrating that non-polar and polar substances do not mix.
- **Molecular Incompatibility (Top Center):** A diagram comparing oil molecules ($CH_2$ chains) and water molecules ($H_2O$). The water molecules are shown with red dashed lines representing **hydrogen bonding**, while the oil molecules lack such polar interactions, explaining their immiscibility.
- **Sugar Dissolution (Bottom Left):** A sequence showing a sugar cube at the bottom of a beaker. It disintegrates into microscopic particles which then distribute uniformly to form a "Sugary" solution.
- **Ionic Dissolution (Right):** A 3D model titled **"Salt Crystal Dissolving"**. It depicts water molecules (red and white) pulling individual ions ($Na^+$ and $Cl^-$) away from a green and white crystal lattice, showcasing the solvation of ions.

# Slide 18

![Like Dissolves Like](Solution-018.png)

## **Like Dissolves Like**

**Polar solvents love polar solutes to be dissolved in it and non polar with non polar. Often said as like dissolves like.**

### **Pedagogical Analysis of Diagrams:**
- **Ionic Solvation (Left & Center):**
    - The **NaCl crystal structure** is shown as a lattice of charged spheres.
    - When placed in water, the diagram shows how water molecules orient themselves based on charge: the negative oxygen ends (red) face the positive $Na^+$ ions, and the positive hydrogen ends (white) face the negative $Cl^-$ ions.
- **Polarity Comparison (Right):** A simplified diagram labeled "polar/nonpolar" shows red polar ellipsoids with clearly defined "+" and "-" poles separated from grey, featureless non-polar spheres, reinforcing the concept that substances with similar electronic properties mix more readily.

### **Pedagogical Analysis of Diagrams:**
This slide focuses on the dissolution of Sodium Chloride (Common Salt) in water:

# Slide 20

![Factors Affecting Solubility](Solution-020.png)

## **Factors Affecting Solubility**

### **Pedagogical Analysis of Diagram:**
The slide uses a radial mind map to summarize the key variables that influence how much solute can be dissolved in a given amount of solvent.
- **Central Concept:** Factors affecting solubility.
- **Influencing Factors:**
    1.  **Particle size:** Smaller particles have more surface area, often increasing the rate of dissolution.
    2.  **Particle shape:** Can affect how particles interact and fit within the solvent matrix.
    3.  **Temperature:** Usually increases the solubility of solids in liquids but decreases the solubility of gases.
    4.  **Ionization of solute & solvent:** Charged particles interact strongly with polar solvents.
    5.  **Nature of solute and solvent:** Based on the "like dissolves like" principle.
    6.  **Physical forms of drug:** Particularly relevant in pharmacology (e.g., crystalline vs. amorphous).
    7.  **Pressure:** Significantly affects the solubility of gases (Henry's Law).
    8.  **Polarity:** Determines the compatibility of the components.
