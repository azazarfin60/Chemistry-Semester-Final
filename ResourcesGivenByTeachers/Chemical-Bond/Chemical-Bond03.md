## Slide 12

### Van Der Waals Forces of Attraction
It is the intermolecular attractive force which exists between all kinds of atoms, molecules and ions when they are sufficiently close to each other.

### Types of Van der Waals forces:

1.  **Dipole-dipole interactions:** These forces are found in polar molecules having permanent polarity in them (e.g., Carbonyls).
2.  **Ion-dipole interactions:** Polar molecules are attracted to ions. The negative end of the dipole is attracted to the cation and vice versa (e.g., $NaCl + H_2O$).
3.  **Dipole-induced dipole interactions:** Found in a mixture of polar and non-polar molecules. When a non-polar molecule is brought near a polar molecule, the positive end of the polar molecule attracts the mobile electrons of the non-polar molecule, inducing polarity (e.g., $HCl + Ar$).
4.  **Instantaneous dipole-induced dipole interactions (London Forces):** Found in non-polar molecules such as diatomic gases ($H_2, O_2, N_2$).

**Mechanism of Instantaneous Dipole:**
As the electron cloud around the positive center oscillates, it becomes more dense on one side temporarily, creating an instantaneous dipole. This self-polarized molecule then polarizes neighboring molecules, creating induced dipoles.

## Slide 13

### Diagram Analysis: Van der Waals Interactions

**1. Dipole-dipole interactions**
*   **Description:** Shows several ellipsoidal polar molecules with positive ($+$) and negative ($-$) ends. They are aligned so that opposite charges face each other, facilitating attraction.

**2. Ion-dipole interaction**
*   **(a) Cation ($M^+$):** Shows a central positive ion surrounded by polar molecules, with their negative ends pointing toward the cation.
*   **(b) Anion ($A^-$):** Shows a central negative ion surrounded by polar molecules, with their positive ends pointing toward the anion.

**3. Dipole-induced dipole interaction**
*   **Process:** Starts with Molecule A (Polar) and Molecule B (Non-polar). Interaction results in Molecule A remaining a Permanent Dipole while Molecule B becomes an Induced Dipole with its electron cloud shifted.

**4. Instantaneous dipole-induced dipole interaction**
*   **(a)** A non-polar molecule with a symmetrical electron cloud around a positive center.
*   **(b)** Temporary displacement of the electron cloud makes the molecule polar.
*   **(c)** This temporarily polar molecule induces a dipole in a neighboring molecule.

## Slide 15

### Postulates of Valence Bond Theory (VBT)

**Core Principles:**
*   A covalent bond forms only when half-filled orbitals of two atoms overlap.
*   Each overlapping orbital should contain an unpaired electron with an opposite spin.
*   Overlapping orbitals must have nearly comparable energies.
*   Electron spins are neutralized and electrons pair up upon overlapping.
*   Electron density increases between the two nuclei during overlap, decreasing nuclear repulsion and liberating energy.
*   Bonds form at an equilibrium distance where the system has minimum potential energy and maximum stability.
*   **Bond Strength:** Depends on the extent of overlap. Greater overlap leads to more energy release and a stronger bond.

**Types and Geometry:**
*   **Sigma ($\sigma$) and Pi ($\pi$) bonds:** Sigma bonds form by overlapping along the nuclear axis; Pi bonds form by sideways overlapping.
*   The number of covalent bonds equals the number of unpaired electrons in the valence shell.
*   Covalent bonds have **directional characteristics** (except for s-orbitals which are spherically symmetrical).
*   **Molecular Geometry:** Decided by the directional orientation of overlapping orbitals (p, d, f). Orbitals orient themselves to minimize electronic repulsion and maximize symmetry (staying as far apart as possible).
