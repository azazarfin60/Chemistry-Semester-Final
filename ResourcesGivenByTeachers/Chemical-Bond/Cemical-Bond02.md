## Slide 7

### Polarization
When a cation approaches closely the anion, it withdraws the electrons of the anion towards itself and the electron cloud of the anion gets distorted from its symmetrical shape and is elongated towards the cation, this phenomenon is known as **polarization**.

### Factors affecting the magnitude of polarizing power of cation and polarizability of anion:
1.  **Charge on cation and anion:** Higher charge increases both.
2.  **Size of cation and anion:** Small cation and large anion favor polarization (e.g., $NaI$ vs $NaCl$; $NaOH$ vs $KOH$).
3.  **Electronic configuration of cation:** Presence of d orbital increases polarizing power (e.g., $Na^+$ vs $Cu^+$).

### Variation in the Periodic Table

**1. Polarizing power of cation:**
*   **In a period (Across):** Increases (from s-Block to p-Block).
*   **In a group (Down):** Decreases.

**2. Polarizability of anion:**
*   **In a period (Across):** Decreases (due to decrease in both negative charge and size).
*   **In a group (Down):** Increases (increase in size increases polarizability).

### Diagram Analysis: Polarization Visualization
*   **Diagram (a):** Shows a cation ($C^+$) and an anion ($A^-$) where the anion's electron cloud is still symmetrical.
*   **Diagram (b):** Shows the same pair where the anion's electron cloud is distorted and elongated towards the cation, representing the polarized state.
*   **Flowcharts:** Visualize the trends mentioned above, showing increasing/decreasing directions for polarizing power and polarizability across s-Block and p-Block elements.

## Slide 8

### Hanny and Smyth Equation
Used to calculate the percentage of ionic character in a bond based on electronegativity differences ($X_A$ and $X_B$).

**Formula:**
$$ \% \text{ ionic character in A-B bond} = [0.16(X_A - X_B) + 0.035(X_A - X_B)^2] \times 100\% $$
$$ = [16(X_A - X_B) + 3.5(X_A - X_B)^2]\% $$

**Example Problem:**
Calculate the percentage of ionic character in Na-Cl bond in NaCl molecule ($X_{Na} = 0.9$ and $X_{Cl} = 3.0$).
*   **Solution:** Electronegativity difference ($\Delta X$) = $3.0 - 0.9 = 2.1$
*   $\% \text{ ionic character} = [16 \times 2.1 + 3.5 \times (2.1)^2]\% = 49\%$

### Applications of Dipole Moment
Describes the degree of polarity: $\mu = e \times d$

**Example (NaCl):**
*   **Given:** $d = 2.365 \text{ \AA}$, $\mu_{\text{exp}} = 8.5 \text{ D}$
*   **Theoretical value of } \mu = (1 \times 4.8 \times 10^{-10} \text{ esu}) \times 2.365 \text{ \AA} = 11.352 \text{ D}$
*   $\% \text{ ionic character} = \frac{\mu_{\text{exp}}}{\mu_{\text{theo}}} \times 100\% = \frac{8.5}{11.352} \times 100\% \approx 75\%$

## Slide 9

### Nature of Metallic Bond
A metal is regarded as a group of positive metal ions packed together as closely as possible in a regular geometric pattern and immersed in a **sea of electrons** (electron-pool or electron-cloud). These mobile or delocalized electrons move freely in vacant valence orbitals. The attractive force that binds the metal ions to the mobile electrons is called the **metallic bond**.

### Properties of Metals
*   Electrical conductivity
*   Thermal conductivity
*   Luster
*   Ductile
*   Photoelectric effect

### Diagram Analysis: Sea of Electrons Model
*   **Description:** A schematic representation showing "Positive Metal Cores" (represented by circles with '+') arranged in a lattice.
*   **Mobile Electrons:** Represented by small dots distributed between the cores.
*   **Interactions:** Arrows indicate the electrostatic attraction between the positive cores and the surrounding delocalized electrons, which constitutes the metallic bond.

## Slide 10

### Types of Hydrogen Bond
A hydrogen atom may be linked to two similar or different electronegative atoms. It is bonded to one by a covalent bond and to the other by a weaker **hydrogen bond**.

**1. Inter-molecular hydrogen bond:**
*   Occurs between two or more molecules of the same or different compounds.
*   **Examples:** $NH_3, H_2O, HF$, and p-nitrophenol.
*   **Diagram:** Shows p-nitrophenol molecules associated through H-bonds between the oxygen of a nitro group and the hydrogen of a hydroxyl group on an adjacent molecule.

**2. Intra-molecular hydrogen bond:**
*   Formed between a H-atom and an electronegative atom present in the **same molecule** (intra means within).
*   **Diagrams/Examples:**
    *   **o-nitro phenol:** H-bond between the $-OH$ and $-NO_2$ groups.
    *   **o-hydroxy benzaldehyde (salicylaldehyde):** H-bond between $-OH$ and $C=O$.
    *   **o-hydroxy benzoic acid (salicylic acid):** H-bond between $-OH$ and the $C=O$ of the carboxyl group.

### Comparative Observations:
*   Melting point of **o-nitrophenol** is $214^\circ C$, while its **p-isomer** is $279^\circ C$ (due to association in the p-isomer).
*   o-nitrophenol is volatile in steam and less soluble in water.
*   p-nitrophenol shows inter-molecular H-bonding, resulting in the association of molecules.
