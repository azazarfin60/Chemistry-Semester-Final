# Chemical Bond - Slides 031-035

## Slide 31

### Molecular Orbital Theory (MOT)
In MOT, all atomic orbitals of the participating atoms approach each other and mix to give an equivalent number of new orbitals that belong to the molecule as a whole. These are called **molecular orbitals (MO)**.

### The Principles of MOT:
1.  **Orbital Conservation:** The number of molecular orbitals produced equals the number of atomic orbitals combined.
2.  **Energy Levels:** Bonding molecular orbitals (BMO) are lower in energy than the parent orbitals; antibonding molecular orbitals (AMO) are higher in energy.
3.  **Aufbau Principle:** Electrons are assigned to orbitals starting from the lowest energy level.
4.  **Effectiveness:** Atomic orbitals combine most effectively when they have similar energies.

## Slide 32

### Electronic Configuration of Molecular Orbitals

The order of filling changes based on the total number of electrons in the molecule:

**For $\leq 14$ electrons:**
$$\sigma 1s < \sigma^* 1s < \sigma 2s < \sigma^* 2s < (\pi 2p_x = \pi 2p_y) < \sigma 2p_z < (\pi^* 2p_x = \pi^* 2p_y) < \sigma^* 2p_z$$

**For $> 14$ electrons:**
$$\sigma 1s < \sigma^* 1s < \sigma 2s < \sigma^* 2s < \sigma 2p_z < (\pi 2p_x = \pi 2p_y) < (\pi^* 2p_x = \pi^* 2p_y) < \sigma^* 2p_z$$

### Utility of MOT:
*   **Bond Order (B.O.):** $\frac{N_b - N_a}{2}$ (where $N_b$ is the number of bonding electrons and $N_a$ is the number of antibonding electrons).
*   **Bond Length:** Inversely proportional to Bond Order.
*   **Stability:** Proportional to Bond Order and inversely proportional to the number of antibonding electrons.
*   **Magnetic Property:** Paramagnetic if unpaired electrons exist; Diamagnetic if all electrons are paired.

## Slide 33

### Case Studies in MOT

| Molecule | Configuration | Bond Order | Stability | Magnetic Nature |
| :--- | :--- | :---: | :---: | :--- |
| **$He_2$** | $\sigma 1s^2, \sigma^* 1s^2$ | 0 | Unstable | - |
| **$H_2$** | $\sigma 1s^2, \sigma^* 1s^0$ | 1 | Most Stable | Diamagnetic |
| **$H_2^+$** | $\sigma 1s^1, \sigma^* 1s^0$ | $1/2$ | Stable | Paramagnetic |
| **$H_2^-$** | $\sigma 1s^2, \sigma^* 1s^1$ | $1/2$ | Less Stable* | Paramagnetic |

*\*Stability Order: $H_2 > H_2^+ > H_2^-$ (Antibonding electrons in $H_2^-$ decrease stability relative to $H_2^+$).*
**Bond Length Order:** $H_2 < H_2^+ = H_2^-$

## Slide 34

### Formation of $\sigma$ and $\pi$ Molecular Orbitals

Molecular orbitals are formed by the interference of electron waves:

1.  **Bonding M.O. (BMO):** Result from **constructive interference** (waves add up). Nuclei are shielded from each other, repulsion is minimum, and energy is lower.
2.  **Antibonding M.O. (AMO):** Result from **destructive interference** (waves cancel out). Electron density between nuclei is very low, nuclei repel each other, and energy is higher.

### Diagram Analysis: Visual Interference
*   **s-s combination:** Shows two circular s-orbitals merging into a single large oval ($\sigma 1s$) or splitting into two lobes with a node in between ($\sigma^* 1s$).
*   **p-p head-on:** Shows dumbbells merging along the axis for $\sigma 2p_z$ and $\sigma^* 2p_z$.
*   **p-p side-on:** Shows dumbbells overlapping sideways to form $\pi 2p$ (two lobes above/below axis) and $\pi^* 2p$ (four lobes).
*   **Wave Interference:** Shows sine waves adding (Constructive $\to$ higher peak) and subtracting (Destructive $\to$ zero amplitude/flat line).

## Slide 35

### Extended Molecular Configurations (Handwritten Reference)

*   **$Be_2$:** $\sigma 1s^2 \sigma^* 1s^2 \sigma 2s^2 \sigma^* 2s^2$ (B.O. = 0)
*   **$B_2$:** $\sigma 1s^2 \sigma^* 1s^2 \sigma 2s^2 \sigma^* 2s^2 (\pi 2p_x^1 = \pi 2p_y^1)$
*   **$C_2$:** $\sigma 1s^2 \sigma^* 1s^2 \sigma 2s^2 \sigma^* 2s^2 (\pi 2p_x^2 = \pi 2p_y^2)$
*   **$N_2$:** $(KK)(KK) (\pi 2p_x^2 = \pi 2p_y^2) \sigma 2p_z^2$ (B.O. = 3)
*   **$N_2^-$:** $(KK)(KK) (\pi 2p_x^2 = \pi 2p_y^2) \sigma 2p_z^2 \pi^* 2p_x^1$
*   **$O_2$:** $(\sigma 1s^2 \sigma^* 1s^2) (\sigma 2s^2 \sigma^* 2s^2) \sigma 2p_z^2 (\pi 2p_x^2 = \pi 2p_y^2) (\pi^* 2p_x^1 = \pi^* 2p_y^1)$
*   **$O_2$ Magnetic Nature:** **Paramagnetic** (Due to two unpaired electrons in antibonding $\pi^*$ orbitals).
