## Slide 16

### Types of Overlap of Atomic Orbitals

A covalent bond between two atoms results from the following three types of overlap:

1.  **s-s overlap:** Half-filled s-orbital of one atom overlaps with the half-filled s-orbital of another atom.
2.  **s-p overlap:** Half-filled s-orbital of one atom overlaps with the half-filled p-orbital of another atom.
3.  **p-p overlap:** Singly-filled p-orbital of one atom overlaps with the half-filled p-orbital of another. This occurs in two ways:
    *   **(a) p-p head-to-head overlap:** Also called head-on, end-on, or **linear overlap**. Occurs along the line joining the nuclei (nuclear axis/bond axis).
    *   **(b) p-p side-to-side overlap:** Also called side-wise, side-way, or **lateral overlap**. Occurs along a line perpendicular to the molecular axis. The p-orbitals must be parallel.

### Diagram Analysis: Overlap Visuals
*   **(a) s-s overlap:** Two spherical s-orbitals overlapping.
*   **(b) s-p overlap:** A spherical s-orbital overlapping with one lobe of a dumbbell-shaped p-orbital.
*   **(c) p-p head-to-head overlap:** Two p-orbitals overlapping end-to-end along the horizontal axis.
*   **(d) p-p side-to-side overlap:** Two p-orbitals standing parallel and overlapping sideways.
*   **Note:** Shaded portions indicate the overlap region containing an electron pair with opposite spins.

## Slide 17

### $\sigma$ (Sigma) Bond
A covalent bond formed by the overlap of half-filled atomic orbitals along the line joining the nuclei (nuclear axis). It is produced by **head-to-head** overlap.

### Characteristics of $\sigma$-Bond:
1.  **Shape:** The boundary surface takes up an ovoid shape (recorded as "avoid" in slide).
2.  **Symmetry:** The electron cloud is symmetrical about the bond axis.
3.  **Electron Distribution:** Contains two electrons with opposite spins. The probability of finding them is maximum in the region between the two nuclei on the bond axis.
4.  **Attraction:** Electrons are attracted equally by the nuclei of both atoms.

### Diagram Analysis: Formation of $H_2$
*   **Process:** $H(1s^1) \uparrow + H(1s^1) \downarrow$
*   **Result:** The two s-orbitals merge to form an s-s overlap, resulting in the $H-H$ $\sigma$ bond.

## Slide 18

### $\pi$ (Pi) Bond
A covalent bond formed by the overlap of singly-filled p-orbitals along a line perpendicular to their nuclear axis (**side-to-side** overlap).

### Characteristics of $\pi$-bond:
1.  **Nodal Plane:** Has one nodal plane containing the nuclear axis. This divides the bond into two "sausage-like" halves (one above and one below the plane). It has two lobes.
2.  **Electron Density:** Concentrated above and below the plane of the $\sigma$ bond. It is **unsymmetrical** about the nuclear axis.
3.  **Inter-nuclear Region:** Increased electron density exists in the inter-nuclear region, but not directly on the bond axis.

### Diagram Analysis: Multiple Bonding
*   **Process:** Shows atoms (likely Nitrogen) with three p-orbitals each ($2p_x, 2p_y, 2p_z$).
*   **Formation:**
    *   One pair forms a **head-to-head overlap** ($\sigma$ bond).
    *   The other two pairs form **side-to-side overlaps** (two $\pi$ bonds).
*   **Final Structure:** A central $\sigma$ bond cloud with two $\pi$ bond clouds (lobes above/below and front/back).

## Slide 19

### Importance of Valence Bond Theory
VBT introduced five foundational concepts:
1.  Delocalization of electrons over two nuclei.
2.  Shielding effect of electrons.
3.  Essential covalent character of bonds.
4.  Partial ionic nature in covalent bonds.
5.  Concept of resonance and its link to molecular stability.

### Limitations of Valence Bond Theory
1.  **Paramagnetism of Oxygen:** Predicts $O_2$ should be diamagnetic (no unpaired electrons), but it is experimentally found to be para-magnetic.
2.  **Electron-Deficient Molecules:** Fails to explain bonding in molecules like $B_2H_6$ which have fewer electrons than needed for full octets.
3.  **Bond Equivalence ($BeCl_2$):** Predicts different bond types ($2s-3p$ and $2p-3p$) which should have different energies/lengths. Experimentally, both Be-Cl bonds are identical.

## Slide 20

### Hybridization
The process of mixing pure atomic orbitals of nearly equal energy on an atom to produce a new set of orbitals. These hybrid orbitals:
*   Are equal in number to the original orbitals.
*   Have the same energy and identical shapes.
*   Are symmetrically disposed in space.

### Rules for Hybridization:
1.  Only orbitals of similar energies from the same atom can hybridize.
2.  The number of hybrid orbitals produced equals the number of orbitals mixed.
3.  Hybrid bonds are generally **stronger** than non-hybridized bonds of comparable energy.
4.  Hybrid orbitals differ in their spatial orientation.
5.  Hybridization allows for the prediction of molecular geometry and bond angles.
6.  The spatial orientation is determined by the number of mixed orbitals and the specific axes ($x, y, z$) of the original pure orbitals.
