## Slide 2

### Chemical Bond
A chemical bond is defined as the attractive force that holds two or more atoms together in a molecule or an ion.

### Cause of Chemical Bonding
1.  **Net attractive force between atoms.**
2.  **Octet rule or rule of eight.**
3.  **Lowering of energy of combining atoms:** the process of chemical bonding between the atoms decreases the energy of the combining atoms and gives rise to the formation of a system which has lower energy and hence has greater stability.

## Slide 3

### Types of Bonds

*   **Ionic Bond:** The chemical bond formed between two atoms by the transfer of one or more valence electrons from one atom to the other is called ionic bond. This bond is also called **electrovalent** or **polar bond**.
*   **Covalent Bond:** The chemical bond between two atoms in which the electrons (in pairs) are shared by both the participating atoms is called covalent bond. This bond is also called **electron pair bond**.
*   **Coordinate Bond:** A covalent bond which is formed by the mutual sharing of two electrons both of which are provided entirely by one of the linked atoms (or ions) is called a coordinate bond.
*   **Metallic Bonding:** The peculiar type of bonding which holds the metal atoms together in a metal crystal is called metallic bonding.
*   **Hydrogen Bond:** The attractive electrostatic force between a hydrogen atom which is already covalently attached with a strongly electronegative atom of a molecule and another electronegative atom (O, N, F) of some other molecule (same molecule or different molecule) is known as hydrogen bond.
*   **Van der Waals Forces:** These are very short-lived inter-molecular attractive forces believed to exist between all kinds of atoms, molecules and ions when they are sufficiently close to each other.

## Slide 4

### Formation of Ionic Bond

**Diagram 1: General Formation**
*   **Process:** Atom A (1 valence electron) and Atom B (7 valence electrons) interact. Atom A transfers its electron to B.
*   **Result:** Formation of a cation ($A^+$) and an anion ($B^-$). The electrostatic attraction between them is the electrovalent bond.

**Diagram 2: Formation of $Al_2O_3$ (Aluminum Oxide)**
*   **Reactants:** Two Aluminum atoms ($2, 8, 3$) and three Oxygen atoms ($2, 6$).
*   **Transfer:** Each Aluminum atom loses 3 electrons ($Total = 6$). These 6 electrons are distributed among the three Oxygen atoms (each receiving 2).
*   **Products:** $2 Al^{3+} (2, 8)$ and $3 O^{2-} (2, 8)$.
*   **Formula:** $(Al^{3+})_2(O^{2-})_3$ or $Al_2O_3$.

### Factors Favoring the Formation of Ionic Compounds
1.  **Number of valence electrons**
2.  **Low Ionization Energy:** The minimum energy required to remove the most loosely bound electron from a neutral isolated gaseous atom.
3.  **High Electron Affinity:** The amount of energy released when an electron is added to a neutral isolated gaseous atom.
4.  **High Lattice Energy:** The energy released when one gram mole of a crystal is formed from its gaseous ions.
5.  **High Electronegativity Difference:** A difference of 2 or more between atoms A and B is necessary.

### Properties of Ionic Compounds
1.  Physical state (crystalline solids)
2.  Electrical conductivity (in molten or aqueous state)
3.  Low volatility and high melting and boiling points
4.  Solubility in polar solvents (like water) and insolubility in non-polar solvents
5.  Hard and highly brittle
6.  High density

## Slide 5

### Illustration of the formation of covalent bond

**Diagram 1: General Formation**
*   **Process:** Atom A and Atom B each contribute one electron.
*   **Result:** The electrons are shared in an overlapping region (Shared Electron Pair), forming the bond $A-B$.

**Diagram 2: Formation of $NH_3$ (Ammonia)**
*   **Reactants:** One Nitrogen atom ($2, 5$) and three Hydrogen atoms ($1$).
*   **Process:** Nitrogen shares three of its valence electrons with three Hydrogen atoms.
*   **Result:** Nitrogen achieves an octet ($2, 8$) and each Hydrogen achieves a duplet ($2$). A lone pair remains on the Nitrogen atom.

### Factors Favoring the Formation of Covalent Compounds
1.  High ionization Energy
2.  Equal electron affinities
3.  High nuclear charge and small inter-nuclear distance
4.  Number of valence electrons
5.  Equal electronegativity

### Properties of Covalent Compounds
1.  Physical state (gases, liquids, or soft solids)
2.  Crystal structure
3.  Melting and boiling points (generally low)
4.  Electrical conductivity (generally non-conductors)
5.  Solubility in polar and non-polar solvents (often soluble in non-polar)
