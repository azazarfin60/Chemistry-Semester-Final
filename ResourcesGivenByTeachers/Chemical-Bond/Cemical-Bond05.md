## Slide 21

### Formation of two Be-Cl bonds in $BeCl_2$ molecule (sp hybridization)

Based on atomic orbital theory, the two Be-Cl bonds in $BeCl_2$ would be:
1.  **$2s (Be) - 3p_x (Cl)$** bond
2.  **$2p_x (Be) - 3p_x (Cl)$** bond

**The Hybridization Explanation:**
These two bonds, if formed from pure orbitals, would differ in energy and length. However, experimental evidence shows they are identical. This is because the $2s$ and $2p_x$ orbitals of the Beryllium atom in its excited state mix (hybridize) to form two equivalent **sp hybrid orbitals**.

### Diagram Analysis: Orbital Transitions
*   **Ground State Be ($2s^2$):** Shows a filled 2s orbital and empty 2p orbitals.
*   **Excited State Be ($2s^1 p^1$):** One electron from 2s is promoted to 2p.
*   **sp Hybridized State:** The 2s and one 2p orbital mix to form two $sp$ orbitals of equal energy.
*   **Visual:** Shows the two sp hybrid orbitals oriented at **$180^\circ$** from each other.

## Slide 22

### sp hybridization
The process of mixing one 's' and one 'p' orbital to produce two equivalent sp hybrid orbitals.

**Characteristics of sp hybrids:**
1.  **Geometry:** Linear arrangement with a **$180^\circ$** angle between orbitals.
2.  **Strength:** Stronger than the pure s and p orbitals from which they originate.
3.  **Lobe Structure:** Each orbital has one large lobe and one small lobe. The large lobe allows for greater overlap with the reacting atom's orbital, resulting in a stronger bond.

### Diagram Analysis: $BeCl_2$ Formation
*   **Visual:** Shows a central Beryllium atom with two sp hybrid orbitals (large lobes pointing in opposite directions).
*   **Overlap:** Two Chlorine atoms (using their $3p_x$ orbitals) overlap with the large lobes of the sp hybrids, forming two **sp-p $\sigma$ bonds**.

**Examples:** $BeCl_2, NO_2, CO_2, HCN$ etc.

## Slide 23 & 24

### $sp^2$ hybridization
The process of mixing one 's' and two 'p' orbitals to produce three equivalent $sp^2$ hybrid orbitals.

**Characteristics of $sp^2$ hybrids:**
1.  **Geometry:** The three hybrids lie in a single plane (e.g., xz plane) and are oriented at **$120^\circ$** to each other.
2.  **Equivalence:** All three are equivalent in shape and energy.
3.  **Composition:** The ratio of p-character to s-character is **2:1** in each hybrid.

### Diagram Analysis: Trigonal Planar Arrangement
*   **Visual:** Shows the combination of $s, p_x$, and $p_y$ orbitals resulting in a three-lobed "fan" structure in the plane.

**Examples:** $BX_3, SnX_2, :SO_2, NO_3^-, :NO_2^-, HCO_2^-$ etc.

## Slide 25

### $sp^3$ hybridization
The process of mixing one 's' and three 'p' orbitals to produce four equivalent $sp^3$ hybrid orbitals.

**Characteristics of $sp^3$ hybrids:**
1.  **Geometry:** Directed toward the corners of a regular **tetrahedron**.
2.  **Bond Angle:** **$109.5^\circ$**.

### Diagram Analysis: $CH_4$ and $NH_3$
*   **$CH_4$ (Methane):** Shows a central Carbon atom with four $sp^3$ hybrids overlapping with the $1s$ orbitals of four Hydrogen atoms, forming four $\sigma$ bonds in a tetrahedral shape.
*   **$NH_3$ (Ammonia):**
    *   **Ground State N ($2s^2 2p^3$):** Shows five valence electrons.
    *   **Hybridized State:** Forms four $sp^3$ orbitals. One orbital contains a **lone pair (lp)**, while the other three have unpaired electrons for bonding.
    *   **Visual:** Shows the "umbrella" shape of ammonia with the lone pair at the top.

**Examples:** $CH_4, NH_4^+, :NH_3, H_2O, :NX_3, :PX_3$ etc.
