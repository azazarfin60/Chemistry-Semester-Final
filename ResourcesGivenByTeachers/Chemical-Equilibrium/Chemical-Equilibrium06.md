## Slide 26
![Chemical Equilibrium-026](file:///home/azaz/AntigravityData/Chemistry-Semester-Final/ResourcesGivenByTeachers/Chemical-Equilibrium/Chemical%20Equilibrium-026.png)

### Text Content
**Numerical Problem**

Consider the following heterogeneous equilibrium:
$$CaCO_3(s) \rightleftharpoons CaO(s) + CO_2(g)$$

At $800 ^\circ C$, the pressure of $CO_2$ is 0.236 atm. Calculate (a) $K_p$ and (b) $K_c$ for the reaction at this temperature.

**Strategy:** Pure solids do not appear in the equilibrium constant expression. Use $K_p = K_c(0.0821T)^{\Delta n}$.

**Solution:**
(a) $K_p = P_{CO_2} = 0.236$

(b) From Equation $K_p = K_c(0.0821T)^{\Delta n}$:
$T = 800 + 273 = 1073 \text{ K}$ and $\Delta n = 1$.
$$0.236 = K_c(0.0821 \times 1073)$$
$$K_c = 2.68 \times 10^{-3}$$

### Text Content
**Numerical Problem**

A mixture of 0.500 mol $H_2$ and 0.500 mol $I_2$ was placed in a 1.00-L stainless-steel flask at $430 ^\circ C$. The equilibrium constant $K_c$ for the reaction $H_2(g) + I_2(g) \rightleftharpoons 2HI(g)$ is 54.3 at this temperature. Calculate the concentrations of $H_2$, $I_2$, and $HI$ at equilibrium.

**Strategy:** Given initial moles and volume, calculate initial molarities ($0.500 \text{ M}$). Since no $HI$ is initially present, the reaction shifts right. Let $x$ be the change in concentration.

**Solution:**
**Step 1:** Define the stoichiometry. 1 mol $H_2$ reacts with 1 mol $I_2$ to yield 2 mol $HI$. Let $x$ be the depletion in concentration (mol/L).

## Slide 28
![Chemical Equilibrium-028](file:///home/azaz/AntigravityData/Chemistry-Semester-Final/ResourcesGivenByTeachers/Chemical-Equilibrium/Chemical%20Equilibrium-028.png)

### Text Content
**Numerical Problem (Continued)**

**ICE Table:**
| | $H_2$ | $I_2$ | $2HI$ |
| :--- | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| Initial (M): | 0.500 | 0.500 | 0.000 |
| Change (M): | $-x$ | $-x$ | $+2x$ |
| Equilibrium (M): | $(0.500 - x)$ | $(0.500 - x)$ | $2x$ |

**Step 2:** The equilibrium constant is:
$$K_c = \frac{[HI]^2}{[H_2][I_2]}$$
Substituting values:
$$54.3 = \frac{(2x)^2}{(0.500 - x)(0.500 - x)} = \frac{(2x)^2}{(0.500 - x)^2}$$
Taking the square root of both sides:
$$\sqrt{54.3} = 7.37 = \frac{2x}{0.500 - x}$$
Solving for $x$:
$$x = 0.393 \text{ M}$$

**Step 3:** Final concentrations at equilibrium:
- $[H_2] = (0.500 - 0.393) \text{ M} = 0.107 \text{ M}$
- $[I_2] = (0.500 - 0.393) \text{ M} = 0.107 \text{ M}$
- $[HI] = 2 \times 0.393 \text{ M} = 0.786 \text{ M}$

### Text Content
**Le-châtelier’s Principle**

If a system at equilibrium is disturbed by a change in temperature, pressure, or the concentration of one of the components, the system will shift its equilibrium position so as to counteract the effect of the disturbance.

The equilibrium shown is: $N_2O_4(g) \rightleftharpoons 2 NO_2(g)$

### Diagram Analysis: Syringe Experiment
The visual demonstrates the effect of pressure (volume) change on the $N_2O_4 \rightleftharpoons 2 NO_2$ equilibrium:
1. **Left Panel**: Initial equilibrium. Brown $NO_2$ and colorless $N_2O_4$ coexist.
2. **Middle Panel**: Compression (decreased volume) increases pressure. The color temporarily darkens as the concentration of $NO_2$ increases.
3. **Right Panel**: Equilibrium re-established. The color becomes lighter than the initial state because the system shifts left (toward $N_2O_4$) to reduce the total number of gas molecules and counteract the pressure increase.

### Text Content
**Effect of Temperature**
**EFFECT OF TEMPERATURE CHANGES**

Almost every equilibrium constant changes in value as the temperature changes. In an endothermic reaction, heat is absorbed as reactants are converted to products. Increasing the temperature causes the equilibrium to shift to the right and $K$ to increase. Lowering the temperature shifts the equilibrium in the direction that produces heat, to the left, decreasing $K$.

Reaction: $Co(H_2O)_6^{2+}(aq) + 4 Cl^-(aq) \rightleftharpoons CoCl_4^{2-}(aq) + 6 H_2O(l)$

### Diagram Analysis: Cobalt Complex and Temperature
The experiment visualizes the endothermic nature of this complex ion reaction:
- **At Room Temperature**: Violet color (mixture of pink $[Co(H_2O)_6]^{2+}$ and blue $[CoCl_4]^{2-}$).
- **Heating**: Shifts equilibrium to the **right**. The solution turns **blue** due to increased $[CoCl_4]^{2-}$ formation.
- **Cooling**: Shifts equilibrium to the **left**. The solution turns **pink** due to increased $[Co(H_2O)_6]^{2+}$ formation.
- **Conclusion**: Heat acts as a "reactant" for this endothermic process.
