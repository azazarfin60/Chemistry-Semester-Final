## Slide 06

### Text Content
**Chemical Equilibrium**

**Types of Chemical Equilibrium**
There are two types of chemical equilibrium:
**Homogeneous Equilibrium**: In this type, the reactants and the products of chemical equilibrium are all in the same phase. It can be further divided into **two types**: 
- Reactions in which the number of molecules of the products is equal to the number of molecules of the reactants. For example-
  - $H_2 (g) + I_2 (g) \rightleftharpoons 2HI (g)$
  - $N_2 (g) + O_2 (g) \rightleftharpoons 2NO (g)$
- Reactions in which the number of molecules of the products is not equal to the total number of reactant molecules. For example,
  - $2SO_2 (g) + O_2 (g) \rightleftharpoons 2SO_3 (g)$
  - $COCl_2 (g) \rightleftharpoons CO (g) + Cl_2 (g)$

**Heterogeneous Equilibrium**: In this type, the reactants and the products of chemical equilibrium are present in different phases. A few examples are listed below.
- $CO_2 (g) + C (s) \rightleftharpoons 2CO (g)$
- $CaCO_3 (s) \rightleftharpoons CaO (s) + CO_2 (g)$

Thus, the different types of chemical equilibrium are based on the phase of the reactants and products.

## Slide 07
![Chemical Equilibrium-007](file:///home/azaz/AntigravityData/Chemistry-Semester-Final/ResourcesGivenByTeachers/Chemical-Equilibrium/Chemical%20Equilibrium-007.png)

### Text Content
**Law of Mass Action**

Law stating that the rate of any chemical reaction is proportional to the product of the masses of the reacting substances, with each mass raised to a power equal to the coefficient that occurs in the chemical equation.

The concentration of reactants and products, at equilibrium, are constant at a given temperature. Consider the following simple reversible reaction where A & B are the reactants whereas C & D are the products.
$A + B \rightleftharpoons C + D$

This relation can be equated as follows.
$$Kc = \frac{[C] \times [D]}{[A] \times [B]}$$
[Here, $K_c$ is called the equilibrium constant.]

> [!NOTE]
> The slide image contains a typographical error in the mathematical expression for $Kc$, showing $[C] \times [D]$ in the denominator as well. The transcribed version above corrects this to the intended $[A] \times [B]$.

**Equilibrium Constant-** For a certain chemical reaction, the equilibrium constant is defined as the ratio between the amount of reactant and the amount of product which is used to determine chemical behavior. The equilibrium concentrations of reactants and products may vary, but the value for Kc remains the same.

**Other Characteristics of Kc**
- Equilibrium can be approached from either direction.
- Kc does not depend on the initial concentrations of reactants and products.
- Kc does depend on temperature.

### Text Content
**Equilibrium Constant**

For a balanced reaction of the type,
$$aA + bB \rightleftharpoons cC + dD$$

| Equilibrium Constant Representation | Expressed in terms of | Expressed as |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| $K_c$ | Concentrations of reactants and products | $\frac{[C]^c [D]^d}{[A]^a [B]^b}$ |
| $K_p$ | Partial pressures of reactants and products (only for the substances which are in gaseous state) | $\frac{P_C^c P_D^d}{P_A^a P_B^b}$ |
| $K_x$ | Mole fractions of reactants and products | $\frac{X_C^c X_D^d}{X_A^a X_B^b}$ |

### Text Content
**Equilibrium Constant**
**What Does the Value of $K$ Mean?**

- **If $K >> 1$**, the reaction is **product-favored**; product predominates at equilibrium.
  - Diagram: Small box for "Reactants" $\rightleftharpoons$ Large box for "Products". Equilibrium "lies to the right".
- **If $K << 1$**, the reaction is **reactant-favored**; reactant predominates at equilibrium.
  - Diagram: Large box for "Reactants" $\rightleftharpoons$ Small box for "Products". Equilibrium "lies to the left".

*When $10^{-3} < K < 10^3$, the reaction is considered to contain a significant amount of both reactants and products at equilibrium.

## Slide 10
![Chemical Equilibrium-010](file:///home/azaz/AntigravityData/Chemistry-Semester-Final/ResourcesGivenByTeachers/Chemical-Equilibrium/Chemical%20Equilibrium-010.png)

### Text Content
**Equilibrium Constant**

For the reaction: $aA + bB \xrightleftharpoons[k_{-1}]{k_1} cC + dD$

Rate of the forward reaction $= k_1 [A]^a [B]^b$
Rate of the reverse reaction $= k_{-1} [C]^c [D]^d$

At equilibrium:
Rate of the forward reaction = Rate of the reverse reaction
$$k_1 [A]^a [B]^b = k_{-1} [C]^c [D]^d$$
$$\frac{k_1}{k_{-1}} = \frac{[C]^c [D]^d}{[A]^a [B]^b}$$
$$K_{eq} = \frac{[C]^c [D]^d}{[A]^a [B]^b}$$

### Diagram Analysis: Rate and Molecular Views
1. **Reaction Rates vs. Time Graph**: Re-emphasizes that equilibrium is achieved when the forward (decreasing) and reverse (increasing) rates intersect and become equal.
2. **Molecular Visualization**:
   - **Before (Starting conditions)**: Shows a separation between reactants ($A$ - blue spheres) and products ($B$ - purple spheres). 
   - **After (At equilibrium)**: Shows a mixture of both types with green arrows indicating the continuous conversion of $A$ to $B$ and $B$ to $A$.
   - **Key takeaway**: The reaction is in **dynamic equilibrium** because conversion continues in both directions at equal rates.
