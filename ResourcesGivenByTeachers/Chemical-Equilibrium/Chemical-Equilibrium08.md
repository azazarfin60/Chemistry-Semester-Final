### Text Content
**The Haber Process: Concentration and Stress**

The Haber-Bosch process is an equilibrium between the reactants ($N_2$ and $H_2$) and the product ($NH_3$). When more $N_2$ is added, the system favors the forward reaction until equilibrium is reestablished.

### Diagram Analysis: Concentration vs Time
The graph illustrates the dynamic response of the system to a concentration stress:
1. **Initial Equilibrium**: $N_2, H_2,$ and $NH_3$ concentrations are constant.
2. **Stress (Dashed Line)**: More $N_2$ is suddenly added, shown by the vertical spike in the $N_2$ curve.
3. **Shift**: To counteract this addition, the system consumes the excess $N_2$ and some $H_2$ (both curves slope downwards) to produce more $NH_3$ (curve slopes upwards).
4. **New Equilibrium**: Concentrations level off again at new values.

**Summary of Concentration Stress:**
| Stress | Response |
| :--- | :--- |
| Addition of reactant | Forward reaction favored |
| Addition of product | Reverse reaction favored |
| Removal of reactant | Reverse reaction favored |
| Removal of product | Forward reaction favored |

**The Haber Process: Yield Analysis**

### Diagram Analysis: Pressure and Temperature Effects
Two graphs analyze the percentage yield of ammonia:
- **Left Graph (% Yield vs Pressure)**: At any given temperature, the yield of $NH_3$ increases as pressure increases. This is because the forward reaction reduces the number of gas molecules (4 moles $\rightarrow$ 2 moles).
- **Right Graph (% Yield vs Temperature)**: At any given pressure, the yield of $NH_3$ decreases as temperature increases. This confirms that the forward reaction is exothermic.

**Industrial Considerations:**
1. **Kinetic Necessity**: Although low temperature favors yield, the reaction rate would be too slow to be commercially viable.
2. **Balance with Pressure**: High pressure favors yield but increases equipment costs and safety risks.
3. **Optimum Compromise**: Industrial conditions (approx. 450 °C and 200 atm) represent a compromise between high yield, high speed, and manageable costs.

**Numerical Problem: Haber Process**

For the Haber process, $N_2(g) + 3 H_2(g) \rightleftharpoons 2 NH_3(g)$, $K_p = 1.45 \times 10^{-5}$ at $500 ^\circ C$. In an equilibrium mixture of the three gases at $500 ^\circ C$, the partial pressure of $H_2$ is 0.928 atm and that of $N_2$ is 0.432 atm. What is the partial pressure of $NH_3$ in this equilibrium mixture?

**Equilibrium Pressures (atm):**
- $P_{N_2} = 0.432$
- $P_{H_2} = 0.928$
- $P_{NH_3} = x$

**Calculation:**
$$K_p = \frac{(P_{NH_3})^2}{P_{N_2}(P_{H_2})^3} = \frac{x^2}{(0.432)(0.928)^3} = 1.45 \times 10^{-5}$$
$$x^2 = (1.45 \times 10^{-5})(0.432)(0.928)^3 = 5.01 \times 10^{-6}$$
$$x = \sqrt{5.01 \times 10^{-6}} = 2.24 \times 10^{-3} \text{ atm}$$
$$P_{NH_3} = 2.24 \times 10^{-3} \text{ atm}$$

**Comment:** The result can be verified by recalculating $K_p$ using the solved value:
$$K_p = \frac{(2.24 \times 10^{-3})^2}{(0.432)(0.928)^3} \approx 1.45 \times 10^{-5}$$
