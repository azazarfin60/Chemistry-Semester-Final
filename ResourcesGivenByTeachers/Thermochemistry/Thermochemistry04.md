> [!TIP]
> **SOLVED PROBLEM 1.** The heat of reaction $\frac{1}{2} H_2 + \frac{1}{2} Cl_2 \longrightarrow HCl$ at $27^\circ\text{C}$ is $-22.1 \text{ kcal}$. Calculate the heat of reaction at $77^\circ\text{C}$. The molar heat capacities at constant pressure at $27^\circ\text{C}$ for hydrogen, chlorine and $HCl$ are 6.82, 7.70 and 6.80 cal mol$^{-1}$ respectively.
>
> **SOLUTION**
> Here,
> $$\frac{1}{2} H_2 + \frac{1}{2} Cl_2 \longrightarrow HCl \quad \Delta H = -22.1 \text{ kcal}$$
>
> $\Delta C_P = \text{Heat capacities of products} - \text{Heat capacities of reactants}$
> $$= 6.80 - \left[ \frac{1}{2} (6.82) + \frac{1}{2} (7.70) \right]$$
> $$= 6.80 - 7.26 = -0.46 \text{ cal} = -0.46 \times 10^{-3} \text{ kcal}$$
>
> $T_2 = 273 + 77 = 350 \text{ K}; \quad T_1 = 273 + 27 = 300 \text{ K}$
> $T_2 - T_1 = (350 - 300) \text{ K} = 50 \text{ K}$
>
> Substituting these values in Kirchoff’s equation, we have
> $$\Delta H_2 - \Delta H_1 = \Delta C_P (T_2 - T_1)$$
> $$= -22.1 + (-0.46 \times 10^{-3}) \times 50$$
> $$= -22.1 + (-0.023)$$
> $$= -22.123 \text{ kcal}$$
>
> $\therefore \text{Heat of reaction at } 77^\circ\text{C} \text{ is } \mathbf{-22.123 \text{ kcal}}$

> [!TIP]
> **SOLVED PROBLEM 2.** The heat of reaction $N_2 + 3H_2 \longrightarrow 2NH_3$ at $27^\circ\text{C}$ was found to be $-21.976 \text{ kcal}$. What will be the heat of reaction at $50^\circ\text{C}$?
> The molar heat capacities at constant pressure and at $27^\circ\text{C}$ for nitrogen, hydrogen and ammonia are 6.8, 6.77 and 8.86 cal mol$^{-1}$ degree$^{-1}$.
>
> **SOLUTION**
> Here, $\Delta H = -21.976 \text{ kcal}$
> $T_2 = 273 + 50 = 323 \text{ K}$
> $T_1 = 273 + 27 = 300 \text{ K}$
> $[T_2 - T_1] = (323 - 300) \text{ K} = 23 \text{ K}$
>
> $\Delta C_P = \text{Heat capacities of products} - \text{Heat capacities of reactants}$
> $$= (2 \times 8.86) - [6.8 + (3 \times 6.77)]$$
> $$= 17.72 - (6.8 + 20.31) = -9.39 \text{ cal}$$
> $$= -9.39 \times 10^{-3} \text{ kcal}$$
>
> Substituting these values in Kirchoff’s equation, we have
> $$\Delta H_2 = \Delta H_1 + (T_2 - T_1) \Delta C_P$$
> $$= -21.976 + [23 \times (-9.39 \times 10^{-3})]$$
> $$= -21.976 + (-0.216) = -22.192 \text{ kcal}$$
>
> $\therefore \text{Heat of reaction at } 50^\circ\text{C} \text{ is } \mathbf{-22.192 \text{ kcal}}$

### DIFFERENT TYPES OF HEAT (ENTHALPY) OF REACTION

The heat or enthalpy changes accompanying chemical reactions are expressed in different ways, depending on the nature of the reaction. These are discussed below.

### HEAT OF FORMATION

The heat of formation of a compound is defined as :

**The change in enthalpy that takes place when one mole of the compound is formed from its elements.**

It is denoted by $\Delta H_f$. For example, the heat of formation of ferrous sulphide and acetylene may be expressed as :
$$Fe(s) + S(s) \longrightarrow FeS(s) \quad \Delta H_f = -24.0 \text{ kcal}$$
$$2C(s) + H_2(g) \longrightarrow C_2H_2(g) \quad \Delta H_f = +53.14 \text{ kcal}$$

Similarly, the reaction between gaseous hydrogen and gaseous chlorine to form gaseous hydrogen chloride is represented by the equation
$$H_2(g) + Cl_2(g) \longrightarrow 2HCl(g) \quad \Delta H = -44.0 \text{ kcal}$$

It may be noted in this case that $-44.0 \text{ kcal}$ is not the heat of formation of hydrogen chloride because this amount of heat is evolved when two moles of hydrogen chloride are formed. The heat of formation of hydrogen chloride, therefore, would be $-44.0/2 = -22.0 \text{ kcal}$ and the equation can be written as
$$\frac{1}{2} H_2(g) + \frac{1}{2} Cl_2(g) \longrightarrow HCl(g) \quad \Delta H_f = -22.0 \text{ kcal}$$

### STANDARD HEAT OF FORMATION

The standard heat of formation of a compound is defined as :

**The change in enthalpy that takes place when one mole of a compound is formed from its elements, all substances being in their standard states (298 K and 1 atm pressure).**

The standard heat of formation of some compounds are given in Table 8.2.

### TABLE 8.2. STANDARD HEAT OF FORMATION OF SOME COMPOUNDS
| Substance | $\Delta H_f^\circ$ kcal mol$^{-1}$ | Substance | $\Delta H_f^\circ$ kcal mol$^{-1}$ |
| :--- | :---: | :--- | :---: |
| $H_2O(g)$ | $-57.84$ | $CH_4(g)$ | $-17.89$ |
| $H_2O(l)$ | $-68.38$ | $C_2H_6(g)$ | $-20.23$ |
| $HCl(g)$ | $-22.08$ | $C_6H_6(g)$ | $+11.86$ |
| $HBr(g)$ | $-8.70$ | $C_2H_2(g)$ | $+53.14$ |
| $NH_3(g)$ | $-11.02$ | $CH_3OH(l)$ | $-57.17$ |
| $CO_2(g)$ | $-94.13$ | $C_2H_5OH(l)$ | $-66.26$ |
| $SO_2(g)$ | $-71.00$ | $CH_3COOH(l)$ | $-115.83$ |

By convention the standard heat of formation of all elements is assumed to be zero.

#### Standard Heat of Reaction ($\Delta H^\circ$) from Standard Heat of Formation ($\Delta H_f^\circ$)
We can calculate the heat of reaction under standard conditions from the values of standard heat of formation of various reactants and products. **The standard heat of reaction is equal to the standard heat of formation of products minus the standard heat of formation of reactants.**

That is,
**$$\Delta H^\circ = \left[ \text{Total standard heat of formation of products} \right] - \left[ \text{Total standard heat of formation of reactants} \right]$$**
**$$\Delta H^\circ = \sum \Delta H_f^\circ (\text{products}) - \sum \Delta H_f^\circ (\text{reactants})$$**

Let us consider a general reaction
$$aA + bB \longrightarrow cC + dD$$
The standard heat of reaction is given by
$$\Delta H^\circ = \Delta H_f^\circ (\text{products}) - \Delta H_f^\circ (\text{reactants})$$
$$= [c \times \Delta H_f^\circ(C) + d \times \Delta H_f^\circ(D)] - [a \times \Delta H_f^\circ(A) + b \times \Delta H_f^\circ(B)]$$

> [!TIP]
> **SOLVED PROBLEM 1.** Calculate $\Delta H^\circ$ for the reaction
> $$CO_2(g) + H_2(g) \longrightarrow CO(g) + H_2O(g)$$
> given that $\Delta H_f^\circ$ for $CO_2(g), CO(g) \text{ and } H_2O(g)$ are $-393.5, -111.31 \text{ and } -241.80 \text{ kJ mol}^{-1}$ respectively.
>
> **SOLUTION**
> Here we have
> $$CO_2(g) + H_2(g) \longrightarrow CO(g) + H_2O(g)$$
> $$\Delta H^\circ = \sum \Delta H_f^\circ (\text{products}) - \sum \Delta H_f^\circ (\text{reactants})$$
> $$= [\Delta H_f^\circ(CO(g)) + \Delta H_f^\circ(H_2O(g))] - [\Delta H_f^\circ(CO_2(g)) + \Delta H_f^\circ(H_2(g))]$$
> $$= [-111.3 + (-241.8)] - [-393.5 + 0]$$
> $$= -353.1 + 393.5$$
> **$= 40.4 \text{ kJ}$**

> [!TIP]
> **SOLVED PROBLEM 2.** The standard heats of formation of $C_2H_5OH(l), CO_2(g) \text{ and } H_2O(l)$ are $-277.0, -393.5 \text{ and } -285.5 \text{ kJ mol}^{-1}$ respectively. Calculate the standard heat change for the reaction
> $$C_2H_5OH(l) + 3O_2(g) \longrightarrow 2CO_2(g) + 3H_2O(l)$$
>
> **SOLUTION**
> We know that :
> $$\Delta H^\circ = \sum \Delta H_f^\circ (\text{products}) - \sum \Delta H_f^\circ (\text{reactants})$$
> In the present case
> $$\Delta H^\circ = [2 \times \Delta H_f^\circ(CO_2(g)) + 3 \times \Delta H_f^\circ(H_2O(l))]$$
> $$- [\Delta H_f^\circ(C_2H_5OH(l)) + 3 \times \Delta H_f^\circ(O_2(g))]$$
> $$= [2 \times (-393.5) + 3 \times -285.5] - [-277.0 - 0]$$
> $$= -1643.5 - (-277)$$
> **$= -1366.5 \text{ kJ}$**
