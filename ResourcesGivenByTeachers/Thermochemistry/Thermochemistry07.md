> [!TIP]
> **SOLVED PROBLEM 2.** The heat of combustion of ethyl alcohol is $-330 \text{ kcal}$. If the heat of formation of $CO_2(g)$ and $H_2O(l)$ be $-94.3 \text{ kcal}$ and $-68.5 \text{ kcal}$ respectively, calculate the heat of formation of ethyl alcohol.
>
> **SOLUTION**
> We are given
> (a) $C_2H_5OH(l) + 3O_2(g) \longrightarrow 2CO_2(g) + 3H_2O(l) \quad \Delta H = -330.0 \text{ kcal}$
> (b) $C(s) + O_2(g) \longrightarrow CO_2(g) \quad \Delta H = -94.3 \text{ kcal}$
> (c) $H_2(g) + \frac{1}{2} O_2(g) \longrightarrow H_2O(l) \quad \Delta H = -68.5 \text{ kcal}$
>
> We have to manipulate these equations so as to get the required equation :
> $$2C(s) + 3H_2(g) + \frac{1}{2} O_2(g) \longrightarrow C_2H_5OH(l) \quad \Delta H = ?$$
>
> Multiplying equation *(b)* by 2 and equation *(c)* by 3 and adding up these, we get
> $$2C(s) + 3H_2(g) + 3\frac{1}{2} O_2(g) \longrightarrow 2CO_2(g) + 3H_2O(l)$$
> $$\Delta H = -394.1 \text{ kcal}$$
>
> Subtracting equation *(a)* from the above equation, we have
> $$2C(s) + 3H_2(g) + \frac{1}{2} O_2(g) \longrightarrow C_2H_5OH(l)$$
> $$\Delta H = -394.1 - (-330.0) = -64.1 \text{ kcal}$$
>
> Thus the heat of formation of ethyl alcohol is **$-64.1 \text{ kcal}$**.

> [!TIP]
> **SOLVED PROBLEM 3.** Determine $\Delta H$ of the reaction
> $$C(s) + 2H_2(g) \longrightarrow CH_4(g)$$
> from the following data :
> (i) $C(s) + O_2(g) \longrightarrow CO_2(g) \quad \Delta H = -393.7 \text{ kJ}$
> (ii) $H_2(g) + \frac{1}{2} O_2(g) \longrightarrow H_2O(l) \quad \Delta H = -285.7 \text{ kJ}$
> (iii) $CH_4(g) + 2O_2(g) \longrightarrow CO_2(g) + 2H_2O(l) \quad \Delta H = -890.3 \text{ kJ}$
>
> **SOLUTION**
> Multiplying equation *(ii)* by 2 and adding to equation *(i)*, we get
> (iv) $C(s) + O_2(g) + 2H_2(g) + O_2(g) \longrightarrow CO_2(g) + 2H_2O(l)$
> $$\Delta H = (2 \times -285.7) + (-393.7) = -965.1 \text{ kJ}$$
>
> On subtracting equation *(iii)* from equation *(iv)*, we get
> $$C(s) + 2H_2(g) = CH_4(g) \quad \Delta H = -965.1 - (-890.3)$$
> $$\Delta H = -74.8 \text{ kJ}$$
>
> Thus the heat of formation of methane is **$-74.8 \text{ kJ}$**.

**(2) Determination of Heat of Transition**
The heat of transition of one allotropic form to another can also be calculated with the help of Hess’s law. For example, the enthalpy of transition from monoclinic sulphur to rhombic sulphur can be calculated from their heats of combustion which are :
(i) $S_{\text{rhombic}} + O_2(g) \longrightarrow SO_2(g) \quad \Delta H = -291.4 \text{ kJ}$
(ii) $S_{\text{monoclinic}} + O_2(g) \longrightarrow SO_2(g) \quad \Delta H = -295.4 \text{ kJ}$

**(3) Determination of heats of various reactions**
By using Hess’s law we can calculate the heats or enthalpies of many reactions which otherwise cannot be measured directly. For example, from the following equations the enthalpy of dimerisation of $NO_2$ can be calculated.
(i) $N_2(g) + 2O_2(g) \longrightarrow 2NO_2(g) \quad \Delta H = 67.9 \text{ kJ}$
(ii) $N_2(g) + 2O_2(g) \longrightarrow N_2O_4(g) \quad \Delta H = 9.3 \text{ kJ}$
Subtracting equation *(i)* from equation *(ii)* we have
$$2NO_2(g) \longrightarrow N_2O_4(g) \quad \Delta H = (9.3 - 67.9) \text{ kJ} = -58.6 \text{ kJ}$$
Thus the heat of dimerisation of $NO_2$ is **$-58.6 \text{ kJ}$**.

### BOND ENERGY

When a bond between two atoms is formed, there is a release of energy. The same amount of energy is absorbed when the bond is broken.

**The bond energy is defined as the average amount of energy required to break all bonds of a particular type in one mole of the substance.**

Thus the bond energy of $H-H$ bond is the energy required to break all the bonds in one mole of the gas. It is expressed in $\text{kcal mol}^{-1}$ or $\text{kJ mol}^{-1}$. For example, the bond energy of $H-H$ bond is $433 \text{ kJ mol}^{-1}$ or $103.58 \text{ kcal mol}^{-1}$. The bond energies of some common bonds are listed below :

| Bond | Bond Energy |
| :--- | :--- |
| $Cl-Cl$ | $243 \text{ kJ mol}^{-1}$ |
| $O=O$ | $499.0 \text{ kJ mol}^{-1}$ |
| $C-H$ | $414.0 \text{ kJ mol}^{-1}$ |
| $O-H$ | $460.0 \text{ kJ mol}^{-1}$ |

### Bond Energy is a Measure of Strength of the Bond
In other words, bond energy is the force with which the atoms are bonded together. **It depends upon :**
(i) Size of the atom
(ii) Electronegativity
(iii) Bond length

A knowledge of bond enthalpy is useful for calculating heats of reaction for gaseous reactions for which no thermal data is available and which involve substances having covalent bonds.

Suppose we desire to determine the bond energy of $C-H$ bond in methane. For this purpose we need to know the enthalpy change for the reaction
$$C(g) + 4H(g) \longrightarrow CH_4(g)$$
This is obtained by combining the heat of formation of methane from $C(s) + H_2(g)$ with the heat of sublimation of carbon *i.e.*, $C(s) \longrightarrow C(g)$ and the heat of dissociation of hydrogen into atoms *i.e.*, $H_2(g) \longrightarrow 2H(g)$.

The calculation of heat of reaction with the help of bond energies is illustrated in the following examples.

> [!TIP]
> **SOLVED PROBLEM 1.** Given that energies for $H-H, O=O$ and $O-H$ bonds are 104, 118 and $111 \text{ kcal mol}^{-1}$ respectively, calculate the heat of the reaction
> $$H_2(g) + \frac{1}{2} O_2(g) \longrightarrow H_2O(g)$$
>
> **SOLUTION**
> In this reaction, two $O-H$ bonds are formed and one $H-H$ bond is broken. Therefore we can write for $\Delta H$
> $$\Delta H = -2 \Delta H(O-H) + \Delta H(H-H) + \frac{1}{2} \Delta H(O=O)$$
> $$= (-2 \times 111) + 104 + (\frac{1}{2} \times 118)$$
> $$= -222 + 104 + 59$$
> $$= -59 \text{ kcal mol}^{-1}$$
>
> $\therefore$ The heat of the given reaction is **$-59.0 \text{ kcal mol}^{-1}$**

> [!TIP]
> **SOLVED PROBLEM 2.** Calculate the bond energy of $HCl$, given that $H-H$ bond energy is $433 \text{ kJ mol}^{-1}$, $Cl-Cl$ bond energy is $242 \text{ kJ mol}^{-1}$ and $\Delta H_f$ for $HCl$ is $-91 \text{ kJ mol}^{-1}$.
>
> **SOLUTION**
> Given :
> (i) $\frac{1}{2} H_2(g) + \frac{1}{2} Cl_2(g) \longrightarrow HCl(g) \quad \Delta H = -91 \text{ kJ}$
> (ii) $H_2(g) \longrightarrow 2H(g) \quad \Delta H = +433 \text{ kJ}$
> (iii) $Cl_2(g) \longrightarrow 2Cl(g) \quad \Delta H = +242 \text{ kJ}$
> Multiplying equation *(i)* by 2, we get
> (iv) $H_2(g) + Cl_2(g) \longrightarrow 2HCl(g) \quad \Delta H = -182 \text{ kJ}$
> Adding equation *(ii)* and *(iii)*, we have
> (v) $H_2(g) + Cl_2(g) \longrightarrow 2H(g) + 2Cl(g) \quad \Delta H = +675 \text{ kJ}$
> Subtracting equation *(iv)* from *(v)*, one has
> $2HCl(g) \longrightarrow 2H(g) + 2Cl(g) \quad \Delta H = +857 \text{ kJ}$
> Dividing this equation by 2 we get
> $HCl(g) \longrightarrow H(g) + Cl(g) \quad \Delta H = 428.5 \text{ kJ}$
>
> Thus the bond energy of $H-Cl$ is **$428.5 \text{ kJ}$**.
